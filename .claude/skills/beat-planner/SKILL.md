# Beat Planner

A chapter that's planned with too few beats will come out short no matter how well it's written — the L1-L6 test chapters proved this: all 6 were planned with 3-4 beats each, and all 6 landed between 954 and 1,909 words, below the 15-20 minute target even though the prose itself passed review. Stretching the writing to compensate produces exactly the overwriting/purple-prose problem the chapter-review skill already caught on L1 (chained similes, image accumulation). The fix is at the planning stage: more real beats, not longer beats.

This skill exists to make that fix repeatable instead of something that has to be re-derived by hand every time.

## When to run this

Before writing prose for any chapter — new or already outlined. Also run it retroactively on any existing beat plan that has fewer than 6 beats (see `content/lore/expansion_progress_libro1.md`'s 2026-08-08 audit note — as of that date that was all 6 Lament arc chapters and all 18 new-subplot chapters; both are now 100% complete per that same tracker, so this note is historical context rather than an open action item as of 2026-08-20).

## Inputs required

- The chapter's current beat list (however thin) and its POV character.
- `content/lore/expansion_guidelines.md` — the 6 approved expansion categories and the hard target (6-8 beats, 1,800-3,000 words).
- `content/lore/beat_density_test.md` — real measured density (~342-450 words/beat) to sanity-check the math.
- `content/lore/prompt_universo.md` / `personajes.md` — for any secondary character being pulled in to fill a beat, and for reveal-pacing constraints (nothing about the Forgotten leaks early).

## Step 1 — Classify the chapter's narrator pattern

Six patterns have already proven out across the Lament arc test chapters. Match the chapter to the closest one (or note that it's a genuine new shape — don't force a bad fit):

1. **Descent/quest** (POV: someone pursuing an object/goal into hostile unknown territory — e.g. B1L01). Shape: arrival/atmosphere → false signal/overconfidence → obstacle/reveal → underestimation & setback → reflection/resolve. 5 slots, naturally splits to 6-7 beats.
2. **Council/debate** (POV: someone bringing evidence into a group with competing agendas — e.g. B1L02). Shape: discovery/evidence → bringing it to the room → 2-3 competing arguments (each its own beat) → decisive voice/compromise → cost of the compromise, visible to the reader but not the characters. 5-6 slots.
3. **Recovery + hidden threat** (POV: someone wounded/set back, who then receives news of a parallel threat — e.g. B1L03). Shape: aftermath/cost → hidden threat delivered via messenger or news → motivation shift (fear/pride recalculated) → renewed attempt with a new strategy. 4 slots — pad with a secondary-character beat (category 1 or 3 from expansion_guidelines.md) to reach 6.
4. **Escalating pressure + consequence** (POV: someone under mounting internal strain that leaks into the external world — e.g. B1L04). Shape: escalating pressure (2 beats — don't compress into 1) → external consequence cut to a secondary/mortal POV → near-breaking point → breakthrough/discovery. 5-6 slots.
5. **Mirror/claiming climax** (POV: someone reaching the true prize of a multi-chapter quest — e.g. B1L05). Shape: arrival at the real thing (subverting the buildup — often smaller/humbler than expected) → vision/origin-story of the object → refusal/struggle against what the vision demands → claiming without surrender (or with, if that's the character's arc). 4 core slots — this pattern legitimately runs denser per beat (climax chapters can lean toward 400-450 w/beat), but still add 2 more beats: a sensory/world-building beat before the vision, and an aftermath beat after claiming, to reach 6.
6. **Aftermath convergence** (multiple POVs converging after a prior chapter's climax — e.g. B1L06). Shape: release/return (1) → 2-3 parallel consequence beats, each a different POV or location → political/social reckoning → forward-looking unresolved tension. 5-7 slots depending on how many POVs converge.

7. **Manipulación/Trampa** (POV: someone engineering a betrayal or deception against a target who doesn't see it coming — e.g. B1C11 "The Devil's Gambit"). Shape: private motive/calculation (grounded in an interaction or decision, not pure solitary interiority — satisfy this via category 5 dialogue, not narrated exposition) → the performance in front of the target → the target's evaluation/reaction (give them a plausible, in-character reason to fall for it — never just "arrogance," root it in something specific to their fiche) → a witness's unresolved suspicion (a secondary character who notices but, for a stated reason, stays silent — decide explicitly whether this seeds a future payoff or is closed atmospheric texture, per the consistency rule) → the mechanical step that sells the deception → the trap closing, with dramatic irony toward the reader. 6 slots. Added 2026-08-09 during the Parte 2 beat-planner pass, per Codex review of B1C11.

8. **Montaje de dominio / Mastery montage** (POV: someone learning to control a power or tool that has been resisting them — e.g. B1C20 "The Master of Lament"). Shape: (1) initial struggle/failure → (2) a secondary-character reaction to that weakness (ambition, doubt — category 1) → (3) a *tactical* turning point, never an emotional-surrender one if the character's established arc is domination-not-submission (check against prior chapters before writing this beat — B1C20 needed this correction twice against L5/L6's "Belial never submits, he bends the weapon to his ego": the tactical-pivot beat itself, and the demonstration beat that follows, both have to keep showing the internal cost/resistance, not just assert it in a note) → (4) demonstration of the new capability, with the personal cost still visibly paid, not resolved → (5) a political/strategic consequence beat tying to already-established secondary plots, stated only as a shift in the *existing* plot's balance, never as a new maneuver that plot hasn't earned → (6) proclamation/public consequence. 6 slots. Added 2026-08-09 during the Parte 2 beat-planner pass.

9. **Interludio atmosférico / Atmospheric interlude** (a short, deliberately mysterious chapter introducing a reveal-pacing-sensitive element without resolving or naming it — e.g. B1C21 "The Seer on the Summit," introducing the third Forgotten). Shape: stillness/introduction (unnamed) → environment texture (category 4, pure geography, no supernatural agency attributed to the place itself) → a distant, deliberately vague cosmic disturbance reaches the place (never juxtapose two already-named/established causes side by side if doing so would let the reader connect this scene explicitly to a prophecy or reveal already delivered elsewhere — keep the disturbance's origin unattributed within the text) → first physical change → closing image of suspense with no resolution, no explicit "third point of X" framing if a prophecy involving a count/set has already been revealed to the reader elsewhere. 6 slots, deliberately kept toward the low end of the word range (tone: "brief, mysterious," matching B1C18's "short, quiet" restraint). Added 2026-08-09 during the Parte 2 beat-planner pass, per Codex review of B1C21 — this pattern's whole point is protecting a reveal, so re-verify every beat against the relevant character's canon fiche and against any prophecy/reveal chapters already written, not just against the immediate chapter's own original text.

10. **Crisis coral + decisión personal / Choral crisis + personal decision** (a wide-scale, multi-front crisis shown through aggregate chaos rather than individual argued positions, resolving not in a collective decision but in one character's private, costly choice — e.g. B1C22 "The Eve of Ruin"). Shape: simultaneous attack/crisis across many fronts → a concrete micro-POV of the impact on one of those fronts (category 6, explicitly a *different* front/unit than any already dedicated to its own chapter elsewhere, to avoid duplication) → aggregate chaos (reports, an institution realizing its doctrine is obsolete) → strategic helplessness (a leader with no move left) → an intimate appeal, not a command → the personal decision. 6 slots. Do not force this into pattern 2 ("Council/debate") just because a war room is involved — pattern 2 requires actual competing arguments toward a collective decision; this pattern's resolution is deliberately personal, not institutional. Added 2026-08-09 during the Parte 2 beat-planner pass, per Codex review of B1C22.

11. **Fortaleza quebrada / Broken bulwark** (a disciplined, competent unit or leader faces a threat their normal tools cannot touch — e.g. B1C23 "The Despairing Front"). Shape: initial strength/competence established → humanize the character about to break (category 1, using an already-fiched secondary character, never an invented one — check the full roster in `prompt_universo.md` before naming anyone new) → an unconventional threat that conventional strength can't counter → the collapse, with a specific individualized casualty (not an anonymous crowd) → a failed doctrinal response (category 1, the leader tries their one real tool and it does nothing) → a warning/message that redefines the conflict for the reader. 6 slots. Added 2026-08-09 during the Parte 3 retroactive audit, per Codex review of B1C23 — this pattern's chapter also taught a standing lesson: when a chapter later (in narrative order) claims a character explicitly died/was lost, that death needs its own causal beat or explicit bridge — a "psychic collapse" and a "died in battle" are not automatically the same thing, and asserting the second in a later chapter without ever showing or bridging to it in this one is a continuity gap, not an inference the reader can be expected to make alone.

12. **Introducción en la ignorancia / Introduction in ignorance** (a solo-POV chapter reintroducing a character who doesn't yet understand their own nature, built from a sequence of small uncanny sensory details that accumulate without a frame to interpret them — e.g. B2C01 "The Professor of Lost Things," reintroducing Mikel Ardon in Book II). Shape: an ordinary scene undercut by one small wrong detail → a second, escalating wrong detail (physical/sensory, not yet explained) → a beat with a secondary character that grounds the character's isolation in real interaction (category 5, never just narrated loneliness) → the strongest uncanny detail yet (often a dream or vision) → a physical object/anchor that makes the mystery tangible without resolving it → a second secondary-character beat closing the isolation thread from earlier, showing the character actively refusing an offered connection. 6 slots. Added 2026-08-11 during the Libro II beat-planner pass, for B2C01.

13. **El que observa y no golpea / The one who watches and doesn't strike** (a POV chapter for a character whose established combat style is manipulation/deception rather than direct force — e.g. B2C07.5 "What Even He Does Not Know," Lucifer's first-ever scene in the project). Shape: routine display of omniscience/control → attention snags on something specific → a secondary character's question surfaces the tension without naming it (category 5) → the character searches their own vast knowledge and comes up empty → the composure holds, but a beat of real stillness shows the crack to the reader without the character admitting it → a decision to act indirectly, consistent with their established non-confrontational style, closing on an unresolved question. 6 slots. Added 2026-08-11 for a Lucifer-POV chapter in the Libro II beat-planner pass — the whole point of this pattern is showing power through omniscience and composure rather than combat, for characters whose fiche explicitly rules out direct confrontation.

14. **El interrogatorio inquebrantable / The interrogation that doesn't break** (POV: an interrogator whose method has always worked, facing a captive whose resistance isn't strategic — no defiance, no training, just a conviction that doesn't move — e.g. B2C08.5 "The Silence That Doesn't Break," Balam and Barbas's first scenes in the project). Shape: (1) capture/competence established for whoever hands the prisoner over (category 6, combat) → (2) a decision that character makes, characterizing them by what they refuse to do rather than what they do (category 1) → (3) the handoff, in real dialogue, to the second anchor character, characterizing both by how they talk to each other (category 5) → (4) the interrogator's reputation established through a secondary witness reacting to them, never through direct narration alone (category 1/3) → (5) the usual method applied, with the expected result stated explicitly before it's tested → (6) the anomaly — the expected result doesn't arrive, framed strictly as the interrogator's professional inference, never as narrated fact about the captive's interior state → (7) a second attempt/escalation that also fails, deepening the specific quality of the mystery without resolving it, and showing the interrogator's control starting to cost them something (an unusual pause, an unnecessary repetition) rather than asserting discomfort directly → (8) the decision to stop short of what the superior would want, and the withheld reason as the closing image. 8 slots — this pattern runs toward the high end of the beat count because it needs two full character introductions (captor who hands off, interrogator who receives) inside one chapter. Added 2026-08-18 during the Barbas/Balam interlude pass, per the project's own Death-State/POV-discipline rules: since the interrogation is the chapter's core, framing every claim about the captive's internal state as the POV character's inference (never as narrator fact) is not optional stylistic flavor for this pattern — it's the mechanism that keeps a tight-third POV chapter about torture from accidentally asserting things the POV character cannot actually know.

15. **Vigilia institucional sin resolución / Institutional vigil without resolution** (a character sustains a long, specialized effort toward a goal the wider narrative has already fixed as unresolved for this book, and the chapter's shape is emotional endurance rather than plot progress — e.g. `B2C03.6` "What the Hands Are For," Laila's introduction, searching for a relic the book's own established canon confirms Heaven never recovers). Shape: (1) routine of preparation/devotion established, showing the character's method and specialty, not generic diligence → (2) a reassignment/order beat that changes their resources without changing their goal, explicitly tied to an already-established institutional event rather than invented from nothing → (3) real dialogue (category 5) with a subordinate about the emotional cost of a vigil that may never end → (4) a lead/hope beat, concrete, not abstract → (5) the lead's failure, specific and named, never vague — the point is a real, particular disappointment, not atmosphere → (6) a small protective/maternal beat toward a subordinate discouraged by the failure, characterizing the POV character through action rather than narrated compassion → (7) closing image of the vigil resuming, unresolved but resolute, without false hope and without despair. 6-7 slots. Added 2026-08-20 for Laila's introduction in the Libro II beat-planner pass.

16. **Amenaza descubierta + veredicto dividido / Threat discovered + divided verdict** (two captains under the same commander with complementary domains — one detects/fights, the other judges — hand a live incursion off into a judgment over the conduct of the first captain's own soldier, not the enemy's — e.g. `B2C23.5` "What the Staff Weighs," Raguel and Hadriel's introduction). Shape: (1) vigilance/detection established for the captain whose domain is security, showing method and specialty → (2) the incursion/combat beat, category 6, demonstrating that captain's established combat style directly → (3) capture, with a subordinate's already-fiched personality trait (not an invented quirk) pushing toward excess against a target that has stopped being a threat → (4) the detecting captain's hesitation — protective of her own soldier's judgment, but not blind to the problem (category 1) → (5) the judging captain summoned or already present, real dialogue (category 5) that actually weighs the situation rather than simply pronouncing a verdict → (6) the verdict delivered, costing something real to both the subordinate and the detecting captain, without humiliating either → (7) a closing coda tying back to the shared commander, silent or brief, matching that commander's already-established voice. 7 slots. Added 2026-08-20 for Raguel and Hadriel's introduction in the Libro II beat-planner pass.

15. **Cuerpo bajo doctrina / Corps under an unspoken doctrine** (POV: three subordinates with distinct temperaments carrying out a commander's mandate whose real reasoning he withholds from them — e.g. B2C36.7 "No One Trained to Hear It," Eshriel/Jahiel/Zaphiel's first scenes in the project). Shape: (1) the commander's private decision to route the mission through his own captains instead of the wider chain of command (category 1, characterizing the commander's caution before anyone else appears) → (2) the briefing, real category-5 dialogue where the three subordinates' fixed methods surface as open disagreement over means, not goal, and the commander gives a directive that deliberately withholds his deeper reason even under direct challenge → (3) arrival/establishing beat for the most volatile of the three, at a new minor location (category 4) → (4) a second subordinate's methodical assessment beat, revealing the true nature of the threat is not what the briefing assumed → (5) the third subordinate's temperament tested against emerging moral stakes (a bystander at risk, category 1), with real category-5 dialogue pushback from a colleague → (6) the action beat itself (category 6), each subordinate's signature power shown distinctly, resolving the physical threat without resolving the disagreement over method — keep this beat in ONE subordinate's tight-third POV throughout; describe the other two's actions only as that POV character externally observes them, never as their own interiority, or it becomes head-hopping → (7) the decision about how to close it out, made by whichever subordinate's expertise actually fits, not by rank → (8) return to the commander, who receives the result without disclosing what he privately recognizes in it, closing on the same withheld-knowledge tension he opened with, now passed down a level. 8 slots — runs to the high end because it needs three full character introductions plus the commander's own frame. Added 2026-08-20 for the Eshriel/Jahiel/Zaphiel interlude — Codex's independent read caught a real contradiction in the first draft (a detail all three subordinates witnessed in the field being described in the closing beat as something they'd "never been permitted to read"); when this pattern's field team recovers a physical document, decide explicitly and early what they did and didn't have context to recognize in it, rather than leaving "what they saw" ambiguous until the closing paragraph is drafted.

17. **Trampa y destino invertido / Trap and inverted fate** (two captains under the same commander whose domains combine mechanically in one field operation — an architect who builds the trap, and a fate-twister whose power only matters once the architect's certainty runs out — e.g. `B2C08.6` "What the Ring Corrects," Orobas and Malphas's first scenes in the project). Shape: (1) the shared commander's directive, brief and impatient, characterizing him by how little explanation he bothers to give (category 1, consistent with an already-established commander's voice — do not re-explain a personality trait his own prior chapter already demonstrated) → (2) the architect captain's domain established: building the terrain/trap, category 4 new minor location, precision and pride in the design as a craft → (3) real dialogue (category 5) between the two captains on arrival, establishing friction — the architect's calculated certainty versus the fate-twister's ambiguous, always-knows-more manner → (4) the trap sprung, category 6 combat, the architect's design working exactly as engineered → (5) a complication: one enemy breaks the design's logic — not through raw strength alone, but through a choice the trap didn't account for → (6) the fate-twister's power resolves it, shown as a distinct mechanism (swapping which of two combatants' outcomes actually lands), never as a generic magic save → (7) a private beat between the two captains afterward, real dialogue, that deepens the friction from beat 3 without resolving it → (8) closing image held in the fate-twister's ambiguity, not the architect's — leave open whether he steered the whole engagement from the start, and do NOT let the architect's private doubt/crisis surface here if that captain has a reserved future beat elsewhere in the outline (check `arco_argumental_completo.md` before writing this beat — a small, different unease is fine; the specific reserved doubt is not). 8 slots. Added 2026-08-20 for the Orobas/Malphas interlude.

18. **Prueba de lealtad / Loyalty test** (two captains under the same commander whose domains combine mechanically to extract the truth from a target's loyalty — one demolishes conviction to see what's underneath, the other supplies the threat that makes the demolition matter — e.g. `B3C44.5` "What the Cube Found," Flauros and Amy's first scenes in the project, and Agares's first real presence in the prose). Shape: (1) the shared commander's directive, in his own already-established voice (theatrical, persuasive, willing to let truth do a lie's work) tied explicitly to an already-written institutional task he's known to be performing, not invented from nothing → (2) the demolition captain's method established on a live target, category 4 new minor location, category 5 real dialogue — philosophical, patient, never physically threatening on its own → (3) friction beat, real dialogue (category 5) between the two captains, the second captain's impatience with the first's slow method → (4) the target's conviction gives way, but unevenly — reveals something true that isn't the thing the mission was sent to find, complicating rather than simply resolving → (5) the second captain forces the remainder, category 6, a threat or act of violence distinct from the first captain's method → (6) the two captains' aftermath dialogue (category 5) naming the friction from beat 3 as a standing disagreement, not resolved, seeded for future use → (7) the report loop back to the commander, closing on the demolition captain privately keeping back a piece of what she actually found, mirroring the commander's own established philosophy of arranged truth rather than invented lies. 7-8 slots. Added 2026-08-20 for the Flauros/Amy interlude — the whole point of this pattern is that the two captains' methods produce a truth messier than the mission asked for, and the closing beat should not resolve that mess into a clean report.

19. **Capitanes que prueban a un comandante herido / Captains testing a wounded commander** (two captains under the same commander react, in opposite directions, to a peer's death or setback that leaves their commander visibly weaker — one opportunistic, calculating whether the weakness is an opening to exploit; the other fatalistic, reading the same weakness as a trap for anyone foolish enough to test it — e.g. `B2C21.6` "The Grief They Mistook for Softness," Marbas and Raum's first scenes in the project, following directly on Malthus's death in B2C21.5). Shape: (1) the opportunistic captain's method/personality established through ordinary work, showing the specific cold calculation that reads the peer's death as a resource question rather than a loss (category 1) → (2) the fatalistic captain's method/personality established separately, ideally through their own signature power used on an unrelated subject, producing an unplanned, unsettling result that circles back toward the commander without the captain choosing it to (category 1, and a light touch of category 6 — the power in action, not combat) → (3) real dialogue (category 5) between the two captains, the opportunist proposing a concrete overreach dressed as initiative, the fatalist warning against it from instinct rather than sentiment → (4) the opportunist commits the overreach alone, shown as a real scene, not summarized in a single transitional paragraph — this beat is the chapter's central transgression and needs proportional space (~350-450 words), not a compressed status update → (5) the wounded commander's response, in his own already-established voice: cold, precise, and costly without being explosive — the punishment keeps the captain's usefulness intact while making the theft impossible to mistake for accepted initiative (category 5, real confrontation dialogue) → (6) the fatalist captain's brief aftermath scene with the commander, a quiet, unwarm acknowledgment that costs the commander nothing to give and the fatalist nothing to ask for → (7) closing reflection, from the commander's own POV, tying the small episode explicitly to a larger, still-unresolved debt or rivalry already established elsewhere in the canon (never invented fresh for this chapter) — the small test confirms the method the commander intends to use later, at greater stakes. 7 slots. Added 2026-08-20 for the Marbas/Raum interlude — keep any scene shift to a rival commander's own POV (even a single closing sentence) strictly behind its own `---` separator; asserting that rival's private certainty inside the wounded commander's own closing scene is a head-hop, not a thematic mirror, however tempting the parallel is.

20. **Disenso canalizado / Channeled dissent** (two captains under the same commander react to their commander's controversial, unconventional strategy — one out of professional paranoia specific to their own domain, the other out of simple restlessness at being denied their usual outlet — and the commander resolves neither objection with reassurance, only by handing each captain a concrete task that channels their fixed trait into something useful without settling the underlying disagreement — e.g. `B3C44.6` "The Door No One Sealed," Valefar and Vine's first scenes in the project, reacting to Vepar's alliance with a Host commander after Belial's fall). Shape: (1) the paranoid/security-minded captain's method established through routine vigilance, alarm specifically rooted in their own fiched domain (not generic distrust) at a strategy no one has audited → (2) the restless captain's contrasting temperament established through a concrete, non-combat use of their own signature power, showing what peacetime/restraint is costing them specifically → (3) real dialogue (category 5) between the two captains airing the concern, deliberately not the same objection in both mouths → (4) confrontation with the commander, real dialogue (category 5) in his own already-established voice — he does not reassure, he reasons, and closes by assigning each captain a task that fits their own domain exactly rather than resolving the doctrinal question → (5) the paranoid captain's task beat: a real, specific finding, small enough not to blow up the wider alliance, large enough to validate the underlying caution without vindicating the extreme version of it → (6) the restless captain's task beat, category 6, real action distinct from and safely separate from the political arrangement under dispute → (7) aftermath dialogue (category 5) between the two captains naming the disagreement from beat 3 as still open, explicitly not resolved by either captain's task → (8) closing coda from the commander's own POV, receiving both reports without full reassurance either way, implicitly contrasted against a rival commander's failure elsewhere in the same stretch of canon. 8 slots. Added 2026-08-20 for the Valefar/Vine interlude — this pattern's whole point is that channeling dissent into productive work is not the same as resolving it; do not let the commander's closing coda talk either captain out of their position, or the chapter loses the standing tension it was built to preserve.

If none fit, name the new pattern explicitly and add it to this list (this library is meant to grow, not stay fixed at 6).

## Step 2 — Fill empty slots with approved-category content, not filler

For each slot the current beat list doesn't already cover, pull the missing content from one of the 6 approved expansion categories in `expansion_guidelines.md` (secondary character development, in-story reincarnation/lore exposition, clarify who's who, new minor locations, more dialogue, more epic battles). Never invent a slot's content just to hit a beat count — if no approved-category content genuinely fits, the chapter's pattern classification in Step 1 was probably wrong; go back and re-classify rather than padding.

Every new beat follows the same consistency rule already in `expansion_guidelines.md`: no contradiction with a Fundamental Law or fixed character fiche; new lore gets marked explicitly for tracking; anything reintroduced (weapon, character, place) that won't reappear gets an explicit decision (intentional atmosphere vs. needs a future payoff).

## Step 3 — Check the math before handing off to prose

- Beat count: 6-8 (below 6 → not done; above 9 → check the chapter isn't secretly two chapters).
- Expected word count: `beats × 342-450`. Must land in 1,800-3,000, target 2,250-3,000.
- If the math lands short even at 8 beats, the pattern likely needs a 7th/8th slot from Step 2, not denser beats.
- **Dialogue check** (added 2026-08-10, after an audit found 50/53 planned chapters had zero category-5 beats and several written chapters — B1L01, B1L04, B1L05 — landed near 0% quoted dialogue): at least one beat must be real category-5 content, a conversation between two or more characters that reveals character, not narrated interiority or a single line of plot-transmission dialogue. The only exception is a chapter that explicitly declares itself dialogue-free as a deliberate tonal choice (e.g. pattern 9 "Interludio atmosférico") — and that declaration has to be written down, not assumed.

## Step 4 — Output

Write the expanded beat list back into the chapter's planning document (same format/level of detail as the existing beats — one-line summary + narrative function per beat, matching the style already used in `book1_lament_arc.md` and `book1_new_subplots_beats.md`). Note in the document which narrator pattern was used, for consistency tracking across the book.

## Relationship to chapter-review

This skill runs first, at planning time. `chapter-review` runs after, on the finished prose. A chapter that passed through beat-planner with a full 6-8 beat plan should never fail chapter-review's Pacing & Structure check for being under length — if it does, that's a signal the pattern-slot content in Step 2 was too thin when written, not that the plan was wrong.
