# B2C28 – The Convergence Point

Mikel returned to the special collections room that night with the map folded in his coat pocket, though he no longer needed to consult it. He had walked the route in his mind so many times over the past three days that his feet found it now without asking his attention for help — down the main stairwell past the reading room, through the service corridor the librarians used for reshelving, and from there into the older wing the university's own floor plans barely acknowledged still existed.

He had spent those three days telling himself he was simply confirming the research, cross-checking one final time before he trusted a conclusion this large. He understood, walking the corridor now with his flashlight's beam trembling slightly against walls that hadn't seen renovation in longer than the building's own records reliably tracked, that he had run out of honest reasons to delay any further. The foundation beneath this library was old in a way the rest of the campus wasn't, stone laid by hands that had nothing to do with the university's official founding date, and three independent manuscripts had agreed on this exact ground closely enough that Mikel no longer had room left to doubt them.

The passage narrowed as he went, the ceiling dropping low enough in places that he had to duck without breaking stride, and the air took on a mineral coldness that had nothing to do with the season outside. He passed doorways sealed decades ago with brick rather than proper doors, the university apparently having decided at some point that closing off whole sections of its own foundation was simpler than maintaining them. A rusted sign still hung crooked over one bricked archway, half its lettering worn past reading, and Mikel paused just long enough to make out the word RECORDS before he kept walking, unwilling to let a hundred-year-old bureaucratic decision slow him down tonight.

He had brought nothing with him tonight beyond the flashlight and the notebook, no tools, no weapon, nothing that might have made this feel like the kind of expedition a more careful man would have prepared for properly. He'd told himself, packing the bag hours earlier, that he was only confirming a location, that tonight was reconnaissance and nothing more. He didn't fully believe that anymore, standing in a passage this far beneath the building he'd taught in for nine years, but he kept walking regardless, past every sealed opening that wasn't the one his research had pointed him toward, until the corridor finally opened onto a heavier door than the rest — iron-banded, warded in some fashion he couldn't have named precisely but felt regardless, a faint resistance in the air the closer he drew to it.

He stood before it a long moment, flashlight beam settling steadily on the old ironwork, and felt the same cold certainty he'd felt sitting in his car after the map's three points had finally aligned. Whatever waited on the other side of this door had been part of his life for longer than he'd known to look for it, and he had no proper way yet to understand how long.

---

Arin picked up the trail an hour before Mikel reached the door, tracing the demonic signature through the campus's older maintenance tunnels with the same patient, methodical care he'd used to track every threat since the granary. The scout's interrogation three nights ago had given him more than he'd expected going in — a crossing point waking after years of dormancy, a strike timed to catch both signals together. He recognized the destination before he'd fully arrived at it.

The warded service door. He'd catalogued it nearly two weeks ago during his first sweep of this campus, tested its construction without forcing it, filed it away as one anomaly among several he hadn't yet earned the right to understand. He understood now. The harvesting operation, the disguised scout, the quiet office with its light burning too late, the ambient wrongness that had followed him through every corner of this deceptively ordinary campus — all of it led back to this exact stretch of forgotten stone.

He allowed himself, moving through the last stretch of tunnel, a single moment to weigh how far this hunt had carried him from where it had started. He'd come to Navarion chasing a harvesting operation, one demon and one nest to shut down before it grew any further. He was leaving that simple version of the job behind him tonight. What waited ahead now was a second signal, a portal, and a trap Belial had spent weeks preparing. He set the thought aside before it could slow him. Whatever this had become, it would be decided, one way or another, somewhere close to where he was walking now.

He moved through the tunnels with the tactical economy a decade of contracts had trained into him, checking each junction before committing to it, reading the ward's construction from a distance the way he'd read it the first time, searching now for whatever had changed in the two weeks since his first pass. The ward itself felt different in a way that took him a moment to place — hollowed out somehow, its old confusing pressure gone slack, more scar tissue now than active defense, though he couldn't have said what had disarmed it. Whatever slept beyond it hadn't been touched the same way. He counted two details the dulled ward couldn't account for: a faint new warmth radiating off the stone itself that hadn't been there before, and a low, almost inaudible hum running through the walls like something settling deeper into wakefulness with every hour that passed. Whatever slept beneath this door was closer to waking than it had been two weeks ago, and Arin felt the old, familiar tightening in his chest that had always preceded a job about to become considerably more dangerous than its opening moves had suggested.

---

Mikel passed the park bench on his way from the parking lot without a glance toward it, his attention already fully claimed by the corridor waiting ahead of him. He didn't register that the bench sat empty tonight, that the guitar case wasn't propped against its usual leg, that the low, unhurried music which had drifted across this corner of campus most evenings for weeks simply wasn't there to be missed. He had never once consciously catalogued the musician as a fixture of this place worth tracking, and a man doesn't notice the absence of something he never thought to count.

---

Arin crossed the park's northern edge an hour earlier still, moving fast and low through the trees, his focus narrowed entirely to the tunnel entrance and the trail he meant to follow through it. He passed within thirty yards of that same empty bench and never once glanced toward it either, his trained attention filtering out everything that didn't bear on the hunt ahead of him. The bench, and whatever its emptiness might have told him, simply never entered the calculation.

---

Mikel reached the iron-banded door first, though only by minutes, and pressed his palm flat against the cold metal without yet attempting to force it. The ward's resistance pushed back faintly against his skin, an unpleasant pressure he couldn't have described to anyone who hadn't felt it themselves, and he held his hand there anyway, some stubborn scholar's instinct in him needing to confirm with his own body what his research had already told him with certainty.

He crouched to study the ironwork more closely, running his flashlight's beam along a line of symbols worked into the door's lower band, half-effaced by age but still faintly legible under close attention. He recognized fragments of two separate scripts among them, one distantly related to a liturgical hand he'd cross-referenced weeks ago and quickly abandoned as a dead end, the other entirely unfamiliar. Neither matched anything from the folklore he'd spent months collecting. That fact unsettled him more than finding nothing at all would have. Whatever had sealed this door had done so using a vocabulary older than the myths he'd built his entire search around, older, he suspected, than the myths themselves had any memory of.

He heard the footsteps before he saw anything, careful and deliberate, approaching from a passage he hadn't known connected to this one, and went still against the door with his flashlight snapped off, heart suddenly loud enough in his own ears to drown out the sound he was straining to place. He had come down here half expecting to face whatever waited beyond that door alone. He had not expected to find he wasn't the only one who'd found this place tonight.

---

Arin heard it too, from his own side of the passage, the soft scrape of a shoe against old stone that didn't belong to anything he'd catalogued about this location across nearly two weeks of careful watching. He stopped mid-step, one hand already closing loosely around the door's edge, every trained instinct in him assessing the sound for threat before he'd consciously decided to. Not a demon's footfall. Too careful for a careless scout, too hesitant for a trained soldier. He ran through every possibility his tactical mind could offer — a lost security guard, a graduate student who'd wandered too far off the marked path, a second scout he hadn't yet catalogued — and discarded each of them in turn, none of them fitting the particular quality of the sound reaching him now.

Something else was standing on the other side of this door, close enough now that Arin could feel a second presence pressing against the same ward he'd been reading for weeks, a presence that felt, against every tactical expectation he carried into this moment, oddly familiar. He held perfectly still, weight balanced and ready to move either toward the sound or away from it, and let the seconds stretch out while he searched for whatever it was about that stillness on the other side of the door that had unsettled his instincts more than an outright threat would have. It took him a moment longer than he'd have liked to admit that the unsettling part wasn't threat at all. It was the absence of it, where every hard-won instinct he carried told him threat should have been.

---

Mikel didn't reach to force the door open. He stayed pressed against the cold iron instead, close enough now that he could make out the faint, unmistakable sound of someone else breathing on its far side, through the narrow gap where the frame had warped with age.

He felt it there as a kind of recognition he had no proper name for, a plain certainty arriving the same way the manuscripts' three converging points had arrived days earlier, except this had nothing to do with research. He had spent this entire search bracing himself for a danger he couldn't name, rehearsing, in whatever quiet moments the work allowed him, what he might do if the thing waiting at the end of it turned out to be exactly as dangerous as every fragment of folklore had warned. None of that bracing matched what he felt now, standing this close to whoever waited on the door's far side. Whoever stood there was not, whatever his research or his fear tried to insist otherwise, the danger he had spent months quietly preparing himself to face. He finally lowered his hand from the metal and sat back against the opposite wall, willing to wait this out rather than force a confrontation he no longer felt certain was a confrontation at all.

---

Arin didn't force the door either, listening instead to the same faint breathing carrying through the same warped gap in the frame. He felt an unbidden reluctance to force the encounter, entirely unwelcome to the trained caution that had kept him alive across a decade of contracts and a month of careful hunting since. He had learned, across that whole long month, to trust nothing that hadn't earned his trust first, a rule that had kept him breathing through worse odds than a locked door in a forgotten basement. And yet some older, quieter part of him refused the caution outright. Whatever waited less than an arm's length away on the door's far side was not, whatever the tactical part of his mind still wanted to assume, an enemy. He settled into the same patient stillness he'd have used to wait out a target, except this waiting carried none of a hunt's usual weight, and stayed there a long while in the cold, mineral dark, certain of something he couldn't have explained to anyone who asked him to.
