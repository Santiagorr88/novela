# B1T04 – What Neither of Them Keeps

## The Real Find

Cassiel found it at the center of the island's spiraling paths, in a clearing where bare stone broke through the surrounding growth — an echo carved into both rock and sound at once, unmistakably tied to Solmire, older than anything he had encountered on any of his previous visits. He knelt beside it a long while, letting Silens draw out what detail it could. No name surfaced from the reading. No face accompanied it either. What came through instead was a plain, negative certainty, nothing he could build an argument from but enough that he trusted it without reservation: whatever account Heaven currently held of the sword's origin, this resonance did not agree with it. The reading carried one further detail, faint but unmistakable once he isolated it — this echo predated Miguel's own claim to the blade by some span of time Silens could not measure precisely, old enough that the sword had plainly existed, and mattered to someone, long before Miguel had ever touched it.

He sat with that certainty a long moment, turning it over the way he turned over any difficult reading, patient even now with something that unsettled him more than anything the island's spiraling terrain had thrown at him so far. This was his fourth crossing to Turein, and he still did not fully understand what had first drawn him back after the initial visit. He understood, at least, that whatever he had expected to eventually find here, a contradiction with no name attached to fill it was not it.

He considered, kneeling there, exactly how he would carry this finding back to anyone who needed to hear it. Iofiel would want specifics he did not have — a name, a face, some detail concrete enough to build an argument around. He had none of that to offer her, only a resonance that contradicted the official account and nothing further he could honestly claim beyond that contradiction. He had spent his entire service learning to trust exactly this kind of quiet certainty over anything louder or more immediately provable. He did not intend to abandon that trust now, however little the finding actually amounted to laid out plainly in his own mind.

He rose eventually, memorizing the clearing's exact position the way he had memorized it on his first visit, unwilling to let the island's own strange geography cost him his way back here again. The resonance held steady beneath his renewed attention, unaffected by his leaving it, waiting for nothing in particular except whatever further reading he might eventually choose to give it.

## The Confrontation

He felt the presence behind him before he heard anything, some old instinct sharpening past the meditative calm he had held throughout the crossing, and turned to find a demon standing at the clearing's edge, bow already in hand though not yet drawn. Cassiel rose slowly, Silens shifting into a ready grip without quite becoming a weapon raised in earnest. Neither of them spoke for a long moment. The demon's stance read, as far as Cassiel could judge from across the clearing, more measured than aggressive — a hunter's readiness rather than an immediate intention to strike, though he was not prepared to stake anything on that reading yet.

He held his own ground rather than retreating toward the resonance behind him, unwilling to let the demon see exactly how much he might have to protect if the confrontation actually turned to violence. Whatever this stranger already knew about the clearing, about Solmire, about anything Cassiel had spent four visits carefully piecing together, giving away more than necessary now seemed like precisely the wrong instinct to follow.

He studied the demon's bearing for any further detail worth reading — the particular way he held the bow, neither raised nor fully lowered, the stillness in his stance that suggested, as far as Cassiel could judge, long practice rather than nervous hesitation. This did not look, to Cassiel's eye, like a soldier caught unprepared by an unexpected encounter. Whatever brought him to this exact clearing, he seemed to have arrived with some plan already in mind for exactly this possibility.

The silence between them stretched longer than Cassiel found entirely comfortable, though he made no move to break it first. He had learned, across centuries of coaxing wounded souls back from the edge of their own panic, that the first person to speak in a tense silence often surrendered more advantage than the silence itself was worth. He held his ground and waited, Silens steady in his grip, trusting the demon's own evident reluctance to close the distance as much as his own patience to eventually produce whatever came next.

## Selm's Choice

Selm studied the scout across the clearing, weighing the shape of the confrontation with the same flat calculation he brought to any assessment of a fight he had not yet committed to. The angle was clean enough. The scout looked capable but not, from what Selm had already observed across two days of careful watching, especially eager for violence himself. A fight here would cost time neither of them plainly had to spare, and might well destroy or scatter whatever this place actually held before either side properly understood it. Selm had learned long ago that a fight won at the cost of the actual objective rarely counted as a victory worth having.

He ran the calculation the way he ran any before committing to violence — cost against benefit, certainty against risk, the specific value of whatever this scout might still be carrying weighed against the simple satisfaction of an easy kill. Two days of patient observation had taught him more about this particular opponent than any single fight would reveal in its opening seconds. He knew the scout's temperament now, his caution, the particular unhurried care he brought to unfamiliar ground. Killing him now would end any chance of learning more, cutting off the one remaining source who might still lead him to a fuller understanding of this place.

There was also the matter of what waited behind the scout, in whatever clearing he had finally reached after two days of careful, deliberate searching. Selm had followed this trail precisely because Vepar's apparatus wanted to understand what it actually was, not simply to eliminate whoever else happened to be looking for it. A body left here answered none of the actual questions his orders required him to answer. He preferred, given the choice, to leave with something worth reporting rather than a kill that told Hell nothing beyond the fact that a rival scout had once existed and now did not.

He had already gathered one detail worth carrying home regardless of how the rest of this encounter resolved: the scout's own bearing, the fierce, guarded stillness with which he held his ground rather than retreat toward whatever lay behind him. A man did not defend empty ground that way. Whatever sat in that clearing, the scout's own posture had already confirmed it mattered enough to be worth defending with his life if it came to that — confirmation Selm intended to report back exactly as he had read it, plainly, without embellishment.

He lowered Specter, not fully, only enough to signal the intention without committing to it as a promise. "I haven't come here to fight," he said, his voice carrying none of the urgency the moment might otherwise have demanded.

## What Passed Between Them

"Good," Cassiel said, Silens still held ready but no closer to becoming a weapon than the demon's own bow. "Because I don't have time to waste on this either — my own side already has enough weighing on it." Selm was silent a moment before answering in kind. "Mine as well," he said. Neither of them offered anything further, the exchange settling into a silence that felt, to Cassiel's ear, less like hostility than a shared, unspoken acknowledgment that whatever waited beyond this clearing mattered considerably more than whatever satisfaction either of them might have taken from a fight neither could fully afford.

Cassiel studied the demon a moment longer, searching for any sign the restraint was tactical rather than genuine, and found nothing he could point to with real confidence either way. It did not especially matter, he decided. The outcome, whatever the reasoning behind it, amounted to the same thing either of them actually needed right now.

He allowed himself, watching the demon's continued stillness, a small measure of professional curiosity about what exactly had brought Hell's own hunters to this same remote clearing. The demon's presence here, on ground so far outside any charted route that finding it at all required more than luck, seemed unlikely to be mere coincidence. Whatever had drawn Cassiel's own instincts here weeks ago, it seemed likely that something comparable had reached the other side as well. That possibility alone was worth carrying home, whatever else came of this particular encounter.

He wondered, too, whether the demon opposite him carried any better answer than Cassiel's own fragmentary reading — some fuller understanding of what this place actually was, purchased through methods Heaven's own scouts would never have access to. He did not ask. Whatever advantage either side held today, neither of them seemed inclined to trade it away over idle curiosity, and Cassiel was not about to be the one who broke that unspoken understanding first.

## Parting Ground

Neither of them moved first for a long moment, the standoff settling into something closer to mutual assessment than active tension, until Selm finally stepped back toward the treeline, bow still lowered but not put away. "We both walk away with something," he said, not quite a question. "We both walk away with something," Cassiel agreed, offering nothing further about what exactly he had already found. Selm's expression gave away nothing in return, and Cassiel understood, watching him retreat into the spiraling growth, that he would likely never know precisely what the demon had actually taken from this place, any more than the demon would ever know the full shape of what Cassiel now carried back with him.

Neither side had won cleanly. Cassiel left the clearing with the resonance's confirmation intact in memory, nothing physical to show for it, and no illusion that Hell's own hunter had left entirely empty-handed either. It was not the outcome he might have hoped for on his very first crossing to this island. It was, weighed against every alternative that confrontation might have produced instead, one he could accept without regret.

He made his way back across Turein's spiraling terrain at the same unhurried pace that had carried him through every stretch of it so far, unwilling to abandon the discipline that had gotten him this far simply because the day's most immediate danger had already passed. The light overhead moved through its familiar strange colors as he walked, a phenomenon he no longer bothered questioning, and the paths themselves, whatever governed them, offered him no further resistance on the way out. Whatever test the island had set for him across four separate visits, he suspected he had finally passed it, or at least earned enough of its patience to be allowed to leave.

He thought, walking, of the demon's parting words, the shared acknowledgment that neither side had claimed the whole of whatever this place actually held. It was an uncomfortable kind of victory to carry home, incomplete and unresolved in ways his own service rarely tolerated. He carried it anyway, all the way back across open water and onto solid, ordinary ground, already turning over in his mind exactly how he would explain an incomplete finding to a Council that generally preferred its evidence considerably more finished than this.

## What Cassiel Reported

He brought his findings to Iofiel two days later, once the journey back and the ordinary demands of his other duties had finally given him a proper window to request her attention, choosing his words with the same careful restraint he had held throughout the entire assignment and offering only what he could actually confirm, nothing his own speculation might have added on top of it. "Solmire's origin isn't what the Council currently believes," he told her, watching her expression shift from mild professional interest into something considerably more guarded. "I don't have a name. I don't have a face. What I found predates Miguel's own claim to the blade — and it doesn't agree with the official account. That's all I can honestly confirm."

Iofiel said nothing for a long moment, turning the report over with the same careful attention Cassiel recognized from her own work on the Codex Mortalis. "That's not nothing," she said finally. "It isn't evidence I can bring to Gabriel yet, either." Cassiel offered no further certainty than what he actually had, unwilling to overstate a finding this fragile even to someone he trusted with the rest of it.

"There's something else," he added, choosing the detail carefully. "I wasn't the only one interested in that particular stretch of water. Hell had someone searching that ground as well." Iofiel's expression sharpened at that, a different kind of attention entirely from the one she had given the resonance itself. "Did it come to a fight?" she asked. "No," Cassiel said. "We both left with something. Neither of us with everything." He did not elaborate further, and Iofiel, after a moment's consideration, did not press him for more than he had already offered.

Whatever the resonance on Turein actually meant, he understood, watching Iofiel file the report away among her own still-unfinished work on the Codex, it would take considerably more than one careful scout's testimony before the Council knew what to do with it at all. He had done what his own service required of him — found what could be found, reported what could honestly be confirmed, and left the rest for wiser judgment than his own to eventually resolve.
