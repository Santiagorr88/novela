# B3C03 – A Path to Hell

The passage had gone colder with every step since the echo of laughter reached him, and Thaeriel walked it without slowing, the sound still resonating somewhere behind his sternum long after the last note of it should have faded. He did not know what had made that sound, or why hearing it had loosened something in his chest that three days of walking alone hadn't managed to loosen on its own. He knew only that it had, and that he trusted the feeling enough to keep walking toward whatever waited at the passage's end rather than away from it. The passage itself gave him nothing to navigate by beyond its own steady downward slope, the walls close enough on either side that he could trail his fingers along both at once if he chose to, though he mostly didn't bother.

The rock around him gave way gradually to open air, and he stepped out at last onto a stretch of desert scarred by some old war he didn't recognize — cracked earth, the blackened ribs of structures that had burned decades ago and never been rebuilt, silence of the particular kind that settles over ground nobody has bothered to reclaim. Not Hell. Not yet. He understood, standing there, that the passage had delivered him back to the ordinary world first, one final stretch of Earth to cross before the real descent began.

He walked it without hurrying. He had learned, across the last month, that hurry rarely improved anything he was walking toward. The sun rose and set twice while he crossed that stretch of ruin, and he ate little, sleeping when his body demanded it rather than on any set schedule, the way he'd once slept on long hunts before he'd had a name for what he actually was. He passed a rusted vehicle half-buried in sand, its make and model foreign to him, evidence of some conflict fought here long before he'd ever walked the earth in either of his lives. He didn't stop to study it. Whatever war had scarred this place belonged to it alone, unconnected to the one he carried inside himself.

---

He found the Hellgate two days later, at the base of a narrow canyon whose walls ran a deeper black than the desert around it, as if the rock itself had been scorched from within long before any war reached this place. It was not the grand and burning threshold he might have pictured once, back when he'd still thought of himself as Arin Cross and imagined the underworld the way frightened men imagine it — a heat-haze instead, shimmering above a fissure of obsidian, faint voices repeating themselves somewhere inside the shimmer without ever quite resolving into words. He stood at its edge a long moment, listening to those voices without trying to make out what they said.

He placed a hand flat against the stone, not to force the fissure wider, but simply to feel what it actually was. The rock was warm under his palm in a way ordinary stone never ran warm, a heat that came from somewhere far beneath rather than from the sun overhead. He felt, beneath that warmth, something else — a kind of pressure, old and patient, the sensation of a boundary that had been tested a great many times by a great many things and had never once actually broken. He was not here to break it either. He simply needed it to let him through.

He stood there long enough that the light shifted noticeably behind him, the shadows in the ravine stretching and merging as the sun crossed toward evening. He had expected, walking here, that arriving would feel like a kind of relief, the end of a long and difficult approach. It didn't. It felt instead like standing at the edge of a decision he'd already made weeks ago, one that simply hadn't finished asking anything more of him yet.

---

Before he stepped forward, he closed his eyes, and let himself see, for the length of one held breath, the man he had been. Arin Cross, moving through a decade of contracts with the same flat certainty he'd once mistaken for peace. The grit of a hundred small towns. The particular ache of a debt collected badly, or collected well and regretted anyway. He did not push the memory away. He held it instead and examined it the way a craftsman examines a blade before deciding whether it still holds an edge, and found that it did — not as rage anymore, not as the force that had once driven him, but as a whetstone, something that had sharpened him toward exactly this moment without his ever understanding, at the time, what he was being sharpened for.

His purpose had changed since then, and he let himself say the shape of it plainly, one final time before he crossed. He was not here to punish Belial. He had told himself that lie in a dozen forms across the last month, and he no longer needed it. He was here to restore something to its true function, a weapon that had been made to carry grief honestly and had instead spent centuries being made to carry someone else's theft. That distinction mattered more to him now than anything vengeance had ever offered.

He thought, briefly, of the broker he'd called before leaving, the last conversation of his old life closed cleanly instead of left to rot the way most of his old ties had been left. He thought of Mikel, somewhere on his own road, chasing a different kind of answer. He did not know where either thread would lead by the time this was finished. He found he could hold that uncertainty without it costing him anything, a steadiness that would have been impossible for the man he'd been even two months ago.

---

He stepped through the shimmer and felt the world change around him in a single motion, heat and noise arriving all at once, the outer circle of Hell opening before him in every direction he could see. It struck him fully, the way it would have struck anyone — the heat pressed against his skin, real and considerable, and the roar of distant torment filled his ears with a volume that took real effort to hold steady against. He was not immune to this place. Every step forward cost him something, a deliberate, sustained concentration he had to keep actively spending, the way a man wades against a current rather than simply walking on dry ground.

But the flames nearest to him wavered as he approached, drawing back by some fraction that had nothing to do with wind or fuel, and the screaming closest to his path fell quiet for the length of a breath before resuming somewhere further off. Not because the fire couldn't touch him. He suspected, if he stood still long enough, it eventually would. It hesitated instead, the way any creature hesitates before something it has no framework to classify, a moment of animal uncertainty rather than actual retreat. He kept walking, unwilling to test how long that uncertainty would hold if he stopped moving through it.

---

He walked for what felt like hours through that outer circle, the effort of it settling into his shoulders the way a long march settles into a soldier's legs. He didn't let the discomfort slow his pace. He passed the burned husks of structures that had never been built to withstand a war, passed things he chose not to look at too closely, and kept his attention fixed instead on the sensation growing steadily stronger somewhere ahead of him — not a sound, not quite, but something adjacent to sound, a resonance he recognized the way a man recognizes his own name spoken in another room.

A pair of lesser demons crossed his path early in that walk, low creatures more animal than intelligent, drawn by the scent of something that didn't belong in their territory. They took one look at him and scattered without engaging, the same hesitant retreat he'd already felt from the flames themselves. He didn't slow to watch them go. He had the growing sense, walking deeper into that outer circle, that nothing here was going to test him directly until something with enough will to actually decide to.

Lament. Still far from him, still held by hands that had no right to hold it, but close enough now that he could feel the shape of its presence the way he'd once felt the shape of an old wound on a cold morning. It was not calling to him exactly. It simply existed nearby, and its existing nearby was enough to pull at something in his chest that had been pulled at for longer than he'd had proper words for the sensation.

He tried, walking toward that pull, to name exactly what he felt from it, and found the attempt harder than he'd expected. Not longing, precisely. Not anger either, though anger would have been the easier and more familiar thing to name. It felt closer to recognition, the specific, quiet certainty of approaching something that had been waiting, patiently and for a very long time, to finally stop waiting. He did not know if the spear itself could feel anything at all in the way he understood feeling. He suspected, walking closer to it with every step, that the distinction mattered less than he'd have once assumed.

---

In Dis, in a private chamber Belial had not left in three days, the spear trembled once across his knees, a small and sudden motion he felt before he consciously registered it. He set down the report he'd been reading and looked at the weapon with the particular attention of a man noticing something familiar behave in a way it never had before. Lament had trembled before, countless times, in the low, familiar rhythm of a grief that never fully quieted. This was different. This carried an eagerness he had never once felt from it in all the years he'd carried it, something closer to recognition than to pain.

He gave it his full attention, unwilling to let even a stray glance elsewhere distract him from whatever it was about to tell him, the commanders he'd meant to summon forgotten for the moment entirely. He did not understand, in that first instant, exactly what the tremor meant. He understood, a moment later, watching the light along the spear's edge brighten faintly of its own accord, that it did not need him to understand. It was not asking his permission to feel whatever it was feeling. It was simply announcing, in the only language it had ever spoken to him honestly, that something it recognized was drawing closer, one deliberate step at a time, through the burning circles that separated it from him.

He set his own hand against the haft, an old and habitual gesture worn into him across every night he'd carried the weapon since taking it, and found the tremor hadn't stopped, hadn't even slowed. If anything it had strengthened under his touch, a current running through the metal that felt, for the first time in all the years he'd carried it, like something almost cheerful. That word landed strangely in his own mind, applied to a weapon that had never once given him anything but grief in every other year of their acquaintance. He set the thought aside. Whatever the spear was feeling, it plainly wasn't going to explain itself to him tonight, any more than it ever had before.

Belial rose from his chair, unhurried on the surface and entirely unsettled underneath it, and gave the order to summon his commanders. Whatever was coming, he intended to meet it standing, and armed with rather more than an uneasy weapon's sudden, unexplainable joy. He crossed to the window of his chamber and looked out over the burning circles below, searching without much hope for any sign of what had put that tremor in the spear's haft. He saw nothing unusual. The fires burned the way they always burned, the distant screams continued the way they always continued, and somewhere beneath all of it, patient and unhurried, something was still walking closer.
