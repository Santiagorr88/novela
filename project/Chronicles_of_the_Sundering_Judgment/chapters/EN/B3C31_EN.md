# B3C31 – The War Against Nothingness

Camael stood at the prow of his flagship, watching Thamorak fill the whole of the forward viewport, and understood immediately that whatever they had faced in every prior engagement had only ever been this thing's outer edge. It no longer simply spread. It pulsed, a slow, deliberate rhythm that reminded him of nothing so much as a heartbeat scaled up past anything a heart was ever meant to hold. It was, for the first time since this campaign had begun, unmistakably awake.

He had read every report that described its earlier, smaller manifestations — a border valley, a lonely watchtower, an entire realm swallowed whole in the space of a few terrible minutes. None of those reports had prepared him for the scale of the thing actually waiting at the end of this crossing, vast enough now that his own flagship's sensors struggled to render its true edges on any display built for measuring ordinary threats.

---

He gave the order without further hesitation, unwilling to let the sight of it cost him the momentum this crossing had spent so much to earn. "All ships, engage," he said, and felt the fleet answer as a single body, gold and black hulls surging forward together across the last stretch of open space that remained.

Something old and clean rose in him as the ships broke into their attack vectors, a fury he recognized from a thousand smaller battles but had never once felt aimed at a target this vast. He had buried enough soldiers to distrust anger as a strategy. He let himself feel it anyway, this once, because the alternative — facing this thing with nothing but cold calculation — struck him as its own kind of surrender.

He thought, watching the gold shield surge forward exactly as drilled, of every soldier who had questioned this alliance in the weeks leading to this hour, every officer who had asked him privately whether trusting Hell's discipline was worth the risk it carried. He had no answer left to give any of them beyond what the fleet was currently doing without hesitation — holding formation, gold and black together, against an enemy that made every older grievance between them look, for the length of this crossing at least, entirely beside the point.

---

Vepar felt the charge differently, watching his own dark hull cut through the gap between formations toward the waiting abyss. Where what he could see of the gold ranks alongside him looked like righteous fury given form, his own Legions answered with something colder, a hunger for the fight itself that Vepar had spent centuries cultivating in every soldier under his command. He found the sensation settling over him now with unusual clarity — not hatred, not even ambition, simply the clean, familiar pleasure of a battle finally worth the name.

He studied the old enmity he still carried for the gold ships flanking his own formation, and found it, for the length of this charge at least, oddly absent. Whatever centuries of war had built between the Host and his Legions belonged to a fight that suddenly looked small next to the one bearing down on both of them now. He gave his own order without ceremony, matching Camael's pace exactly.

"Hold the line," Vepar said. "Whatever comes out of that dark, we answer it together, or we do not answer it at all."

He watched his own bridge crew relay the order down through the ranks, none of them questioning it the way he half expected some part of his command to, even now, after weeks of drilling this exact cooperation into them. He thought of the compromise he'd struck with Camael weeks ago, the forward shield he'd argued against on principle before agreeing to it on grounds of simple necessity, and found himself, watching that same shield now hold steady ahead of his own advancing ranks, no longer entirely certain which of those two motives had actually been closer to the truth.

---

The horrors came as the fleets closed the final distance, and Camael understood within seconds that nothing in his long service had prepared him to name what he was actually looking at. The abyss's surface convulsed and released shapes that broke every rule of form he'd ever trusted his eyes to report accurately — angles that shouldn't close, distances that refused to hold steady. Some unfolded into being with a sound his own ears insisted on reading as a scream, though nothing about the horrors themselves suggested they possessed a voice to make one with.

They did not attack with claw or fire. They simply arrived, and where they arrived, the laws governing that particular stretch of space came apart around them. A gold cruiser at the formation's edge lost its shape entirely as one of the horrors passed near it, hull and crew alike ceasing to hold any coherent form at all, gone before Camael's own instruments could even register what kind of weapon had caused it. He had trained his entire career to answer force with force, strategy with counter-strategy. Nothing in that training offered him a single useful instinct against an enemy that required no strategy at all to win.

"No visible attack," his second reported, voice tight. "It's not weapons fire. It's just... proximity."

"Then we don't let them get close," Camael said, though even as he gave the order he understood how thin a defense that actually was against something that unmade reality simply by existing near it. He watched three more ships lose their shape in the time it took him to finish the thought, and felt, for the first time in this entire campaign, something close to real fear settle into the certainty he'd carried into this battle.

He had commanded soldiers through losses that still weighed on him years afterward, had learned to carry that weight without letting it slow his hand when the moment demanded steadiness instead of grief. This felt different. Grief, at least, arrived after the loss was already counted. Here, he found himself grieving ships and crews he hadn't yet lost, watching the horrors close on formation after formation with a patience that suggested they had no need to hurry toward an outcome they clearly considered already settled.

A black-hulled cruiser near the formation's flank took the same fate a moment later, its crew's readiness reports simply ceasing mid-transmission, no final cry, no wreckage left to mark where they'd been standing only seconds before. Camael heard Vepar's voice cut across the shared channel a moment after, sharper than the flat calm he'd carried through every prior exchange between them.

"We lost the *Hollow Crown*," Vepar said. "No warning. No resistance offered. It simply stopped being there." A pause, brief but unmistakable, before the next order came. "Tighten formation. Do not let a single ship drift far enough to face this alone."

---

The formless horrors pressed deeper into the combined formation, and Camael found himself, within minutes, fighting on his own flagship's outer hull rather than commanding from its bridge, his blade meeting something that had no proper edge to parry against and somehow yielding anyway. Around him, soldiers from both fleets fought the same impossible battle side by side, gold armor and black scale pressed shoulder to shoulder against an enemy that recognized neither uniform.

A massive shape broke through the press near him, horned and armored in obsidian plate, swinging a war-axe with the kind of brutal, practiced economy Camael recognized from a dozen intelligence reports he'd read on Hell's field commanders. He knew the face beneath the helm the instant their eyes met — a Baron he had personally marked for death only days before this alliance had made killing him unthinkable.

Neither of them spoke. The Baron simply turned, putting his back against Camael's own, and swung his axe into the space Camael's blade couldn't quite reach in time. Camael answered in kind a moment later, cutting down whatever had been closing on the Baron's blind side, and found the two of them falling, without a single word exchanged, into a rhythm neither had ever drilled for — one the Baron, judging by the ease of it, seemed to understand every bit as instinctively as Camael did himself.

They fought like that for what felt like hours, back to back, an old enemy's blade the only thing standing between Camael and whatever the horrors would have done to his exposed flank. He thought, in whatever fragments of thought the battle actually left him room for, of every argument he'd made to his own officers about trust being earned rather than given — and understood, feeling the Baron's axe swing true yet again at his unguarded side, that this particular trust had simply been forced into existence by something neither of them had any choice but to answer together.

A screaming lattice of impossible angles pressed close on Camael's left, and he cut through it without fully understanding what his blade had actually connected with, only that the thing unraveled at the strike and did not reform. Beside him, the Baron roared something in a tongue Camael didn't recognize and buried his axe in a hound-shaped horror that dissolved into nothing the instant the blow landed, leaving only a faint, wrongness-tinged absence where it had briefly existed. Neither of them paused to acknowledge the kill. There wasn't time, and neither, Camael suspected, particularly wanted to spend what little breath he had left on words that wouldn't change anything about the next horror already pressing in to replace the last.

He caught, in the brief lull between one wave of horrors and the next, a glimpse of Vepar's own flagship further down the line, black hull scarred but still holding formation, its own soldiers locked in the same desperate, wordless cooperation with whatever gold-armored angel happened to be fighting nearest them. He found the sight steadying in a way he hadn't expected, proof that whatever was happening on his own hull wasn't some isolated fluke of circumstance but the shape the entire battle had actually taken, ship after ship, without anyone above them needing to order it.

The tide of horrors broke at last, retreating back toward the abyss's churning edge as suddenly as it had come, leaving the formation battered but intact, blood and black ichor mingling unremarked on the hull plating beneath them both. Camael lowered his blade, breathing hard, and found the Baron studying him for a long moment across the wreckage of the fight they'd just survived together.

The Baron said nothing. He gave a single, curt nod, the closest thing to acknowledgment Camael suspected the man was willing to offer, and turned back toward his own ranks without another word. Camael watched him go, and found himself, in the sudden quiet, unable to fully name what had actually just passed between them — not friendship, not yet, but something considerably harder to dismiss than the old hatred Camael himself had walked into this fight still carrying.

He thought of the death warrant he'd have signed on that same Baron's name only days ago without a second thought, back when the war they were both fighting still had a single, simple shape to it. That warrant felt, standing here now with the man's ichor still drying on his own blade, like it belonged to a considerably smaller war than the one currently pressing against every ship in this formation.

He straightened, wiping the Baron's ichor from his blade, and looked back out at the abyss, still pulsing, still waiting, patient in a way that made every ship he'd just watched fight and bleed feel small against the scale of what remained ahead.

His second reached him a moment later, damage reports already compiled, casualty counts he could tell from the man's expression alone were higher than either of them wanted to speak aloud yet. Camael took the report without flinching from it, filing each number away the way he'd filed every prior loss across this long campaign, unwilling to let the weight of it slow whatever came next.

"Hold formation," he said, his voice steadier than he currently felt. "We hold as long as it takes."
