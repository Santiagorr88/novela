# B3C16 – The Sage's Plan

The valley dissolved around them without transition, no flash of light or lurch of motion, simply one place giving way to another the instant Azael willed it so. Michael found himself standing in a sanctuary that existed, as far as he could tell, entirely outside the ordinary flow of time — a mountain summit under a sky that held neither sun nor stars, wind that waited rather than blew, stillness so complete it made the valley they'd just left feel almost noisy by comparison.

Ereloth and Thaeriel stood on either side of him, both studying the strange sky with the particular ease of men returning to a room they'd once known well. Michael watched them a moment longer than either of them likely noticed, and understood, with a clarity that settled rather than stung, exactly what he was looking at. Neither of these two was his brother, not in the sense they were each other's. Whatever bond held between the three primordial beings standing in this sanctuary had been forged in an age before Michael existed to share in anything at all. He found the understanding cost him nothing to hold. His place here didn't depend on being counted a fourth member of something that had only ever belonged to three.

Thaeriel stood a little apart from the other two, dust from Hell's roads still faint on him even here, his posture holding the particular readiness of a man who had paused something urgent rather than finished it. Michael recognized that readiness. Whatever Thaeriel had left behind in Hell, he intended to return to it the moment this gathering allowed him to.

Michael took the sanctuary in slowly, unwilling to let its strangeness pass without proper notice. No wind stirred the grass at his feet, and yet the air carried a faint, patient motion regardless, as though it simply hadn't decided yet whether moving was worth the effort. He had stood in strange places before, had commanded from thrones balanced on the edge of realms most mortals would never glimpse. None of them had carried this particular quality of waiting, patient enough to outlast anyone impatient enough to disturb it.

---

Azael settled onto a flat stone at the sanctuary's center, and the other three arranged themselves around him without any visible signal passing between them. Ereloth and Thaeriel each found their places as though returning to something long familiar; Michael simply followed their lead, falling into the open space between them without needing to be told where it was. He studied each of their faces in turn, unhurried, before he finally spoke.

"It has a name," he said, calm in a way that made the words land heavier rather than lighter. "Thamorak. The Throbbing Void." He paused, letting the name settle into the stillness around them. "But it is not an 'it.' It is a 'we.'"

Michael felt the weight of that correction before he understood its full meaning. Azael continued, his voice never rising, never hurrying toward the difficulty of what he was actually saying. "It is the scar tissue of the First Sundering," he said. "The echo of our own unbalanced principles, given enough time and enough neglect to grow past anything we could still recognize as ourselves. Judgment without mercy. Creation without purpose. Justice without compassion." He looked at Thaeriel as he said the first, at Ereloth as he said the second, and let the third settle onto no one in particular, or perhaps onto all three of them equally. "It has grown into a hunger now, cancerous and vast, feeding on everything that once held meaning for us."

"It is our child," Azael said, and for the first time since Michael had met him, something in that even voice caught, however briefly. "And it has come home to devour its parents."

Michael let the claim settle in him fully before responding to it, running back through every fragment of report he'd gathered across the last several weeks — a watchtower's final broken message, a border fleet's light and fire both swallowed whole, a horror repeated too many times in too many corners of the world to still be dismissed as coincidence. Every fragment fit precisely into the shape Azael had just given it. He found no comfort in the fit. He had hoped, some quiet, unexamined part of him, for an enemy he could simply name and oppose. What Azael had just handed him instead was something closer to an accounting — a debt owed by three beings he had only just begun to understand.

---

No one spoke for a long moment after that. Thaeriel's jaw had tightened, the exhaustion in his posture deepening into something closer to grief. Ereloth had gone entirely still, none of his usual restlessness left in him at all. Michael found himself holding the silence carefully, unwilling to be the one who broke it before either of the three actually responsible for this history had finished sitting with it.

Azael reached into the folds of his robe and drew out two broken fragments of pale wood, each one carrying a faint, steady light that pulsed like something closer to a heartbeat than a flame. He held them up, one in each hand, close enough together that Michael could see how precisely their broken edges had once fit.

"Aetheris," Azael said. "This was broken when the three of us first broke the world." He paused, and Michael understood, watching him, that the correction was deliberate — a claim laid plainly at the feet of Ereloth, Thaeriel, and Azael, and nowhere near Michael's own. "It can be remade. But balance cannot be created from itself. It never could. That is the entire nature of what I am, and what this staff was always meant to hold."

He turned the two fragments slightly, studying the light pulsing along their broken edges. "I need a tear of absolute, unconditional faith, drawn from the heart of Heaven itself. And I need a spark of pure, undiluted defiance, drawn from the heart of Hell." He looked up, meeting each of their eyes in turn. "Only then can this be remade as something whole enough to answer what we made together."

Michael studied the fragments in Azael's open palms, the faint pulse of their light steady and unhurried despite the weight of what they represented. He had expected, walking into this sanctuary, some grander instrument of war — a plan built from armies and strategy, the ordinary tools he'd spent centuries learning to wield. What Azael was actually describing sounded closer to a wound needing exactly the right kind of care than to any battle Michael knew how to plan for. He found the difference unsettling and, in some quiet way he hadn't expected, entirely fitting.

---

Thaeriel spoke first, his voice cold and certain in a way that left no room for argument. "A spark of defiance," he said. "Belial. He stole my spear. His pride is the loudest in all of Hell, and I already know exactly where to find it." He looked, for the first time since arriving, almost grimly satisfied. "I will retrieve my weapon, and I will take the spark from him in the same motion."

Michael watched something settle across Thaeriel's exhausted features as he spoke, a purpose sharpening back into focus after however many days of wandering Hell's colder circles had cost him. Whatever this quest had asked of him already, it seemed to be asking something clearer of him now, and Michael found he understood the relief of that clarity better than he might have a month ago.

Michael nodded, feeling the shape of his own part in this settle into place with a certainty that matched Thaeriel's own. "And I will return to the Celestial City," he said. "I will face the council and ask for their faith." He thought, briefly, of Uriel's temper and Gabriel's careful patience, of a council that would need more than his word alone to believe any of what he was about to bring them. "It won't be simple. But it's mine to carry."

He weighed the shape of that homecoming even as he spoke it aloud, considering exactly how much of this story the council would accept and how much would sound, to ears that hadn't stood in this sanctuary themselves, like the ravings of a fallen commander finally come fully undone. He would need more than his own testimony. He understood that plainly enough, and understood too, watching Azael's patient stillness across the small circle they'd formed, that he already had an idea of where the rest of that proof might come from.

Ereloth's grin returned at last, softer than its usual mischief but unmistakably his own. "Well, this should be fun," he said. "I'll go with Miki. He'll need someone to remind him to smile when they try to execute him." The joke landed with real warmth beneath it, and Michael found himself, despite everything, almost smiling back.

"They won't execute me," Michael said. "Probably."

"That's the spirit," Ereloth said, and clapped him once on the shoulder, the gesture carrying the same easy affection Michael had grown accustomed to across their days walking together. "Besides, someone competent needs to be there when Uriel starts shouting. I hear he's very loud."

Azael, silent through the exchange until now, spoke last. "I will go with you as well," he said, looking at Michael and Ereloth both. "A council asked to believe a story this strange will need more than words to trust it. My presence, silent as it is, will carry weight that argument alone cannot."

Michael felt something steady settle into his chest at that, a relief he hadn't expected to need until it arrived. He had walked into this sanctuary prepared to carry the council's judgment alone, the way he'd carried most difficult things across his long service, certain that asking for help was a debt he'd rather not owe anyone. Azael's offer changed the shape of that walk considerably, and he found himself grateful for it in a way that surprised him with its own plain simplicity.

---

Michael studied the three of them a long moment, the coming days finally settling into something he could hold steady in his own mind. Three paths, diverging from this one still sanctuary — Thaeriel walking back into Hell to finish what he'd already started, Ereloth and Azael walking with him toward a council that would test every ounce of faith he still carried, and somewhere beyond all three paths, an old wound waiting to see whether they managed to gather what it would take to answer it.

None of the paths ahead promised anything simple. He understood that plainly, standing in a sanctuary built entirely outside the ordinary reach of time, and found the understanding steadied him rather than shook him. He had spent long enough learning what real cost actually felt like to stop expecting anything worth doing to arrive cheaply.

"Then we know what comes next," Michael said, and found the words steadying him as he said them, plain and simple in exactly the way this moment actually needed. It was not a grand plan, not the kind he'd have once expected a moment this consequential to demand of him. It was simply three tasks, each one belonging to the hands best suited to carry it, and he found that plainness itself more reassuring than any grander scheme could have managed.

Thaeriel rose without ceremony, already turning toward the sanctuary's edge, his stride carrying the same unhesitating certainty Michael had watched settle over him the moment he'd named Belial as his target. He paused once, looking back at the small circle he was about to leave, something almost gentle crossing his flat exhaustion for just a moment. "Bring me something worth the trip when you find me again," he said, and then he was walking, the sanctuary's edge already opening to let him through toward whatever waited for him in Dis.

Ereloth caught Michael's eye, something warmer than his usual performance sitting openly in it now, and Azael simply watched all three of them with the same patient stillness he'd carried since the valley, two broken fragments of pale wood held carefully in his lap, waiting for the pieces that would finally make them whole again.
