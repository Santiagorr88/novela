# B1C13 – The Price of Truth

## The Ambush

The battle broke exactly where Belial had built it to break, Aamon's legion crashing into the angelic patrol with the same reckless hunger for glory that had carried Aamon into this trap in the first place. Celestial light and demonic fire tangled together across the crossing in a chaos convincing enough to hold every eye on the front of it, exactly as Belial had needed it to.

Blades met in a rising din of steel and screaming, the ground already churned to mud beneath a hundred boots on either side. A wounded angel went down near Belial's flank, wings scattering light across the dirt as he fell, and was gone beneath the crush of the advancing line before Belial had to spare him a second glance, one more body among the many this particular deception would cost before the night was through. Demons pressed forward in close ranks, Aamon's banner still flying somewhere near the center of the press, every soldier on both sides fully convinced this was the whole of what tonight demanded of them.

Fire from the demon lines lit the crossing in fitful orange bursts, answered each time by a fresh flare of celestial light from the angelic side, the two colors chasing each other across the battlefield without either ever quite winning ground for long. Screams rose and fell in no particular pattern, swallowed as quickly as they came by the general roar of the fight, and somewhere off to Belial's right an angelic banner-bearer went down hard, his standard toppling into the mud beneath the press of bodies that followed him. Two demon soldiers grappled a wounded angel to the ground a few strides further on, blades rising and falling in quick succession before either side could be called for it, and were gone again into the press before the angel's body had even stopped moving. The mud underfoot grew slicker with every passing minute, churned by boots and blood into something that dragged at every step, sucking at heels and slowing whole formations without either side sparing it a thought. None of it was real, not in the way that mattered. It was theater built to occupy every eye that might otherwise have wandered toward the ground Belial actually needed them not to look at.

Aamon fought at the center of that chaos with the disciplined ferocity his reputation had always promised, Skarth tearing through the angelic line with the kind of brutal precision that had built his name across centuries of campaigns. He carved a path forward with each swing, wrenching the serrated blade free of one body only to bury it in the next, roaring something wordless and triumphant that carried over even the din around him. Not once did he glance down at the ground he stood on, or sideways at the commander riding beside him — nothing about him, not his stance nor the set of his shoulders, suggested he saw anything beyond the battle directly in front of him. Belial kept close enough to watch without appearing to watch, Ruach heavy and ready at his hip, matching Aamon's pace without ever quite drawing even with it, counting down the last moments of a plan that had already stopped belonging to anyone but him.

## The Order That Comes Too Late

The angelic captain leading the patrol caught something in the shape of the demonic formation that read wrong to his trained eye, a discipline too precise for an ambush thrown together on short notice, and shouted a command down his own line before the thought had fully finished forming — regroup, close the right flank before it stretched too thin to hold.

His voice cut clean through the noise around him, sharp with the kind of urgency that soldiers obeyed on instinct before their minds had even finished parsing the words. Nearby angels broke off from their engagements at once, wings snapping wide as they wheeled toward the flank he had named, blades already lowering into a defensive line that might, given another few seconds, have actually held. He had led enough patrols to trust the feeling that had prompted the order, a wrongness in the demons' formation that no ordinary ambush should have carried, and he allowed himself one brief, grim satisfaction at seeing his line answer him so cleanly. His soldiers began the maneuver at once, the line bending toward the shape he had called for, a genuine chance to blunt whatever was actually coming. It never had the chance to matter. A small, silent gesture passed near the enemy commander's position, lost entirely in the battle's noise, and everything the captain had just ordered became irrelevant before his own soldiers could finish the turn.

## The Turn

Belial's own honor guard broke from the angelic line the moment his signal reached them, wheeling as one body away from the angels they had been fighting and directly into Aamon's personal guard instead — soldiers he had chosen for this precise role weeks earlier, drilled privately in a maneuver none of them had been told the true purpose of until tonight.

The reversal took less than a heartbeat to land and considerably longer for anyone else on the field to understand. For one suspended moment the entire battle seemed to hang on the same confused breath, blades frozen mid-swing as both sides tried to make sense of an enemy that had just, impossibly, multiplied. Angels who had braced for demons found demons suddenly cutting into other demons beside them, and the angelic line, trained for a single kind of enemy and confronted now with two of them tearing at each other, wavered and broke apart into confusion. A few angels pressed the sudden advantage without pausing, cutting hard into the exposed flank the chaos had opened; others simply stood frozen for a moment too long, blades lowered, eyes darting between two sets of demons now tearing into each other in front of them. Aamon's own guard, caught entirely unprepared by the turn, fell in moments beneath blades that had been fighting alongside them only seconds before — a captain here, cut down mid-shout before he could even name what was happening; two soldiers there, backs still turned toward the angels they had been holding off when their former allies' blades found them instead. One of the honor guard's own lieutenants pressed the advantage hard, driving three of Aamon's men back against a crumbling ridge of stone until none of them had anywhere left to retreat, cutting them down one after another before any could break free of the rock at their backs. Belial moved through the widening gap they left behind with the unhurried certainty of a man walking a path he had already memorized, stepping over the fallen without slowing, Ruach still clean at his hip because none of this violence had required him to lift it himself.

## The Ritual

He found Aamon staggering at the center of the collapsing battle, already wounded, blood streaking down one arm from a wound his own guard's blades had opened, his eyes darting across the ruin of his own line as though the shape of it made no sense to him yet. Belial drove him back, step by step, Ruach turning aside Skarth in a series of hard, deliberate blocks, herding him without appearing to herd him toward a stretch of ground marked with a sigil no ordinary soldier would have recognized as anything but scorched earth. He had chosen this precise ground weeks ago, walked it alone at night to be certain of its exact position relative to every likely angle of retreat, and every strike he traded with Aamon now only pushed the wounded commander closer to a spot Belial had already fixed with absolute precision in his own mind.

Aamon fought back with everything his wound still allowed him, Skarth swinging in wide, furious arcs that Belial turned aside one after another, giving ground exactly as much as he needed to and not a step more. Each parry rang out sharp over the dying noise of the wider battle behind them, the two of them now isolated in a pocket of relative quiet that neither the angels nor the remaining demons seemed inclined to cross into. Aamon's eyes found the sigil beneath his own feet and widened, his stance faltering for the first time since the fight had begun. "What is this," he demanded, his voice cracking somewhere between the question and the shout he seemed to want it to be, Skarth still raised though his grip on it had visibly loosened. Belial began the invocation without waiting for him to finish understanding, words drawn from a text forbidden to any demon below Commander rank, each syllable landing with a weight that made the air around them thicken. The ground beneath Aamon's feet answered first, a sickly purple light rising up through the sigil's carved lines to wrap around his legs like something with intent behind it. He tried to step clear of it and found his boots would no longer answer him, rooted fast to ground that had held steady under him for centuries and chose, tonight of all nights, not to. "For power, Aamon," Belial said, low enough that only the two of them would ever hear it. "The price must always be paid."

## A Commander's End

Aamon lunged at him anyway, wounded and roaring, Skarth carving one last desperate arc toward Belial's throat with a strength that startled Belial despite everything he already knew about the man he was killing. Belial ducked under the swing, close enough to feel the wind of it pass over his own shoulder, and answered with a single, decisive blow, Ruach's head connecting with enough force to fold Aamon's knees beneath him and drop him hard onto the glowing carved lines.

The seal cracked open beneath them with a sound like a mourner's cry, and Aamon's essence, that vast demonic weight Belial had calculated so carefully back in the loyalty rolls, began to pour out of him and into the widening crack, thin streams of dark light unspooling from his chest and vanishing into the sigil's depths, faster with every passing second as though the crack itself had grown hungrier for what remained. Ruach's spiked head drove into the center of Aamon's black heart a moment later, and no blood answered the strike — only a torrent of raw demonic power, ripped loose and drawn down into the sigil's hungry light, the ground itself seeming to drink it in one final, greedy pull. Aamon's body went still beneath him within seconds, the fury finally gone out of it entirely, his eyes fixed open on nothing at all. Belial straightened over him and let out a long breath he had not noticed himself holding, Ruach's head still smoking faintly where it had struck.

## The Way Is Open

Belial rose from the body slowly, blood-streaked and breathing hard, and found the portal to the Weeping Sepulcher already glowing where the sigil had stood a moment before, patient and waiting in a way nothing else on this battlefield had been. Its light was cold, threaded through with the same sickly purple that had climbed Aamon's legs only moments earlier, and it pulsed once, slow and deliberate, as though acknowledging that the price it demanded had finally been paid in full. A faint draft moved out from its edge, carrying with it a smell like old stone and older grief, cool against skin still slick with battle-sweat.

Around him, the surviving angels were already pulling back from a fight that no longer resembled anything they had marched out expecting, their retreat carrying the particular haste of soldiers who had just understood, far too late to act on it, that something far worse than an ambush had been unfolding beneath their feet the whole time. The captain who had shouted his useless order minutes earlier was among them, dragging a wounded soldier back toward the rear line, his eyes finding Belial across the churned battlefield for one long moment before he turned and kept running, apparently unwilling to risk whatever answer a closer look might have given him. What remained of Aamon's own legion stood scattered and leaderless across the field, some still fighting out of habit, others already beginning to understand that the commander who had led them here was never coming back to lead them out. A few of them had already begun drifting toward the rear in loose, uncertain knots, no one among them senior enough to call an organized retreat, let alone explain to whoever asked later exactly what had happened to their commander.

Somewhere behind him, the last scattered pockets of fighting were already dying out on their own, neither side finding much reason to keep swinging at an enemy that had stopped pressing forward. Belial paid their withdrawal no further attention. He stood over Aamon's body for one last moment, breathing hard, victorious, dark power still fading from Ruach's head and blood cooling on his own hands, the churned ground around them already settling into an uneasy quiet, then turned from it without another glance. He crossed the last few paces of the battlefield without looking back at what he was leaving behind, Ruach still in hand, and stood at last before the threshold he had spent so long building toward, looking into the waiting portal without crossing it. The search that had brought him this far was, at last, within reach.
