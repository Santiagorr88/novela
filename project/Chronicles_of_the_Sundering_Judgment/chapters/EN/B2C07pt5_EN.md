# B2C07.5 – What Even He Does Not Know

Lucifer did not rule by decree, whatever the newer legions believed of him. Decrees were for princes who needed to be seen ruling, who required the theater of command the way a weak flame required a windbreak — something between themselves and the possibility of going out unnoticed. He ruled by attention instead. By the simple, unbroken habit of watching everything worth watching, closely enough and often enough that intervention rarely became necessary at all. A throne that had to remind its subjects who sat on it had already lost something no decree could win back, and he had watched enough thrones fall in exactly that manner — reminding, reminding, reminding, until the reminding itself became the whole of the reign — to have built his own on the opposite principle from the first day he'd claimed it.

The surface hung before him in his private hall, a sheet of smoke drawn taut between two black iron posts, showing him fragments of his domain one after another, unhurried, the way he had watched them every day for longer than the hall itself had stood. A border dispute cooling before it could catch. A captain's loyalty fraying at an edge too fine for anyone but Lucifer to have noticed it fraying at all. A minor court three tiers below his own, quietly renegotiating debts it thought no one above it cared to track. He did none of this for pleasure, exactly, though something in it pleased him — the particular, sustained satisfaction of a mind that had made itself large enough to hold an entire world's worth of small dishonesties at once, and never once dropped one. He did it because he had learned, across an age long enough to make the lesson permanent rather than merely instructive, that the only rulers who were ever truly surprised were the ones who had stopped paying attention first. Surprise, to Lucifer, was not misfortune. It was evidence of neglect, and he permitted himself very little of either.

His gaze, drifting without particular purpose across a dozen minor scenes, caught and held on one that was not minor at all.

Belial sat alone in a private chamber, a spear resting across his knees with the particular stillness of a man keeping careful company with something he did not entirely trust. Lucifer watched the tremor pass through the demon's shoulders each time the weapon's grief pulsed against him, watched him absorb it without complaint, without so much as a flinch he would have permitted anyone else to see. It was, Lucifer thought, a fine performance — the kind Belial had always given, even as a much younger and much hungrier thing clawing its way up through the ranks of Hell's endless minor courts, the kind that had first made Lucifer notice him at all. He recognized the discipline of it. He had worn a version of that same discipline himself, once, in ages Belial had never lived to see.

It was not curiosity, at first, that held Lucifer's attention there. It was something closer to the particular, private satisfaction of a sovereign watching an ambitious subordinate go on paying, week after week, for a prize no one had thought — or troubled — to warn him would cost this much to keep. Lucifer had long ago decided that Belial wanted that spear the way young and hungry things always wanted the thing just beyond their reach, certain that possession alone would settle every account. Lucifer had lived long enough to know that possession never settled anything. It only opened a new ledger, with different debts written into it, and he had watched enough of his own ambitious subordinates learn that lesson to find some quiet use in watching Belial learn it too.

The satisfaction did not last long enough to become comfortable.

---

An attendant entered on the soft, practiced feet of someone who had learned exactly how to approach Lucifer without disturbing whatever he was in the middle of noticing. She waited to be acknowledged before she spoke, a scroll of routine reports held loosely in one hand, mostly unread.

"My lord. Lord Belial's spear continues to show signs of instability. The reports use the word repeatedly enough that I thought it worth bringing to you directly rather than filing it beneath the rest. Shall we prepare a formal inquiry?"

Lucifer did not look away from the smoke. "And why would I do that."

"Forgive me — I assumed, given the reports, that you might wish to intervene."

"Intervene in what, precisely." He let the question sit a moment before he finished it, watching her hesitate rather than answer too quickly. "Why would I intervene in something I cannot even name?" His voice carried none of the heat the words might have suggested on paper. It carried instead the flat and faintly unsettling calm of a man stating a fact he found genuinely, quietly troubling — the calm of someone choosing precision over alarm not because alarm was beneath him, but because he had not yet decided whether alarm was warranted, and would not perform an emotion he had not yet earned the right to feel.

The attendant said nothing further. She bowed and withdrew to the edge of the hall, close enough to be summoned again, far enough to leave the silence that followed undisturbed.

---

Lucifer turned his attention inward then, in the particular way he rarely allowed himself to do in front of witnesses — reaching not into the smoke, but into the long, cluttered archive of everything he had ever learned in an existence that predated most of what either Heaven or Hell now called history. He searched for the spear the way he searched for anything: methodically, exhaustively, certain the answer existed somewhere in the accumulated weight of what he knew, because very little, in his experience, did not. He had catalogued the First Fall itself down to details even his own chroniclers had long since forgotten to record. He had catalogued every treaty broken and every treaty kept, every weapon Hell had ever forged in anger and every relic it had ever stripped from Heaven's own dead in the long war between them. Somewhere in that catalogue, he was certain, a spear old enough to grieve the way this one grieved would have left some trace of its making.

He found nothing. Not a fragment. Not a rumor dressed as fact, not even one of the half-truths his own court traded in so freely that he had long since learned to extract real information from the specific shape of their lying. He remembered the forging of every blade that had ever mattered to the war between Heaven and Hell — remembered Uriel's flaming sword the day it was first drawn, remembered the precise ceremony that had bound three of Hell's own relics to their first bearers, remembered enough about weapons in general to know, at a glance, roughly what a thing like this ought to be. The spear belonged to none of it. It carried no infernal signature he recognized, no divine signature either, nothing that matched the pattern of anything he had ever catalogued in an age spent cataloguing absolutely everything within his reach.

It matched nothing he had. Not one entry, not one half-remembered whisper from before the Fall, in a lifetime spent making certain there was almost nothing left that could say the same. He did not permit the unease that thought carried onto his face.

---

He did not let his composure break. Composure was not, for Lucifer, a performance he put on for others — it was simply how he existed, the way he had existed since long before there was a court left to watch him prove it. But for the length of several long breaths, he simply stopped. Stopped speaking. Stopped moving. Stopped, for what felt like the first time in longer than he cared to measure, pretending even to himself that his attention was on anything other than the fact of not knowing.

He had gone without answers before, on matters far larger than one demon's borrowed weapon. But those absences had always come from a question he had simply not yet gotten around to asking, or one whose answer sat somewhere he had not yet troubled himself to look. This was neither. This was a question he had asked, properly and exhaustively, of the single most complete archive of knowledge he possessed, and the archive had returned nothing at all. That had never happened to him before.

The attendant, still waiting at the edge of the hall, did not move and did not speak. Her eyes stayed down, fixed on the scroll she had never unrolled, and she offered nothing to fill the quiet Lucifer had left standing in the room.

---

"Send for someone discreet," Lucifer said finally, and his voice had recovered every degree of its usual, unbothered calm, as though the silence before it had simply been a pause between two ordinary sentences. "Not one of Belial's people. Someone of my own — someone who owes their position to no favor but mine, and who understands exactly what it costs to disappoint me. I want the origin of that spear traced. Quietly. Thoroughly. And I want it done in a manner that never once reaches Belial's ear."

"May I ask why the discretion, my lord?"

"Because a king who needs answers looks weak," Lucifer said, turning back to the smoke, already letting his attention drift once more across the dozen small dramas of his domain as though nothing at all had interrupted it. "A king who already has them looks inevitable. I intend to remain the second kind for a great deal longer than any of this will take to resolve — and I have no interest in Belial learning, from my own asking, that there is anything left in his little corner of the world that I do not already understand."

"And if the investigation finds nothing at all, my lord?"

Lucifer considered the question longer than its size seemed to warrant, his eyes fixed once more on the small, grieving figure of Belial in the smoke, the spear still resting across his knees. "Then I will have learned something else entirely," he said at last. "Not what the spear is. What it means that something can exist within my own domain, wielded by my own most ambitious servant, that I have never once, in all my long and careful watching, thought to look for."

The attendant bowed and left to carry out the order. Lucifer remained a while longer before the smoke, alone with a question he had not asked aloud even once, watching the small tremor pass again and again through Belial's shoulders. He did not envy him the wound. He only noted, with a discomfort he kept as carefully hidden as everything else he had ever felt, that Belial at least knew the shape of what was costing him. Lucifer did not, and had never once, in all his long watching, had cause to sit with that particular absence before tonight.

He let the smoke drift on, past Belial's chamber, back toward the border dispute and the fraying captain and the minor court still quietly renegotiating its debts, and made himself watch each of them with the same unhurried attention he gave everything else in his domain. It was, he found, harder than usual to keep his gaze from drifting back. He let it drift back once more before he made himself stop, and did not let it happen a second time that night.

---

The investigation into the spear's origin returned nothing worth interrupting him over for weeks, and Lucifer let it run at its own patient pace, the way he let most things run once he had set them properly in motion. It was a different report entirely that reached him on an evening some weeks later, carried not through the discreet channel his own investigator used but through the ordinary traffic of court gossip his attendants brought him each night alongside the routine business of his domain — the kind of thing nobody thought worth a formal audience, only a mention in passing, because on its surface it read as nothing more than fashion.

"Three of the lesser courts have petitioned Lord Belial's people this week for a formal introduction, my lord — they want access to what he's been refining and selling directly," the attendant said, waiting until Lucifer's attention had properly settled on her before continuing, the scroll in her hands carrying nothing more urgent tonight than the week's ordinary correspondence. "Crystallized soul-essence, they're calling it. A taste of primordial power, refined and sold as though it were wine. The princelings are apparently paying obscene sums for the privilege of feeling, for an hour, something close to what Lord Belial now carries permanently."

Lucifer laughed — a short, genuine sound, the first real amusement he had allowed himself in the hall that week. "Of course he has," he said, real fondness buried somewhere in the words, the fondness of a man watching an old and familiar vice perform exactly as advertised. "Pride sold as perfume. I would have been disappointed in him if he'd found some more original use for his own suffering."

"Shall I have the petitions logged, my lord? For the record, if nothing else."

"Log them." He said it lightly, already half-returning his attention to the smoke, to the border dispute still cooling on its own schedule two tiers below him. But something in the shape of the report stayed with him a moment longer than its size warranted, the way a single wrong note stays with a musician long after the rest of a piece has resolved cleanly around it. Three lesser lords, unprompted, seeking Belial directly rather than petitioning through Lucifer's own court for the introduction, as custom required of anything valuable enough to be worth petitioning for at all.

It was a small thing. He turned it over anyway, with the same unhurried thoroughness he gave everything worth turning over. A vassal who could make lesser lords come to him, seeking a favor Lucifer himself had never authorized and had not yet been asked to bless, was a vassal accumulating a currency that ran through none of Lucifer's own ledgers — and a currency that ran outside a king's ledgers had a way, in his long experience, of eventually buying something a king would have preferred to keep unsold.

He did not summon Belial. He did not so much as mention the petitions when the demon's name came up twice more that same week, in reports concerning matters considerably more urgent than a fashionable new luxury. There was time yet to see what Belial did with a currency he did not yet seem to know he was spending, and Lucifer had long since learned that the surest way to understand what an ambitious man intended was to watch, patiently, what he built while he still believed no one above him was counting.

He did nothing. Not yet.
