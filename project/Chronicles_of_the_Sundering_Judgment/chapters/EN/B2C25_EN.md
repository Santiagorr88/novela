# B2C25 – An Unseen Interrogation

The steam tunnels ran beneath the campus in a maze older than any current student's understanding of the university, forgotten service corridors that still carried heat and old pipework past buildings whose administrators had long since stopped remembering the tunnels existed at all. The air down here sat thick and close, warm enough in places to fog his breath against the cold pipe casings, and Arin moved through it with the same patient, silent economy he'd carried through every hunt since the Grey City, letting the tunnel's own darkness do half the work of hiding him.

He had traced the disguised demon's patrol down into these corridors two nights ago, patient across another cycle of watching before he'd finally chosen tonight to close the distance for good. The creature had grown careless in its confidence, exactly as he'd expected it would — a scout accustomed to going unnoticed rarely troubled itself to notice how thoroughly it was being watched in return.

He'd spent the intervening night mapping the tunnel's own geography as carefully as he'd mapped the creature's patrol above ground, tracing every branch and dead end until he understood the space better than the thing that actually worked it. There was only one stretch narrow enough to force a confrontation without giving the demon room to circle around him, one blind corner it had to pass through twice on every loop, and Arin had positioned himself there specifically, patient enough to let two full circuits pass before he finally stepped out to meet the third. He cornered it in that narrow stretch between two rusted valve housings, close enough now that the creature's illusion had nowhere left to hide behind, its borrowed guard's uniform flickering at the edges as it finally registered exactly what stood between it and the tunnel's only easy exit.

The demon bolted the instant it understood it had been made, breaking for a maintenance grate set low in the tunnel wall — the same route Arin had watched it use twice already on its ordinary patrol, a shaft that dropped into the older drainage system beneath the quad and, from there, into open ground half a mile from anywhere he could easily follow. It moved faster than its clumsy patrol had suggested it could, a burst of genuine desperation carrying it three steps ahead before Arin closed the gap. He caught it by the ankle at the very last instant, both of them going down hard against the old concrete, and hauled it back bodily into the tunnel's center while it clawed uselessly at the grate's edge, dragging one long scrape of torn metal across the stone as its grip finally failed.

It fought him the rest of the way, wild and undisciplined, throwing an elbow that caught Arin across the jaw hard enough to leave his ears ringing before he pinned the arm properly and drove the fight out of it with three short, economical blows that cost the creature considerably more than they cost him. There was nothing elegant in the struggle. It was the ordinary, graceless work of two bodies fighting for control of a narrow, unforgiving space, and Arin came out of it with a torn sleeve and a scraped knuckle for his trouble, breathing hard for the first time in longer than this particular hunt had asked of him.

He'd disarmed contracts messier than this one in his old life, back when a fight like this would have ended the moment he decided it should, no more thought given to the loser's fate than a man gives to a door he's already closed behind him. This one refused to end as cleanly. The demon kept twisting even after the last blow landed, some final reserve of desperation driving it to try the grate a second time, dragging Arin half a step forward before he wrestled the arm back down and pinned it flat against the concrete for good. He held it there a long moment, letting the creature feel exactly how little fight it had left in it, before he risked easing his weight enough to let it breathe properly. It didn't try again after that. Something in it seemed to understand, all at once, that the contest for the grate was already over and had been for a while.

---

The last of the physical fight went out of it once Arin had its arm locked and its weight fully pinned, and he reached past the borrowed guard's uniform to close his free hand around the thing actually wearing it — not skin or cloth, but the raw, disorganized essence flickering beneath the disguise, gathered loose in his grip the same way he'd learned to gather every demon's essence since the granary. He had it pinned within seconds after that, one knee against its chest, the threat of unmaking it plain without needing further demonstration. The struggle went out of the creature entirely the moment it felt his grip close around what it actually was rather than the shape it wore. It stopped pulling. Its limbs went slack against the concrete, and it made no further sound beyond the ragged pull of its own breathing.

"Please." The demon's voice came out thin, already broken past whatever posturing it might have managed under better circumstances. "I'm nothing. A scout. I don't decide anything, I just watch and report—"

"Then report to me," Arin said. His voice carried none of the heat the moment might have earned, only a flat, professional patience that seemed to unsettle the creature more thoroughly than shouting would have. "What are you watching for."

"I don't— I only relay what I see. I'm not told the reasons behind any of it." The demon's eyes flicked toward the grip Arin still held loosely around its essence, as though it half expected the threat to tighten with every wrong answer. "Please, I'll tell you whatever I actually know. Just don't—"

"I haven't decided to do anything to you yet," Arin said. "That decision depends entirely on how useful the next few minutes turn out to be. Start with what you were sent here to find, and we'll see how the rest of this goes."

The demon's eyes searched his face for some sign of the mercy it seemed to be hoping to bargain toward, found nothing there to hold onto, and gave up the search almost as quickly as it had started.

---

It came apart quickly after that, the particular fast unraveling of something that had never once been trained to withstand real pressure. "Two signatures," it said, the words tumbling out faster than its own fear seemed able to properly organize them. "Belial found them both weeks ago — one sleeping, one loud enough to wake half of Hell on its own. He's known for a while now that they're both converging on this same ground, this same nexus."

"Converging when."

"Soon. I don't know exactly. Days, maybe less." The demon's eyes darted once toward the tunnel's dark, receding length, as though some part of it still hadn't given up on the idea of running. "He's not sending scouts to watch anymore. He's building toward a strike, timed for whenever both of them finally arrive here together."

"Timed how. Who told you that, and how do you know it's not just a rumor passed down from someone above you making the same guess you're making now."

"I carried the actual orders myself, three days ago, to the captain who's coordinating it." The demon's voice cracked on the word *captain*, and it glanced once toward the tunnel's dark reaches, as though the chain of command it answered to might be listening even here. "He wants them taken at the portal, both at once, before either one has a chance to reach it on their own terms."

"Which portal. Be specific."

"I don't know its name. I only know the crossing point they mean — the old one, the one that's been quiet for years. I was told it's waking back up, and that whatever's waking it is the same thing drawing both signals in." The demon shifted its weight, some instinct in it apparently deciding that offering more, unprompted, might buy it something it hadn't yet dared hope for. "There's a third piece too, if it helps you. Something about the ground itself remembering. I didn't understand that part. I only carried the words, I didn't ask what they meant."

Arin held the information a long moment, letting the full shape of it settle. Belial did not simply suspect the two signals were connected. He had confirmed it, weeks ago, and had been quietly assembling a trap this entire time while Arin himself had still been treating the two threads as separate, unrelated hunts. Every hour he'd spent tracking Navarion's records as though it were a hunt apart from whatever the second signal was doing elsewhere had been an hour spent working half-blind, missing the connection Belial had already acted on.

---

"Does he know what they actually are?" Arin asked. "Not what they're worth to him. What they are."

The demon's trembling didn't ease, but something in its expression shifted, the particular confusion of a creature reaching for an answer it had never once possessed. "He never told us. Only that there were two, and that we should be afraid of both of them equally." It searched Arin's face, some desperate hope flickering that the right answer might buy it something. "Not even he fully knows, does he?"

Arin didn't answer that. There was nothing he could have said that would have cost him less than the silence did.

---

Three years ago, this would not have been a decision at all. A low-ranked demon caught spying on a target worth this much would have been a loose end, closed cleanly and without a second thought, the same tidy arithmetic that had governed every job he'd ever taken before the granary.

It struck him now, plainly, without his having reached for it: killing this creature would not solve anything. Not the two signals converging, not Belial's trap, not the handful of days he might have left before both arrived at whatever crossing point was waking beneath them. Its death would settle nothing that letting it walk away didn't settle just as completely, and the realization arrived whole, uninvited, with none of the careful weighing he'd have once brought to a question like this.

He released the demon's essence slowly, watching it curl in on itself the moment the pressure lifted, still and unmoving for a breath before it seemed to grasp that nothing more was coming.

"Go," Arin said, and nothing else.

---

The demon fled without a backward glance, scrambling back through the same grate it had failed to reach the first time, and Arin remained alone in the tunnel a long while afterward, listening to its footsteps fade into the maze of old pipework until even the echo of them was gone. He sat back against the cold stone, catching his breath properly for the first time since the scuffle had ended, and let the whole shape of what he'd just learned settle into place around him.

He thought of the man he'd been three years ago, the one who would have ended this without a second thought, who had built an entire career on the clean, uncomplicated satisfaction of knowing exactly which lives were worth ending and which weren't. That man would have called tonight's choice weakness, would have argued that a spared enemy was simply a debt deferred rather than a debt cancelled. The argument no longer landed the way it once had. Arin sat with that plainly, without trying to talk himself back into believing it.

He did not fully recognize that man anymore, sitting here in the tunnel's cooling quiet. He wasn't certain whether that was a loss or the first honest gain this whole terrible month had actually given him. He only knew that Belial was coming for both signals at once, that time had grown considerably shorter than he'd let himself believe, and that whatever waited for him at the end of this hunt, he intended to meet it as exactly the man he'd become rather than the one he used to be.
