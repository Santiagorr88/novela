# B3C40 – The Echo of Pride

Belial sat alone on his throne in the great hall of his fortress in Dis, and found the silence around him considerably louder than any battle he'd ever presided over from this exact seat. Dust had begun collecting along the hall's lower steps, unswept, and he noted the detail without any particular urge to correct it, the way a man might note a small crack in a wall he no longer expected to live in much longer.

The legions that had once filled this hall with the constant, satisfying noise of soldiers awaiting his command were gone, scattered across the chaotic new landscape of Hell in search of leaders who still had something worth commanding them toward. He had watched them go, one detachment at a time, over the weeks since Thaeriel had walked out of this exact plaza carrying Lament and the last of Belial's own unpolished defiance sealed inside a crystal. He had not stopped a single one of them from leaving. He had understood, watching the first captains slip away, that he no longer had anything left worth the staying for.

He remembered, distantly, the exact weight of command he'd once carried through these same corridors — the certainty in every officer's salute, the particular pleasure of watching an entire legion move at his own word alone, thousands of soldiers answering to nothing but a single raised hand. He found the memory strange now, as though it belonged to some other version of himself he could recall clearly enough but no longer actually recognize.

He studied the empty hall, the cold stone throne beneath him, the banners still hanging from the vaulted ceiling with no one left to salute them, their once-vivid colors already dulled by weeks of untended dust. He had ruled from this exact seat for centuries, had built an entire identity around the particular, satisfying weight of command. He found himself, sitting here now in the wreckage of it, unable to summon even the energy for proper self-pity.

The throne itself felt different beneath him than it once had, though nothing about its actual shape had changed. He had always sat here as though the seat belonged to him by right, some earned extension of his own ambition. He found himself now simply occupying it, the way a man occupies a chair in an empty room he has no particular reason to leave.

---

A young demon lord burst into the throne room some days later, armored in fresh obsidian plate and carrying himself with the particular hunger of someone who had spent his entire ambitious existence waiting for exactly this kind of opportunity. Belial recognized the type immediately, having been it himself once, longer ago than he currently cared to measure, the same hungry certainty that this exact moment would prove the turning point of an entire glorious career still waiting to unfold.

"I claim this territory," the young lord announced, his voice carrying easily through the empty hall, clearly rehearsed for an audience considerably larger than the one actually present to hear it. "Your fortress, your holdings, everything that remains of what you once ruled here. Step aside, or I will take it from you by force." He struck the floor once with the flat of a wicked-looking blade, the gesture theatrical enough that Belial found himself, distantly, almost admiring the performance of it — the same performance he himself had once given a hundred times, to considerably larger and considerably more attentive halls.

Belial did not look at him. He studied the empty hall instead, the cold stone and the abandoned banners, and found the challenge landing with no weight at all, none of the fury or defensiveness he suspected the young lord had actually prepared himself to answer, however carefully rehearsed those opening lines had plainly sounded.

"The territory is yours," Belial said. "There is nothing left to rule."

The young lord hesitated, plainly thrown by an answer that offered him no fight worth the winning. He studied Belial a moment longer, searching, Belial suspected, for whatever trick or trap the too-easy surrender might actually be hiding. He found none, because none existed. He left eventually, taking the throne room's nominal ownership with him, and Belial felt nothing at all watching him go, not even the satisfaction of having denied the boy his desired confrontation.

He thought, watching the young lord's retreating back, of every rival he'd once crushed for daring to challenge him this same way, back when he'd still had the will to consider the challenge itself an insult worth answering rather than simply a formality now well beneath his notice. He felt no echo of that old fury now, only a distant, almost academic recognition that the boy would learn, eventually, exactly how little this particular prize was actually worth ruling.

---

Weeks passed, and Belial became something considerably less than a ruler in his own former fortress — a solitary figure wandering halls that had once carried the constant sound of his own command, talking to himself in the absence of anyone else left to talk to. He found himself, more often than he cared to admit, reliving the duel with Thaeriel in obsessive, exacting detail, replaying every exchange, every moment where a different choice might have changed the shape of what came after.

He had built his entire existence around performance, he understood now, walking these empty halls — ambition worn as armor over something rawer and more honest that he'd spent centuries keeping carefully hidden even from himself. Thaeriel had reached past all of it in a single duel, had taken the one part of him that had never actually needed conquering. Belial found himself left standing here now with nothing left to perform, and no one left watching even if he'd still had the will for it.

He caught himself, more than once, addressing the empty corridor as though Thaeriel still stood somewhere within it, rehearsing arguments and counterarguments to a duel that had already finished settling itself weeks ago. He recognized the behavior as something close to madness, and found he could no longer summon the energy to actually care whether it was. He wondered, on one particularly long and empty afternoon, whether Thaeriel ever thought of him at all anymore, and suspected, honestly, that the answer was no.

Other demons ruled his former lands now, minor lords who had carved up his territory among themselves with the casual efficiency of scavengers picking clean something already understood to be finished. None of them bothered to kill him. He understood, passing the occasional guard who no longer answered to him, that he had become too irrelevant even for that particular mercy. A death would have required someone to care enough about him to deliver it.

He passed one of the new lord's soldiers in the corridor one afternoon, a demon he half recognized from some prior campaign fought under his own banner, and found the soldier's gaze sliding past him entirely, the way a passerby's gaze slides past an old, unremarkable statue no longer worth a second look. He had spent his entire long existence commanding attention from every room he entered, certain that whatever else changed around him, that particular fact of his own presence never would. He found the absence of it, now, considerably harder to sit with than any wound Thaeriel's duel had actually left on him.

He thought, wandering the same corridor he'd walked a hundred times this same week alone, of every account he'd have once given of his own inevitable end — a glorious last stand, a battle worthy of the songs that would follow it, some final display of the ambition that had defined him. He had rehearsed that imagined death more times across his long existence than he currently cared to count, had taken a certain private comfort in knowing exactly how the story was supposed to end. None of those imagined endings had prepared him for this instead — an ordinary, quiet fading, the kind no one would ever think worth writing a single verse about.

---

Belial felt it happen from the inside, alone in the throne room in the deep hours of one ordinary night, no different on the surface from any of the countless other nights he'd spent wandering these same silent halls. The will that had once held his demonic form together simply grew too thin and too tired to keep doing the work required of it, a slow, unremarkable failing rather than any single violent break. He felt no pain in it. He felt, if anything, something closer to relief, the particular ease of finally setting down a weight he'd been carrying for considerably longer than he'd ever let himself admit.

There was no final vision of the hall around him, no last defiant thought worth the naming. There was simply the plain, ordinary collapse of a form that no longer had anything left in it willing to keep standing, and then, gradually, something else in its place. He had half expected, across whatever fragments of himself had still been capable of expecting anything by that point, that the moment would arrive as some final punishment, a violent reckoning for centuries of accumulated debt. It arrived instead unremarkable and quiet, entirely his own to experience however he actually chose to.

His soul, freed from that spent shape, fell back into the same wide cycle of reincarnation that had once dragged every soul back into its turning without asking permission. He had spent his entire long existence certain he understood exactly what waited for a demon soul once its current form finally gave out — some fixed, punishing destination, the same grim cycle he'd watched claim lesser souls a thousand times across his long reign, never once questioning whether the cycle actually worked the way every account of it had always claimed. He found himself, drifting now through an unfamiliar stillness, unable to locate any trace of that old, familiar pull at all.

He understood, feeling for whatever mechanism should have been dragging him onward by now and finding only patient, waiting quiet instead, that something considerably larger than his own fortress had changed while he'd spent these last weeks haunting its empty halls. He had no way of knowing what, or how, or who had actually accomplished it. He found, oddly, that the not-knowing troubled him considerably less than he'd have expected it to, back when knowing every detail of his own circumstances had still mattered to him as much as it once had.

Whatever came next — rest, return, some other shape entirely — no longer felt like a sentence being handed down to him. It felt, plainly and strangely, like a door simply standing open, waiting for him to decide, in his own time, whether he actually wanted to walk through it.

What Belial would choose, arriving here, he did not yet know himself. He thought of every decision he'd ever made across his long existence in service of some larger ambition, some throne or territory or legion worth the winning. None of those old motives seemed to reach him here at all. He found, drifting in that unfamiliar stillness, that he had no ready answer for what he actually wanted next, only the strange, unfamiliar comfort of understanding that the choice, whatever it eventually amounted to, would finally belong to him alone, for the first time in longer than he could actually remember.

Somewhere behind him now, fading fast, he thought he could still sense the throne room he would likely never occupy again, a laugh that had once filled an entire hall with something that had, for a very long time, passed convincingly enough for pride. He let it fade without reaching for it, unwilling, for once in his very long existence, to spend his own attention on something that no longer had any real claim on him at all.
