# B3C37 – The Sovereigns' Farewell

Gabriel stood alone on the gray, silent waste where the Concordat had first been sealed, and felt, for the first time since this long war had begun, a stillness that carried nothing left in it worth actually fearing. The wound that had once hummed beneath every step he took across this ash — Thamorak's distant, patient pressure, always faintly audible if he let himself listen for it — had gone quiet at last, replaced by something considerably calmer, a low, contented hum where a scream had lived only days before.

He had felt the change arrive gradually rather than all at once, a weight lifting slowly from a place behind his ribs he'd stopped noticing the shape of until it was already gone. He had come to this exact field the moment he'd felt certain enough of it to trust the feeling, unwilling to celebrate a peace he hadn't yet confirmed was actually real. Standing here now, in the same ash where two ancient enemies had once agreed to something neither of them had called reconciliation, he found himself certain of it at last.

He had spent the intervening hours since the wave first reached him fielding reports from every corner of the alliance's shared command, each one confirming some fresh piece of the same impossible fact — the void gone quiet across every sector it had once threatened, fleets standing down without a single further order needed to make them do it, commanders on both sides reporting the same bewildered, grateful silence he now felt settling into his own chest. He had read those reports carefully, one after another, and still found himself needing to stand here, on this exact ground, before he could actually let himself believe any of it.

He was not surprised when Lucifer's shape resolved out of the gray distance a few minutes later, unhurried and alone, the same solitary composure Gabriel remembered him keeping while he waited on this exact ground the first time. No retinue trailed him this time either, though Gabriel supposed there was little need left for one, given how thoroughly the field's original purpose had already been settled.

---

Neither of them spoke as Lucifer closed the remaining distance between them. Gabriel studied him a long moment, searching his face for whatever toll the last several days had actually taken, and found something there he hadn't expected to see on a man who had just watched his own ancient rival succeed at something Lucifer himself had never once believed possible. He looked, Gabriel thought, like a man who had spent every hour since carrying something considerably heavier than victory usually left behind.

He thought of the last time they'd stood on this exact ground, the careful, guarded terms both of them had needed to negotiate before either could trust the other with so much as a shared battle plan. None of that guardedness remained visible now, not in his posture, not in the flat, unhurried way he'd crossed the last stretch of ash between them. Gabriel found the absence of it almost as unsettling as the silence Thamorak had left behind.

"The Void is sealed," Gabriel said, once Lucifer had stopped an arm's length away. It was not a question. He had felt the truth of it too clearly across the last few hours to need it confirmed.

"It is," Lucifer said. His voice carried none of the composed authority Gabriel remembered from their last meeting on this exact ground, only a plain, unadorned weariness that Gabriel suspected cost him something to let show at all. "Azael paid the price."

Gabriel felt the name settle into him with a weight he hadn't fully braced for. He had met Azael only briefly, across the handful of councils that had brought this fragile alliance together, had come to respect the sage's quiet, unhurried certainty without ever once getting close enough to call it friendship. He understood, hearing Lucifer speak the name so plainly, exactly what kind of price was actually being described.

He thought, briefly, of Michael, of the brother who had actually walked this entire strange road alongside Azael from its earliest days, and found himself grateful, in a way he hadn't expected to feel grateful for anything today, that the price paid here hadn't fallen on him instead. He said nothing of that particular relief aloud, unwilling to let it color whatever grief this moment actually deserved from him.

"He's gone," Gabriel said.

"Not entirely," Lucifer said. "Though I confess I don't fully understand the distinction myself. Something remains. I felt it, the same way I felt the Void seal — a presence where I expected only absence." He studied the gray waste around them a moment, as though searching it for some visible sign of what he was describing. "Whatever he became, it isn't nothing. I take what comfort I can in that, for whatever comfort is currently worth to either of us."

"That's more comfort than I expected to hear you admit to needing," Gabriel said, though the words carried none of the old edge they might have carried between them a season ago, back when admitting to any weakness at all would have counted, on either side, as a genuine tactical error.

"I've found myself needing considerably more of it lately than I'm accustomed to," Lucifer said. "I don't expect that to become a habit. I simply note it, for whatever the noting is worth."

---

Gabriel wanted to ask why. He wanted to ask whether it had been worth it, whether the cost Azael had paid actually balanced against whatever peace now sat here in this quiet field between them, whether any of it could have gone differently if someone, anyone, had found a gentler answer sooner. He held the question a long moment, weighing it against Lucifer's own carefully guarded expression, and found, studying him, that the question would serve no purpose either of them actually needed serving. Whatever answer Lucifer might have offered, Gabriel understood it wouldn't change the shape of what had already happened, and suspected asking it aloud would only cost Lucifer something further he had no right to demand of him.

He thought of every version of this conversation he might have rehearsed on the walk here, had he known in advance exactly what he would find waiting for him on this field. None of those rehearsed versions had included the particular restraint he found himself choosing now, setting the harder question aside not out of cowardice but out of something closer to mercy — a recognition that some costs simply didn't deserve to be measured aloud by anyone who hadn't actually paid them.

He said something else instead, the words arriving before he'd fully decided to speak them.

"Be well, brother."

He watched the address land, watched something flicker behind Lucifer's composed features that Gabriel read, cautiously, as genuine surprise — not at the sentiment itself, perhaps, but at the word he'd chosen to carry it. He had not called Lucifer that in longer than he could easily measure, had not expected, walking onto this field today, that the word would come to him so readily once the moment for it actually arrived.

He thought, watching Lucifer absorb it, of every formal title either of them had used across the long negotiations that had built this fragile alliance — Herald, Adversary, Sovereign, words chosen carefully to keep the actual distance between them properly measured. None of those titles had felt adequate to whatever this particular moment actually required. He found he didn't regret reaching past them, whatever awkwardness the choice had apparently cost Lucifer to receive.

---

Lucifer's mouth curved into something that wasn't quite a smile, sad and strange in a way Gabriel found himself unable to fully read. "An interesting choice of words," Lucifer said. "I have no intention of being 'well,' Gabriel. Wellness was never a state I found much use for, even before any of this started. I simply intend to be." He held Gabriel's gaze a moment, something wry entering his expression despite the weariness still visible beneath it. "I'll leave the wellness to you and your council. I understand it suits your side considerably better than it ever suited mine."

"That will have to be enough, then," Gabriel said.

"It generally has been." Lucifer studied him a moment longer, something almost fond crossing his expression before it settled back into its usual careful distance. "You should return to your own council, Herald. They'll want to hear this from you directly, rather than through whatever secondhand report eventually reaches them."

"They will," Gabriel agreed. "Though I suspect none of them will quite believe it fully until they've felt the silence for themselves, the same way I needed to stand here before I could actually trust it myself."

"Few things worth believing ever arrive easily," Lucifer said, something almost gentle passing through his tired voice as he said it. He began to turn away, unhurried, his form already losing its solid edges at the periphery, dissolving into shadow the way smoke loses its shape once it clears a doorway. "Do not expect peace, Gabriel," he said, pausing once, looking back over one shoulder, the last of his composed features already fading into the gray behind him. "Only... a more interesting war."

He was gone before Gabriel could offer any answer to that, the last trace of his shadowed form scattering into the gray ash without leaving so much as a footprint to mark where he'd stood. Gabriel remained a long moment in the silence that followed, studying the empty stretch of waste where two ancient enemies had first agreed to something neither of them had ever quite managed to name properly, and found the warning settling into him with more weight than a simple parting phrase should have carried.

He thought of every account he'd ever read describing Lucifer as a creature built entirely from ambition and spite, records written by scholars who had never once stood close enough to see the particular weariness currently settling into a man who had just watched an ally he'd never expected to trust pay the highest price either side of this war had asked of anyone yet. He suspected few beings on either side of the divide would ever fully understand what today had actually cost either of them, standing here in this exact field, and found himself, walking back toward whatever came next, unwilling to let that cost pass entirely unremembered.

He thought too of Lucifer's parting words, of the particular shape of a war that would apparently keep finding new ways to prove interesting long after this one had finally, properly finished settling. He found he trusted the warning more than he distrusted it, coming from a man who had just spent the better part of an hour demonstrating exactly how little either of them still had reason to lie to the other about anything at all.

The ash shifted quietly beneath his steps as he turned to go, the same ash that had once marked the site of a temporary cessation of hostilities and now marked something else entirely — not friendship, not truly, but a debt honestly acknowledged between two beings who had spent an age refusing each other so much as that. Gabriel found, crossing the last stretch of gray waste back toward his own waiting world, that he intended to carry the memory of it carefully, whatever war still waited somewhere ahead to test it.

He glanced back once before the field fell fully behind him, half expecting, despite everything he already knew about how thoroughly Lucifer's kind tended to vanish, to find some final trace of him still lingering there. He found only the same gray emptiness that had greeted him on his arrival, indistinguishable now from any other quiet stretch of ash he might have crossed. He supposed that was fitting, in its own way. Whatever had actually passed between them today had never needed a witness to make it real.
