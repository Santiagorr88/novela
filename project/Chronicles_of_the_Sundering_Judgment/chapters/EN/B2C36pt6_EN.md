# B2C36.6 – A Familiar Shape in Borrowed Words

Lucifer read everything that crossed his court requiring his blessing, not because a sovereign of his standing needed to, but because he had learned, across an age long enough to make the habit permanent rather than merely careful, that the things a ruler delegated without reading were exactly the things that eventually delegated him out of relevance entirely. Tonight's stack was thinner than most — a trade concession from a border duchy, a captain's request for reassignment, the quarterly accounting from a mine three of his commanders had been quietly contesting for longer than any of them would admit to his face. None of it demanded more attention than the habit itself supplied.

The pages that came last were sealed twice over, first with Agares's own mark and then, unusually, with a note in the commander's own hand rather than a scribe's, asking — not informing, asking — for Lucifer's blessing on the wording before it left Dis for the mortal underground.

It was, on its face, a courtesy no one in Hell's current order strictly required of him. Agares answered to Asmodeus in every matter of ordinary chain and rank, and Asmodeus's own seal appeared nowhere on the pages at all — a detail Lucifer noted the way he noted most small deviations from the shape a thing was supposed to take, filed rather than acted upon, worth remembering precisely because it cost him nothing to remember it. A commander skipping his own lord to ask a favor of the throne above that lord was, more often than not, a commander who had already begun weighing which master's approval would matter more the next time weighing was required. Lucifer had watched that particular calculation made a hundred times by a hundred ambitious subordinates, and had long since stopped finding it either surprising or, on its own, alarming. He filed this one alongside the rest, and read on.

---

The attendant who brought them in did not quite manage to keep the surprise off her face, and Lucifer, watching her fail to manage it, found the failure itself worth a small private amusement.

"Lord Agares rarely troubles anyone for permission smaller than treason, my lord," she said, setting the sealed pages down with the particular care of someone handling a thing she did not fully trust. "He asked that you read it yourself. Not summarized. He was specific about that."

"Agares asking permission at all is worth noting on its own," Lucifer said, breaking the second seal with a single unhurried motion. "Whatever this is, he wants my name on it, not merely my silence. That's not humility. That's a man building himself a shield out of my signature, in case the wording ever needs defending later." He said it without any particular heat, the way he noted most maneuvers made inside his own court — as a fact about the one making the move, not a grievance against him.

"Shall I wait, my lord?"

"No. This will take longer than waiting deserves."

---

He read it once quickly, to take its shape, and a second time slowly, the way he read anything he suspected had been built with more care than its length let on. It was, within the first page, exactly what Agares's gift had always promised and rarely delivered with this much discipline — an account of the Codex's contested fragments that never once stated a falsehood outright, and never once left a reader with anything resembling the truth either. One line, early on, held his attention longer than the rest: *the Codex speaks of three primordial powers whose reunion the old prophecies neither confirm nor forbid — a promise to some, a warning to others, and the wise reader is invited to decide for himself which.* Every clause in it was, strictly, accurate. Nothing in it told a reader what to fear or hope for, and everything in it made certain they would leave the page having chosen one anyway, convinced the choosing had been entirely their own. Every sentence in the pages that followed did the same honest work in isolation. The arrangement between the sentences did the actual lying, so cleanly that Lucifer, reading it a third time purely for the pleasure of watching the mechanism operate, could not locate a single line he would have struck for being untrue.

It was a discipline he respected more than most things demonstrated to him in a given season — the truth, arranged rather than invented, doing more damage than any lie a lesser mind might have reached for instead. Watching Agares wield it with this much precision was not unlike watching a promising captain execute a maneuver Lucifer himself had taught, years before, to someone else entirely — the particular, complicated satisfaction of seeing a lesson land exactly as intended, on a student who would never think to thank him for it, or even to notice where he had learned it.

He sent the pages back before the hour was out, unmarked, with a single line for Agares alone: *Not a syllable changed. Use it exactly as written, and remember whose court taught you to build a sentence this well.* It was not quite praise. It came close enough that Agares would read it as some, and Lucifer judged that close enough would serve its purpose.

---

He might have set the matter down entirely there, folded into the evening's ordinary business alongside the border duchy and the captain's reassignment, if a single phrase from the second page hadn't stayed with him longer than the rest — *three primordials, reunited* — a small, almost decorative flourish in a document built mostly of careful omissions, the kind of phrase Agares had likely judged nothing more than good rhythm.

Lucifer read it again, alone now, the attendant long since dismissed and no one else left in the chamber to notice him doing it. The phrase itself told him nothing he did not already know. He had read a hundred versions of the same three words across his long watch over both realms, prophecy and rumor and deliberate misdirection alike, and had never once found cause to linger on any of them past the length of a single dismissive breath.

He lingered on this one — not for what it claimed, claims true or false having never troubled him in themselves, but for some quality in the shape the words made together, a cadence he could not place and, more troublingly, felt he ought to have been able to place. He had spent longer than Agares had existed learning which shapes in this business were worth examining directly and which were safer left at the edge of attention. This one he found himself unable to set down as easily as the rest, and did not examine too closely why.

There was no one to ask, in any case, and the phrase gave nothing further for the asking. He set the pages face down on the table beside him, and did not turn them over again.
