# B1L01 – The First Threshold

## Across the Threshold

Belial stepped through the portal and felt the battlefield behind him vanish, replaced in the space of a single stride by a silence so complete it pressed against his ears like a physical weight. The Weeping Sepulcher received him without ceremony, without light, without anything resembling a welcome, as indifferent to his arrival as it would apparently be to his departure.

The ground beneath his boots was black crystal, jagged and uneven, and it chimed faintly with every step he took, a sound too close to weeping for Belial's taste, thin and wavering and utterly without source. The air here was thick in a way no ordinary air had any right to be, heavy on his tongue with the taste of salt and something colder than salt, something he did not yet have a word for. Mourning hung over this whole plane like a mist that never lifted, ancient and undirected, grief with no single grave left to mark.

He took a moment to simply stand there and let his eyes adjust to what passed for light in this place — a low, colorless glow that seemed to rise from the crystal itself rather than fall from any sky, throwing long shadows in directions that did not quite match where a proper light source would have cast them. No wind stirred the air, and yet it moved anyway, in slow currents that carried the salt-taste past him and away again, as though the plane itself were breathing very, very slowly. He had crossed into strange places before, in centuries of service that had taken him through planes no sane soul would have chosen to visit twice. None of them had ever felt quite this deliberately unwelcoming, as though the ground beneath him resented the weight of anything still capable of ambition.

## The First Signal

He had not gone twenty paces before something reached him — not a sound exactly, not a sight, but an ache that bloomed suddenly behind his ribs, sharp enough that he nearly mistook it for a wound. It came without warning, the way a fever comes, and left just as suddenly.

It felt, for one disorienting moment, like the tomb itself calling to him across whatever distance still separated them, a signal of suffering so concentrated that surely nothing else on this plane could produce it. Belial straightened, steadied himself against the ache, and turned toward the source of it with the same confidence that had carried him through every calculated risk of the last several weeks.

He had learned, across centuries of hunting down things other demons considered too dangerous or too well-hidden to bother with, that the loudest signal in any unfamiliar territory was usually the one worth following first, and that patience spent chasing quieter clues was, more often than not, patience wasted. Whatever waited at the heart of this place, it wanted finding badly enough to announce itself this loudly, and he saw no reason yet to doubt that instinct now, in a plane that had given him nothing so far but silence and salt-thick air. He quickened his pace toward it, Ruach loose and ready at his hip, entirely certain that confidence alone would be enough to carry him the rest of the way. The ache pulsed once more, sharper, and he found himself almost grateful for it — a landmark, at last, in a landscape that had offered him none.

## What the Ground Refuses

The crystal path narrowed as he pushed forward, forcing his stride into something shorter and more careful than he had any patience for, and when he tried to force his old pace back onto it anyway, the ground answered him with a warning of its own.

A jagged edge caught the side of his boot and opened a shallow cut through the leather, and a step later his heel came down wrong on ground that gave beneath him exactly enough to nearly cost him his balance. A third misstep sent a spray of crystal shards skittering away from his boot with a sound uncomfortably close to a gasp. Belial cursed under his breath and pressed on without slowing, dismissing all three as nothing more than the ordinary hazard of unfamiliar terrain, the kind of minor inconvenience any battlefield offered a commander moving too fast for his own good.

He did not stop to consider that the ground itself might be answering him in the only language it had. Listening to ground was not a skill his long service had ever required of him — only walking across it, and the habit of centuries was not about to break itself for a few scattered cuts and a stumble. If anything, the small resistance only sharpened his impatience, each fresh sting against his boot one more reason to close the distance to whatever waited ahead of him as quickly as his legs would carry him there.

## The Guardian Speaks

The crystal opened into a wider clearing ahead, and something rose to meet him there — a shape built from the same black, weeping crystal as the ground itself, vaguely humanoid, vaster than any single grief should have been able to hold together. It rose slowly, gathering itself out of the surrounding floor the way a tide gathers before it breaks.

It did not attack. It did not raise anything resembling a weapon, only stood at the clearing's center with a stillness that felt, despite its size, entirely without menace. Belial had faced enough guardians across enough conquered thresholds to know the posture of one weighing an attack, and this thing carried none of it — no crouch, no readiness, nothing but the bulk of something that seemed to have stood exactly this way for longer than Belial cared to imagine. When it spoke, the voice that reached Belial did not belong to one throat but to a thousand, fused into a single slow cadence that seemed to come from everywhere in the clearing at once. "Nothing is taken here," it said. "Only given." Belial had not expected the thing he was about to fight to have a voice at all, and for one brief instant he simply stood there, caught off guard by the sound of it rather than any particular respect for what it said, the thousand fused voices still echoing faintly off the surrounding crystal. The pause did not last. "Then give it to me," he answered, contempt already hardening back into his voice. The guardian did not answer him with any further words. It answered with the first blow.

## What Force Cannot Take

The strike came faster than its size should have allowed, a crystalline fist connecting with Belial's shoulder hard enough to send him staggering back several paces across ground that offered him no better footing than it had offered him minutes before. Pain bloomed hot through the joint, and beneath the pain, stranger still, a fleeting wash of sorrow that had nothing to do with the blow itself, as though the impact had carried some fragment of the guardian's own nature into him along with the force of it. He shook the feeling off before it could root any deeper, unwilling to let anything but his own fury occupy the space this fight demanded.

He came up swinging, Ruach meeting the guardian's next blow with a crack that should have shattered crystal and instead only sent a shudder of pain up through Belial's own arm, absorbed rather than returned. He circled left, testing for an opening in the guardian's stance that never quite materialized, and struck again, low this time, aiming for what might have been a knee on a body built along more ordinary lines. The blow landed exactly where he had aimed it, and the guardian did not so much as shift its weight in response, the crystal beneath his boots chiming softly with the effort of the strike he had just wasted.

He struck again, harder, and again found nothing on the other end of the blow but more of the same steady, absorbing resistance, as though the guardian simply had nowhere left in it for violence to land and do anything at all. Belial's frustration sharpened with every failed exchange, each swing faster and less measured than the one before it, the kind of escalation that had won him a hundred fights against opponents who eventually cracked under enough sustained pressure. This one did not crack. It simply waited, absorbed, and waited again, seeming as patient in defense as it had been in speech, its crystalline form registering each new impact with nothing more than a fresh, faint chime. A third strike from the guardian caught him across the ribs and put him on the ground, breath driven entirely out of him, Ruach skidding away across the crystal floor beyond his immediate reach. This time the sorrow did not stay fleeting. It poured into him with the blow itself, undeniable and total — the specific, unbearable weight of a hand opening again and again to let something irreplaceable go, without ever once being allowed to close back around it. He had no name ready for what he was feeling, and no way to shove it back out of himself the way he had shoved the first small ache aside — this one had come in through the wound itself and settled somewhere he could not reach to pull it back out. He lay there a moment with his ears ringing and the taste of salt and blood mixing on his tongue, that borrowed grief still sitting heavy and unwelcome in his chest, the crystal beneath his cheek cold against a face that had just been thoroughly beaten. From this angle, flat against the ground, the guardian looked less like an opponent and more like a fixture of the landscape itself, unmoved, as though patiently waiting for him to decide what came next. He was not dying. He understood that much even through the pain — this was correction, not execution, a lesson forced directly into him rather than merely delivered around him.

The guardian did not press its advantage. It simply withdrew, retreating back toward the clearing's center at the same unhurried pace it had approached with, its crystalline form settling back into a stance no more aggressive than the one it had first risen from. As it went, it repeated the same words it had opened with, seeming to carry no triumph in them at all, no cruelty, only a patience that felt, somehow, older than Belial's own rage by more years than rage could count. "Nothing is taken here." For the first time since he had crossed the portal, Belial found he had no answer ready. He lay there in the settling silence, watching the guardian recede, and understood that its withdrawal did not seem like mercy either — only what he took for the end of a lesson apparently deemed sufficient.

## What the Sepulcher Demands

He lay there a moment longer than his pride would have liked, chest heaving, one hand pressed to ribs that throbbed with every breath, and let the fury build in him unchecked because there was, for once, no audience left here to perform composure for. The crystal chimed faintly around him with every ragged breath he took, the plane's endless mourning entirely indifferent to whatever indignity had just been visited on him, and Belial found that indifference somehow harder to sit with than the pain itself.

He had come into this place expecting exactly what every other plane he had ever conquered had eventually given him — resistance, yes, but resistance that broke, sooner or later, beneath enough force applied in the right place. He turned the fight over in his mind once more, searching for the moment where a different strike, a different angle, might have changed its outcome, and found none. Belial could only read the encounter as a correction rather than a battle: not hatred, as far as he could tell, but evidence that violence held no value here at all, no matter how much of it he spent. Belial pulled himself upright, wincing at the pull of bruised ribs, and crossed to where Ruach had skidded across the crystal, its head still faintly warm from the exchange. He weighed it once in his hand, testing the ache in his shoulder against the weapon's familiar weight, and stood there breathing hard in the ringing silence the guardian had left behind, the clearing around him already settling back into its usual undisturbed quiet. He understood, with a clarity that did nothing to cool his temper, that brute force would win him nothing further on this ground. Whatever this place seemed to demand of him, he would have to learn its terms before it gave up anything at all — and understanding that, for a man who had spent centuries taking whatever he wanted by exactly the method that had just failed him, felt like its own particular kind of defeat. He looked back once toward the clearing where the guardian had already gone still again, its outline already blurring back into the surrounding crystal as though it had never risen at all. Then he turned deeper into the plane, toward wherever the ache in his chest still pointed him. He would find another way in. He always had, and he saw no reason yet to believe this place would prove the exception.
