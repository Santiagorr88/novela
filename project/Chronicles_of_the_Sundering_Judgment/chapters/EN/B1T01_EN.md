# B1T01 – The Island That Appears on No Map

## The Ordinary Work

Cassiel found the wounded soul exactly where Silens told him it would be, curled at the base of a collapsed watchtower on a battlefield three days cold, its light guttering low but not yet gone. He knelt beside it the way he knelt beside every soul Silens led him to, patient and unhurried, letting the staff's quiet pulse guide his hands to wherever the wound actually sat rather than wherever it appeared to sit. This one had been easy to find and harder to steady, a soldier's soul so thoroughly disoriented by its own death that it took the better part of an hour of careful, wordless attention before it settled enough to be guided onward. Cassiel did not mind the hour. He rarely minded the time any soul actually needed.

This was the work, and had been the work for longer than he could easily recall — not glory, not conquest, only the slow, unglamorous business of finding what pain left behind and walking it gently toward wherever it needed to go next. He had learned early in his service that Silens rewarded exactly the kind of attention most soldiers found tedious: stillness, patience, a willingness to sit with suffering rather than rush past it toward the next assignment. He had built his entire reputation on precisely that willingness, and had never once regretted the reputation it earned him, however unglamorous it sounded beside the battlefield exploits of his fellow Captains.

He rose from the watchtower's ruins once the soul had finally settled, brushing ash and old dust from his knees with the same unhurried care he brought to everything else, and took a moment simply to survey the quiet battlefield around him before moving on. Three days cold, and still the ground carried traces of what had happened here — scorched grass, a shattered banner half-buried in churned earth, the particular stillness of a place that had recently hosted violence and had not yet fully settled back into the ordinary rhythm of the world. He had walked a hundred battlefields exactly like this one over the centuries. Each still asked the same patient attention of him as the last, and he had never once begrudged giving it.

Silens hummed quietly against his palm as he walked, its usual steady pulse cataloguing the plane's remaining traces without urgency, the staff doing precisely what it had always done in his hands — finding what needed finding, at whatever pace the finding actually required. He expected, walking the last stretch back toward the watch post, an unremarkable end to an unremarkable assignment. Most days ended exactly that way, and he had long since made peace with the sameness of it.

## The Echo That Wasn't Pain

He felt the anomaly on his way back from the watchtower, faint enough that a less attentive scout might easily have mistaken it for residue from the soul he had just guided onward. Silens pulsed differently for this than it pulsed for wounded souls, a texture Cassiel had never once encountered in all his years of exactly this kind of work — not grief, not fear, not the raw disorientation of a fresh death, but something like laughter caught in the fabric of the place, old and strange and entirely unlike anything Silens had ever led him toward before.

He stood with the sensation a long while, turning it over the way he turned over any unfamiliar reading, patient with his own uncertainty the same way he was patient with every soul he had ever guided. Silens had never once misled him toward nothing. He was not prepared to assume it had started now.

He tried, standing there, to catalogue the reading against every unusual signature he had encountered across his long service, searching for some precedent that would let him file it away cleanly and move on with the rest of his rounds. He found none. Wounded souls carried grief, or fear, or the raw disorientation of a death still settling into memory — sensations Silens had shown him countless times, each one distinct but each one, ultimately, recognizable as some variation on suffering. This carried nothing of that. It carried instead a strange, settled quality he could only compare, however imperfectly, to something remembered rather than something felt in the moment, an echo of an echo rather than a fresh wound.

He walked a slow circle around the spot where the reading had first reached him, testing whether it strengthened or faded with distance, and found it neither strengthened nor faded so much as simply persisted, faint and unchanging regardless of how far he moved in any direction. That, more than anything else about it, unsettled him. Wounded souls had a location. This reading seemed to have none he could pin down, as though it belonged less to any single point on the plane than to the plane itself.

## Whether to Report It

He found Ophaniel at the watch post an hour later, filing his own routine report on the battlefield's remaining stragglers, and mentioned the anomaly in passing rather than as anything requiring immediate escalation. "Anything I should send reinforcements for?" Ophaniel asked, glancing up from his own ledger with what looked to Cassiel like mild, procedural curiosity. "Not yet..." Cassiel said. "It might be nothing. I'd rather not waste anyone's time chasing a hunch." Ophaniel accepted the answer without further comment, as Cassiel had often seen others at the watch do when he gave his assessment, and returned to his own ledger without pressing further.

Cassiel considered, walking away from the post, whether he had been entirely honest in that answer. It was not quite nothing, whatever it actually was. It was, at minimum, the strangest reading Silens had ever handed him, and he had decided, somewhere in the moment of speaking to Ophaniel, that he would rather follow it alone first than commit anyone else's attention to something he could not yet describe clearly enough to justify the request. It was, he told himself, simply good practice — verify before escalating, confirm before committing resources. He did not examine, too closely, whether that practice was the whole of his reasoning or only the most defensible part of it.

He gathered what supplies the assignment might reasonably require before setting out — provisions for several days, nothing that would draw attention if anyone happened to notice him leaving, a decision made as quietly as the reading itself had first reached him. He had never once needed to justify a solo scouting trip before, his record having earned him that particular latitude years ago, and he did not expect this one to be any different. It was, in every practical sense, exactly the kind of assignment he had taken a hundred times before. Only the reading itself, faint and unlocatable and utterly unlike anything else Silens had ever shown him, made this particular trip feel different from the rest.

The trail took the better part of a full day to follow, the reading strengthening only marginally as he moved, offering him nothing so simple as a straight line to walk. He tracked it instead the way he tracked any faint signal — small corrections made continuously, trusting the accumulated pattern of many uncertain readings over any single confident one. By the time the coastline finally came into view, unlisted on any chart he carried and unfamiliar in a way that struck him as more than simple cartographic oversight, he had long since stopped expecting the trail to lead him anywhere ordinary.

## What Turein Held

The trail led him, over the following day, to an island that appeared on no chart he consulted beforehand, its coastline shifting subtly enough between his approach and his landing that he began to suspect the maps themselves were not entirely at fault for missing it. Turein's interior unsettled him the moment he set foot past its treeline — vegetation growing in slow, deliberate spirals rather than any pattern he recognized from anywhere else he had scouted, the wind itself seeming to fold back on its own passage in ways that left the light moving strangely across the canopy overhead.

He felt, standing in the heart of it, some faint and unaccountable conviction that this place held something connected to Solmire — not a certainty he could trace to any single sensory detail, only a persistent, low-grade recognition he could not yet put a name to. He had felt nothing like it scouting any other location in all his years of exactly this work. He trusted the feeling regardless, the same way he had learned to trust every faint signal Silens had ever handed him, certain or not.

He moved deeper into the interior with the same careful, methodical attention he brought to any unfamiliar terrain, cataloguing details as he went out of long habit — the spiraling growth pattern repeating at every scale he examined, from the smallest curling fern to the largest tree's own twisting trunk, as though whatever principle governed this island's growth applied itself with equal insistence at every size. He had scouted strange terrain before. He had never scouted anywhere that repeated a single pattern with quite this much consistency, and the consistency itself, more than any single strange detail, was what convinced him this place was not simply unusual by accident.

The light overhead moved oddly too, he noticed, shifting angle and color in ways that did not track cleanly against the position of the sun he occasionally glimpsed through gaps in the canopy. He filed the detail away alongside everything else, unwilling yet to draw any conclusion from a single day's observation, though the accumulating strangeness of the place had already begun to feel less like coincidence and more like design.

## Losing the Way

The island's spiraling geography disoriented him more thoroughly than he expected, a slow, creeping confusion that built without any single dramatic moment he could point to as its cause. He found himself, an hour into his search, retracing ground he was almost certain he had already covered, the angle of the light overhead shifting in ways that made judging direction by it entirely unreliable. It was, he realized with some genuine surprise, the first time in his long career that he had actually lost his way scouting anything at all — a disorientation total enough that he stopped moving entirely for a long moment, forcing himself to breathe through the rising unease rather than push blindly forward against it.

He steadied himself eventually by focusing on Silens rather than the shifting terrain around him, trusting the staff's own quiet pulse over anything his eyes or his sense of direction could currently offer him. The pulse held steady even where the ground did not, and he followed it rather than his own increasingly unreliable instincts, one careful step at a time back toward whatever had drawn him here in the first place.

It was, he thought, moving carefully forward, an uncomfortable lesson to learn this late in his service — that a scout's greatest asset, honed over centuries of exactly this kind of work, could still fail him completely against terrain built on principles he had never before encountered. He did not resent the lesson. He simply resolved to remember it, filing the experience away alongside everything else Turein had already taught him about the limits of what he thought he knew.

His own pulse had climbed higher than he liked to admit during the worst of the disorientation, an unfamiliar tightness in his chest that took real effort to breathe through evenly. He had faced hostile terrain before, and hostile soldiers, and had met both with the same even composure that had defined his entire service. This was different, a threat with no visible shape to brace against, no enemy to read and counter, only his own senses quietly failing him one careful step at a time. He forced his breathing back into its familiar rhythm before allowing himself to move any further, unwilling to let panic compound whatever the island's geometry was already doing to his sense of direction.

## What He Found

He found it near the island's center, after the better part of another hour spent retracing his own uncertain path, in a small clearing where the spiraling vegetation opened onto bare stone — a resonance unmistakably tied to Solmire, faint and old, carrying none of the sword's usual clean authority and something instead that felt closer to memory than presence, as though the blade itself, or some echo of it, had passed through this exact place long before Cassiel had ever heard its name. He held the reading a long while, letting Silens confirm what his own instincts had already suspected since he first set foot on the island's shifting shore.

Whatever this island actually was, whatever connection it carried to the sword now hanging at Miguel's side, Cassiel understood, standing over the resonance, that he had found something worth the trouble of returning for. He would need better instruments than his own uncertain footing to properly chart this place. He would need, he suspected, considerably more time than a single day's careful scouting had given him.

He knelt beside the resonance a while longer before finally turning to leave, memorizing every detail he could of the clearing's position relative to the coastline he had crossed that morning, unwilling to trust a return trip entirely to Silens's guidance a second time after everything the island's shifting geography had already cost him once. For now, he had exactly what he had come for — confirmation that Turein held something real, and the growing, settled certainty that he intended to keep following it, whatever it eventually turned out to be.
