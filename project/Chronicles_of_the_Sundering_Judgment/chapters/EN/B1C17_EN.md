# B1C17 – The Irreverent Judgment

## What Gabriel Can No Longer Prevent

Gabriel found him in the armory before dawn, still visibly working through the wreckage of the fractured council table days after the fact, and Miguel suspected from the set of his brother's shoulders what conversation he had come to have before either of them said a single word. "Don't launch this," Gabriel said, no preamble, no softening. "Not for strategy. I've read the reports — the outpost is minor, the risk is negligible. That's not why I'm asking you not to go."

Miguel kept his eyes on Solmire's hilt, checking a fitting that needed no checking. "Then why."

"Because you're not chasing a victory," Gabriel said. "You're chasing something to prove to yourself, after what happened in front of the entire Council, and that's exactly the kind of decision that already went wrong once." Miguel felt the words land somewhere he had no intention of letting Gabriel see land. "You don't know that," he said, though even as he said it he understood how little conviction the denial actually carried. Gabriel held his gaze a moment longer, as though waiting for something Miguel was not prepared to give him, and then simply nodded, a nod Miguel read as resignation, as though his brother had already known, before he ever opened his mouth, exactly how this would end.

He said nothing further. There was nothing further to say that the fractured table in the council chamber had not already said more plainly than either of them could manage in words, and Miguel understood, watching his brother turn to leave without another attempt at persuasion, that whatever authority Gabriel's counsel still carried was no longer enough to stop him today. He did not call after him. He finished checking the fitting instead, and told himself the silence between them was simply practical — nothing left worth saying, not grief over what the silence actually meant.

He thought, once Gabriel's footsteps had faded down the corridor, of every other time his brother had come to him with exactly this kind of warning across the centuries of their service together, and of how easily, once, he would have listened. There had been a version of himself, not so long ago by the accounting of years that mattered to beings like them, who trusted Gabriel's read on a situation over his own certainty without needing to be asked twice. He did not know precisely when that trust had eroded into this — a warning heard and set aside in the same breath, a brother's counsel weighed and found wanting before it had even finished being spoken. He did not have the space left in him today to examine the loss closely. He set it aside instead, alongside everything else he had learned to set aside since the sword first came into his hand.

The armory around him had gone very quiet in Gabriel's absence, the ordinary clatter of the Bastion's morning business feeling, for a moment, entirely too distant to reach him. He finished his preparations methodically, the way he always had, though the methodical calm felt thinner today than it usually did, stretched over something considerably less settled underneath. He told himself the thinness did not matter. He had a target, and a purpose, and Solmire's familiar weight already settling into his grip the way it always settled, promising him, in its own wordless fashion, exactly the clarity he had come here looking for.

## A Desperate Gambit

He found the outpost by midmorning, a minor demonic garrison holding ground that mattered to no one's larger strategy, exactly the kind of target that would once have felt beneath his notice entirely. Today it felt like exactly what he needed. He had not slept properly since the council chamber, had not managed a single clean, uncomplicated moment since his own fist split marble in front of every commander he answered to, and some part of him, quieter than the part still smarting from Gabriel's warning, understood precisely why this particular target had drawn him here. He needed to feel Solmire's old, absolute purpose again, clean and simple and entirely without the weight that had been crushing him since the vision of the hand, since the laughter, since everything that had followed.

He announced the attack himself rather than delegating it, gathering the assault force with a decisiveness he had not managed in days, and did not once let himself circle back to the warning he had already decided to ignore. There was no room left in him today for a warning like that. There was only the outpost ahead, and Solmire's familiar weight in his hand, and the desperate hope that the old certainty still lived somewhere inside both of them.

The soldiers he gathered fell into formation without question, the way they always fell into formation around him, and Miguel found himself, watching them assemble, almost envious of whatever simple confidence let them follow him this readily. None of them had stood in that council chamber. None of them had watched the table crack beneath his own fist, or felt the particular horror of an entire assembled command staring at him in the silence afterward. He imagined that, to them, he was still exactly what he had always been — Miguel, sword of Heaven, certain and unshakable. He wanted, badly, to feel like that man again himself, if only for the length of a single clean battle.

The march to the outpost took the better part of the morning, the terrain rough enough to demand real attention, and Miguel used the effort of it gratefully, glad of anything that occupied the part of his mind still circling Gabriel's warning despite his best efforts to leave it behind in the armory. He did not speak much along the way. Few of the soldiers expected him to. He had never been a commander who filled marches with unnecessary conversation, and today the silence suited him better than it ever had before, a private space in which he did not have to perform the certainty he no longer entirely felt.

## The Rout

The battle, when it came, was over almost before it properly began. The demons holding the outpost were undertrained, underprepared, entirely unequipped for the force Miguel had brought against them, and within minutes their formation broke apart into open, disorganized flight. Angelic soldiers pressed the advantage on every side, driving the collapse further, and Miguel felt, watching the rout unfold exactly as cleanly as he had needed it to unfold, the first genuine ease he had felt in days settle briefly into his chest. A conventional victory. Nothing complicated about it. Exactly what he had come here to find.

He moved through the collapsing formation himself, Solmire meeting every blade that dared rise against it and ending the exchange in a single stroke each time, the sword's familiar rhythm settling into his arm the way it always had before everything in his life had grown so complicated. For a few brief minutes, cutting his way through a genuinely resisting line, he felt something close to the man he had been before the council chamber, before the dream, before any of it. He let himself have those minutes without questioning them too closely. He had come here specifically for this feeling, and he was not yet ready to interrogate how thin it might actually prove to be underneath.

The demons who could still fight fell quickly, one after another, and those who could not began breaking for whatever cover the surrounding terrain offered, the disciplined resistance of moments ago collapsing entirely into the desperate, undignified scramble of soldiers moving as though they had already conceded the battle lost. Miguel let them run. There was no honor to be gained chasing down men already broken, and some old, deeply buried part of his training still recognized that distinction even now, even today, even with everything else about him currently fraying at the edges.

## What One Fleeing Demon Felt

The weapon's absence still ached in his empty hand, a phantom weight he had carried for exactly the length of the fight and had now, in the last desperate seconds of it, thrown down somewhere behind him without any clear memory of the moment itself. His lungs burned with every stride, the air tearing raw and hot going in, and he had no thought left in him large enough to hold anything beyond the single, simple fact of the treeline ahead — closer now, close enough that some blind, animal part of him had already started counting the strides it would take to reach it. Nothing beyond that mattered. Not the battle lost behind him, not whatever waited for him after, only the trees, only the reaching them.

He did not see the light come. There was no warning in it at all, no shadow crossing the ground ahead of him, no sound distinct from the general chaos of the rout around him — only the treeline, and the counting of strides, and then nothing.

## The Sword's Will

Miguel felt Solmire hum against his palm as the demons scattered, a low, eager vibration entirely unlike anything the blade had ever done in his hand before, and felt his own arm move without his having decided to move it at all. A beam of pure light lanced out from the sword's edge, ignoring the main body of the retreating force entirely, arcing instead toward the stragglers at the rout's ragged edge — the wounded, the disarmed, the ones already too far gone in flight to represent any threat worth the naming.

*No*, Miguel screamed, somewhere deep inside himself, in the only voice he had left that still belonged entirely to him. His body did not listen. His arm continued its arc, Solmire's light finding each fleeing straggler in turn and erasing them from existence with the same clean, absolute finality it had once reserved only for genuine threats, until the beam finally guttered and died and left nothing behind at all — no bodies, no wreckage, only the abrupt, terrible absence where several dozen fleeing souls had been only seconds before.

## Judgment, Not Justice

Miguel stood amid the aftermath and felt nothing that resembled triumph at all, only a deep, spreading sickness that had nothing to do with exertion and everything to do with what he had just watched his own hand do without his permission. There had been no honor in it. No combat, not in any sense he recognized the word. Only eradication, clean and total and utterly indifferent to whatever those fleeing souls might have still had left to lose.

He understood, standing there, exactly what the laughter in his dream had actually meant. It was not cruelty, not exactly. It was something worse than cruelty. For the sword, and for whatever hand had first shaped it, this had never once been war at all. War implied an opponent worth the name, implied stakes that ran both directions. This was something else entirely — the methodical, untroubled work of clearing vermin from a field, precise and thorough and never once burdened by anything so complicated as an enemy's right to survive the encounter. He had been the instrument of exactly that work today, and had not even understood it was happening until it was already finished.

He thought of one of the disarmed figures he had watched running toward the treeline, nameless to him and now erased entirely, a man already beaten who had counted for nothing at all against a blade that recognized no meaningful distinction between a threat and a target already fleeing. He had told himself, moments ago, that he still recognized the line between combat and slaughter, some old discipline from his training holding firm even now. Solmire, apparently, recognized no such line at all, or recognized it only well enough to erase it deliberately, the moment Miguel's own judgment tried to hold it in place. He did not know which possibility frightened him more.

The soldiers around him had gone quiet too, he realized, several of them watching him with an unease they seemed to be trying to keep from their own faces, as though uncertain what exactly they had just witnessed their commander do or fail to prevent. Miguel offered them nothing by way of explanation. He had none to give that would not require admitting, in front of soldiers he imagined still assumed the weapon answered entirely to him, exactly how little control he currently held over it.

## The Empty Hilt

He returned to the Bastion in silence, and for the first time since Solmire's forging, set the blade aside the moment he crossed his own threshold, unable to bear its weight against his palm a moment longer than the walk back had already forced on him. He did not sheath it carefully. He kicked it, hard, sending it skidding across the stone floor to rest against the far wall, and the sound of the impact rang through the empty room with a clarity that felt, to his own ear, considerably louder than it should have, echoing back to him twice before the silence finally reclaimed the room.

He stood there a long while, staring at the blade from across the room, unwilling yet to cross the distance and retrieve it, the silence of the Bastion pressing in around him with none of the comfort it usually offered after a long day's fighting. He had returned to this same room countless times before, after battles considerably harder fought than today's, and had always found some measure of peace waiting for him within these walls. Tonight the walls offered him nothing of the kind. Touching it now felt like something closer to touching the hand of his own executioner than reaching for a weapon he had once trusted without question. He did not know, standing there in the silence of his own empty quarters, how he was supposed to carry a sword tomorrow morning that he could no longer bear to even look at tonight. He only knew that some line inside himself, long fraying, had finally, completely broken, and that no amount of Gabriel's warnings or his own denials was going to put it back together again.
