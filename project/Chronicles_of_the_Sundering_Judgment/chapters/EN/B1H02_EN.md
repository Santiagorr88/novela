# B1H02 – What Not Even He Can Control

## Ground That Wouldn't Answer

Vem had spent the uneasy pause after first contact doing what he always did in unfamiliar terrain — testing it, quietly and methodically, well away from the standoff's immediate line of sight. Tiltas responded to almost any ground he had ever pointed it at, tilting a plane's foundation just enough to destabilize whatever stood on it, and Vem had built his entire reputation on exactly that reliability. Unstable ground was his native element. He thrived in it the way other soldiers thrived in open, straightforward combat, and some part of him, even amid the general wrongness of wherever this fissure had actually dropped him, had been looking forward to finally putting Tiltas to proper use on terrain this strange.

He aimed the staff at a modest outcropping a short distance from the main standoff, intending nothing more dramatic than a small, controlled tilt to test how the ground here actually responded. Tiltas hummed against his palm the way it always hummed before a working, gathering itself toward the familiar tilt he had performed more times than he could easily count. Nothing happened. He tried again, pushing more deliberately into the working this time, and felt something in the staff's response come back wrong — not absent exactly, but somehow turned back on itself, as though the ground here simply declined to recognize the instruction at all.

He crouched beside the outcropping, running one hand along its surface with the same careful attention he brought to any unfamiliar terrain, searching for whatever quality might explain the working's failure. The stone felt ordinary enough beneath his palm, no different in texture or weight from a hundred other outcroppings he had tilted without incident across his long service. Vem found no property of the stone itself that explained Tiltas's failure. If the cause lay in the ground's own relationship to the working, or somewhere else entirely, he could not yet tell.

He glanced back toward the standoff, still holding at its uneasy distance, and felt a flicker of something almost like excitement cut through his growing unease. A working that failed outright was, in its own strange way, more interesting than a working that simply succeeded the way it always succeeded. He had spent centuries perfecting his command of unstable ground precisely because most terrain, however chaotic, eventually yielded to patient enough pressure. He wanted, badly, to understand exactly what made this ground different before he let the failure simply frustrate him into abandoning the attempt.

## The Working That Turned

He pushed a third time, frustration edging into the working now, demanding more forcefully what Tiltas had always given him without argument. The ground answered this time, but not in any direction he had intended. The outcropping tilted, sharply, toward Vem himself rather than away from him, and he barely managed to throw himself clear before several tons of unstable rock came down exactly where he had been standing a heartbeat earlier.

He lay still for a moment afterward, staring up at the fissure's strange, shifting light, genuinely startled in a way almost nothing about ordinary combat had ever managed to startle him. Tiltas had never once turned against him like that, not in the entirety of his long service. He picked himself up slowly, brushing debris from his shoulder, and studied the staff in his own hand as though it might offer some explanation for what had just happened. It offered him nothing. To Vem, the ground's response suggested rules his own gift had no working knowledge of, though he could not yet tell what, if anything, actually governed them.

His squad had scattered at the sound of the collapse, several of them calling out to confirm he was still standing, and Vem waved off their concern with more confidence than he currently felt, unwilling to let the moment's genuine shock show plainly enough that his own soldiers might start doubting his judgment this early into whatever this assignment had actually become. He had built his reputation, in part, on the particular ease with which he absorbed setbacks that would have rattled a more cautious commander. He intended to keep that reputation intact for as long as he reasonably could, even now, even here.

He tested the staff once more on a smaller, less consequential patch of ground a short distance away, wanting some confirmation that the failure had been specific to that one outcropping rather than a sign of something wrong with Tiltas itself. The smaller working answered normally, the ground shifting exactly as much as he asked it to shift, and Vem felt a measure of relief at that small, functional confirmation even as the larger mystery remained entirely unresolved. Tiltas still worked here, at least on that smaller patch. Why the two attempts had produced such different results remained entirely unclear.

## The Second Wrongness

He gathered his squad back toward the relative safety of more stable-looking ground, unwilling to test the terrain further until he understood it better, and it was there, regrouping, that the second wrongness revealed itself. One of his own soldiers, a squad member named Orwick who had fought at Vem's side across more assignments than either of them had bothered counting, went suddenly, completely rigid, eyes wide and unseeing, as though something had reached directly into him and seized hold of whatever lay beneath the reach of any ordinary weapon.

Vem crossed the distance quickly, checking Orwick's body for the kind of visible wound that would explain the reaction, and found nothing — no blood, no injury, nothing a blade or a spell could account for. Orwick's flesh remained entirely untouched. Whatever was happening to him, it looked to Vem like something happening somewhere his own eyes simply could not follow, well beyond anything a body alone could show him.

He called Orwick's name twice, sharply, the way he might have called to snap any soldier out of a battlefield daze, and received no response at all, not even the small, automatic flinch most demons produced reflexively when their own name was spoken with that much urgency, not even the faintest shift of his weight from one foot to the other. Orwick's eyes remained fixed on whatever nothing they had settled on, unblinking in a way that struck Vem, watching it, as considerably more disturbing than any wound he had ever seen a soldier take in open combat. Wounds, at least, had a shape he understood. This had none.

He gestured the rest of his squad back a further step, some old instinct warning him that the same wrongness might appear in the nearest soldier next if given the chance, though he had no actual evidence yet that proximity mattered to whatever was happening. Better caution unearned than the alternative. He had learned that lesson early in his service and had never once needed to relearn it since, however much his own temperament otherwise leaned toward embracing chaos rather than guarding carefully against it.

## Orwick

Orwick's rigid stillness broke, eventually, into something worse than stillness — a low, continuous sound that was not quite screaming, his eyes still fixed on nothing Vem could see, his body present and functioning while something about him that Vem had no better word for than presence seemed, watching him, to be steadily going out of focus, though nothing Vem could point to explained the impression. Vem had watched soldiers die in more ways than he cared to count across his long service. He had never once watched anything that looked like this, a body remaining stubbornly, horribly intact while Orwick himself seemed, in some way Vem could neither identify nor explain, less recognizably present.

He tried, uselessly, to think of some working Tiltas could offer against whatever was happening to Orwick, something with no physical ground he could actually identify to disrupt. He raised the staff anyway, more out of desperation than any real conviction it would help, and directed the smallest working he knew how to produce at the ground directly beneath Orwick, some half-formed hope that destabilizing the immediate terrain might somehow reach whatever was actually happening to him. The ground shifted obediently, exactly as commanded. Orwick's condition did not change at all. The working's success beneath him gave Vem no reason to think the ground was the source of what he was seeing, though it proved nothing beyond Tiltas's failure to change it, and Vem understood, watching the working accomplish precisely nothing against the actual threat, exactly how limited his own considerable gift actually was outside the narrow territory it had always governed.

Nothing in his own gift's considerable range offered him anything close to an answer. He had never once, in the entirety of his existence, faced a threat his own abilities had absolutely no purchase against, and the particular, total helplessness of that realization settled into him with a weight he had no ready name for.

## What Vem Had Never Felt Before

He recognized the sensation gradually, with a kind of horrified clarity he found almost as unsettling as the sensation itself. He knew it only by what others had always called it. Fear. He had never once felt it before. Not genuine fear, not in any form. He had built his entire reputation on thriving in exactly the kind of chaos that unsettled other soldiers, had made a private, personal delight of collapsing structures and destabilized ground that most of his own kind found merely functional rather than genuinely enjoyable. Nothing about his temperament had ever included room for this — a threat he could not tilt, could not destabilize, could not touch with anything his own long service had ever equipped him to use.

He tried, briefly, to laugh the feeling off the way he laughed off almost everything else that unsettled lesser soldiers, some old habit reaching for levity even now. The laugh would not come. He found, reaching for the easy humor that had carried him through centuries of chaos most demons found genuinely distressing, only a hollow, unconvincing echo of it, doing nothing at all to change what was currently happening to a soldier who had served at his side long enough to have earned better than this. The realization that even his own humor had abandoned him here frightened him nearly as much as Orwick's condition itself.

He thought, standing there, of every other assignment across his long service where chaos had simply been raw material to shape, ground to tilt, structures to collapse into something more interesting than they had been before. He had never once needed courage for any of that, only appetite. This asked something else of him entirely, some quality he had never needed to cultivate because he had never once faced a threat his own considerable gift could not eventually turn to his advantage. He did not currently possess whatever that quality actually was. He understood, standing over Orwick, that he needed to find it, or find someone who already had it, before Orwick worsened further — or, for all Vem knew, before the same wrongness appeared in someone else.

He stood over Orwick, still seeming to fade in some way Vem could only half-perceive, and understood, with a clarity that frightened him considerably more than the threat itself, that what he had witnessed in this fissure followed no principles he could recognize or knew how to fight.

## The Hardest Admission

He turned to the nearest soldier still capable of taking an order, a younger demon named Fessa who had served directly under him for only a handful of assignments so far, and found the words considerably harder to produce than he expected, his usual easy fluency abandoning him entirely at the exact moment he needed it most. He started once and stopped, the sentence catching somewhere it had never once caught for him before, and forced it out slowly on the second attempt. "I need... a second opinion. From anyone. Now."

Fessa did not comment on how strange the request sounded coming from him, whatever she might privately have made of it. She simply nodded, a quick, wordless nod that suggested she understood at least something of what the admission had cost her commander to make, and turned to go find whatever help remained within reach.

Vem watched her go, still standing over Orwick, whatever was happening to him still visibly ongoing, and understood, watching her disappear into the fissure's strange light, that he had just crossed a line in his own long service he had never once expected to cross. He had commanded soldiers across centuries of exactly this kind of chaos without once needing to ask anyone for help solving it. Today, apparently, was going to be the exception, and he found himself unable to summon the particular resentment he might once have expected to feel at admitting it. There was no room left in him for pride, not standing here, not with whatever was happening to Orwick still unresolved in front of him with no end yet in sight.

He needed help. He had no idea, standing there, how long it would actually take to arrive, or whether it would arrive in time to matter at all. He held his position beside Orwick regardless, unwilling to leave his soldier's side even with nothing left in his own power that could actually help him, and waited there, watching the strange light shift overhead in its slow, uneven pulses, for whatever answer Fessa might eventually manage to bring back.
