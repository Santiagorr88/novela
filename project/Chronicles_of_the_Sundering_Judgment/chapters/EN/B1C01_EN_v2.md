# B1C01 — The Tree Outside Time

The march across Serephis was an exercise in unraveling. Not of the body, though the heat fused sand into sheets of black glass beneath his sabatons and the sky hung the color of a healing bruise, pulsing with stars that beat out of rhythm with the world. This was an unraveling of the soul. Miguel, Supreme Commander of the Celestial Host, knew the clean pain of battle — the bite of demonic steel, the searing kiss of infernal fire. Those were honest wounds, wounds with edges you could name and close. This was different. This was a hollow ache lodged in the center of his being, a space where something had been taken, leaving behind not a scar but a relentless, directional hunger. His gauntleted hand went to his chest by instinct, a gesture he had repeated a thousand times since the ache began. He was fundamentally incomplete, and the question that drove him across the blistering wastes was a constant, silent whisper: *what was taken from me?*

He did not think of himself as a man chasing a weapon. He told himself he was chasing an answer — that the ache was a wound from some battle scrubbed from his memory, and that once he understood it, he could return to his life whole. He did not let himself examine how thin that hope had worn. For days the ache had been a dull, tidal throb, easy enough to bury beneath duty. Now it pulsed with a clear, directional authority, a compass needle dragging him forward whether he willed it or not. It reminded him, cruelly, of the clarity of the Divine Word — the way true command settled into the soul as unarguable fact. This new call wore the same certainty. Its origin terrified him. He stopped, grit crunching under his boots, and closed his eyes, hope and dread arm in arm. When he opened them, he had already turned toward the pull, a body surrendering before the mind agreed to it.

The oppressive stillness broke on a chord of pure harmony. Gabriel had arrived, and his presence was a physical relief — a sudden coolness on sun-scorched skin, the smell of rain on stone cutting through the desert's metallic tang. Soft light bloomed around his brother and pushed the gloom back a pace. Miguel felt love, sharp enough to hurt, and close behind it, shame. He did not want Gabriel — the Herald of the Divine Voice, keeper of a thousand tongues — to see him like this: unraveled, hunting a phantom ache across a cursed waste. He straightened, a futile attempt at a commander's bearing, but the weariness was carved too deep to hide. Gabriel's face held no judgment. It held something worse: a raw, aching worry.

"The Council is concerned, Miguel." Gabriel's voice carried that same first harmony, but sharpened now, pleading at the edges. "They fear this is a deception — a lure the enemy crafted to draw you out. Your absence puts fear in the Host."

The words — *Council, duty, fear* — arrived like echoes from another life, irrelevant against the hole in his chest. Gabriel's light seemed to probe the wound, and it flared with fresh heat. Miguel took a half-step back, an arm crossing instinctively over his ribs, as if guarding against a blow already thrown.

"They think me a fool," Miguel said, his voice rough with disuse. "Or a traitor."

"They think you are their commander. Lost, and in pain." Gabriel's voice gentled. "They think of their brother."

The shift — envoy to brother — landed harder than any order could have. The air between them thinned to something intimate, the cosmic stakes forgotten in the held breath of shared history. Miguel was thrown back to the ruin of the Onyx Gates, his wing shattered, his spirit nearly spent, and Gabriel there beside him — not with an archangel's grand power, but with a brother's quiet, patient care. His jaw, clenched for days, loosened for one unbearable second. Gabriel's gaze softened; his hand rose, hesitated, and fell back to his side, the gesture unfinished, the history between them heavier for it.

"Come home," Gabriel said. "Whatever this wound is, we face it together. As we always have."

For a moment Miguel wavered. The thought of the Bastion, of home's familiar harmonies, sang like a promise he had almost forgotten how to want. Then came the colder thought beneath it: to return would be a living death, an eternity carrying this void as a permanent reminder of his own incompletion. The Council would offer comfort, not answers. They would try to heal a wound they could not even see. He had to go forward. He had to *know*.

"I cannot." He met his brother's eyes for the first time since Gabriel had arrived — his own gaze no longer weary, but lit with a terrible, crystalline resolve. "This call is a command more fundamental than any the Council can give me. It is a truth I must find, or I am nothing." He turned, angling his body toward the pull. The choice had already made itself. He was alone now.

Gabriel's light dimmed a fraction, harmony faltering into something like a held note that would not resolve. He offered no further argument. His message was delivered; his plea, spent. A single tear of pure light traced his cheek, and he inclined his head, sorrow written plain across features that rarely showed it. "May you find what you seek, brother." His voice had thickened with a grief that felt older than either of them. Then he was gone, folded back into the space between worlds.

The heat and hush of Serephis crashed back in, heavier for the silence Gabriel left behind. The memory of his brother's sorrowful eyes would follow him now, a fresh wound layered atop the old one. Miguel let the regret surface once, fully, and burn away — replaced by the cold, clean feeling of a severed anchor. The choice was made. He turned and marched on.

He walked an age through unchanging desert, until, without warning, it changed. The transition struck like a physical blow: one step on hot, grating sand, the next on cool, yielding moss. The oppressive air gave way to a still coolness that carried a metallic tang, like the moment after lightning. He had reached the grove. Trees with leaves of pale, leaf-thin metal rose in ranks around him, casting a bright, even light that fell on nothing — no shadows anywhere, a flat and depthless world. His tactical mind screamed *ambush*. Nothing in creation was made this perfectly; it felt manufactured, a lure dressed as a sanctuary. His hand drifted to the space where a sword hilt should have waited, an old habit that now only measured his own vulnerability. Beautiful as it was, this place held the answer he had come for, and he would not let its perfection deceive him.

The quiet here was not an absence but a presence — a heavy, listening quality that made him feel watched, an intruder in a place sacred but not holy. Wet stone. Something like the air after a lightning strike. No breeze stirred the leaves; the temperature never wavered. He tried to recite a verse of the Divine Song, the centering exercise he had used before a thousand battles, and found the words dead in his mouth, swallowed by a stillness too vast to argue with. Every soft crunch of fallen leaf beneath his boots felt like a shout inside a library. He was being measured. He did not yet know for what.

At the grove's heart stood a great ash, bark the color of cooled lava, roots drinking from a pool of starlight so pure it looked poured rather than reflected. Sunk into the trunk was a sword — not forged metal, but living, solidified light. The instant he saw it, the ache in his chest went silent, the void filled by something that pulsed in the same rhythm as his own hollow wound. A name surfaced in his mind unbidden, not remembered so much as *rediscovered*: *Solmire.* It felt less like learning a word and more like watching his own name get quietly overwritten. His steps, cautious a moment before, now pulled him forward almost against his will. This was not the thought *that is a sword*. It was recognition, absolute and terrifying: *that is what was taken from me.*

He stood before the ash, hand trembling as he raised it. The blade's presence pressed on him like an older, more fundamental truth — one that made the name *Miguel* feel, for a heartbeat, like a title someone else had given him to wear. A low hum built behind his eyes, not sound but vibration, resonant with the hollow space beneath his ribs. His fingers hovered inches from the hilt, caught between hunger and dread. If he touched this, would he still be himself afterward? Or only an extension of the blade's will?

The moment skin met hilt, the grove vanished.

He was cast from his own body into a place of cosmic, amoral chaos. Supernovas were quenched like hot steel plunged into a bucket of dark. Gravity swung as a hammer, not a law — a tool for shaping, not restraining. Somewhere, a star-sized hammer rang against an anvil made from a collapsed sun, and the note of it built and unmade galaxies in the same breath. His mind reached for words like *good* or *evil* and found them laughably small against what unfolded before him. He was a mote of dust, witness to something no soul was ever meant to see.

Threaded through all of it was laughter. Jubilant, irreverent, and utterly without concern for consequence — the most frightening sound Miguel had ever heard, because it held no cruelty in it at all, only indifference to every category he had built his existence upon. This blade had not been forged for a cause. It was neither holy relic nor demonic weapon. It had been made, he understood with soul-deep certainty, for the sheer anarchic joy of making it — a cosmic joke, and he had just become its punchline. The vision closed on one final, triumphant peal of laughter as the sword took its last shape, and he was thrown back into his body gasping, his heart hammering with a new species of fear: not damnation, but meaninglessness.

He stood before the sword, the maker's laughter still echoing somewhere behind his sternum. The questions — origin, purpose, maker — did not resolve. They simply stopped mattering. There was only the sword's truth now, and it was enough. He closed his hand around the hilt, no longer hesitant, and drew Solmire from the heart of the ash. The old tree groaned — a sound of deep, ancient mourning — and the grove's light dimmed in sympathy, the air turning unnaturally still and cold. Power flooded him: not warm, not holy, but clean and final. It sealed the wound in his chest, not with peace but with placid, terrible certainty. His weariness vanished, replaced by an unnerving perfection of posture. He was whole. He was also, in some way he could not yet name, hollowed out.

The silence returned changed — no longer heavy and listening, but sterile, swept clean. Somewhere beyond the dying grove, a pulse began to gather, patient as a bell about to be struck at the center of reality itself. He felt it less as an external force than as an extension of his own new voice — a voice the world, whether it wished to or not, would now have to hear. He lowered the blade slowly, weighing its strange, perfect balance in his palm, his face settling into a calm mask, his eyes already fixed on wars not yet fought.

---

Far from the dying grove, in a quiet chamber carved from shadow and old ambition, Lucifer set down the delicate clockwork instrument he had been adjusting. He tilted his head, listening to something no wall could have carried. His expression held no alarm — only pure, unhurried curiosity. He knew this signal. Not as a threat, not as a challenge, but as the echo of a song he had not heard sung in eons, laughter woven so deeply into its structure that no divine hand could have put it there.

A slow, genuine smile spread across his face.

"So," he murmured to the empty room, resuming his work on the little machine as though nothing at all had happened. "One of the old songs is being sung again. How interesting."

Back in the grove, the pulse subsided, leaving Miguel in a hush so complete it felt final. Nothing about him — nothing about the world he was walking back into — would be the same again.
