# B2C32 – A Song of Wrath and Reason

The words left Mikel before he'd finished forming the thought behind them, sharp and certain in a way his ordinary lecturing voice had never once managed to be. "Arin! Left flank, three seconds!" He hadn't consciously counted the seconds. The number had simply arrived, already certain, the way a familiar phone number arrives without effort once a hand has dialed it a thousand times before. He didn't stop to question it. There wasn't room to.

He heard the authority in his own voice a half-beat after he'd already spoken, some part of him standing outside the moment long enough to register how strange it was to hear himself sound like that — not a warning shouted in panic, but an order given by someone who expected, without needing to check, that it would be obeyed. Nine years of lecture halls had taught him how to project his voice across a room full of half-listening undergraduates. This was nothing like that. This was a voice that assumed compliance the way a man assumes the ground will hold his weight, without once considering the alternative. He didn't know where it had come from.

He didn't have time to examine the strangeness further. Another shape was already pressing in from his right, and his borrowed length of shelving rose to meet it before his mind had finished deciding to move. He caught the demon's own strike on the flat of the metal, felt the impact travel up through his wrist and shoulder, and shoved back hard enough to buy himself the half-step of room he needed to reset his footing, his breath already coming ragged.

---

Arin's body answered the order before his conscious mind had finished parsing it, pivoting hard to his left a full half-second before he'd have registered the flanking threat on his own. His forearm caught the demon's blade at the wrist, turning the strike wide, and his other hand drove up under its jaw with enough force to end the question of whether it would trouble either of them again. The demon crumpled, and Arin was already moving past it toward the next threat before its weight had finished settling to the floor, one more body added to a tally he'd stopped bothering to keep track of several exchanges ago. His shoulder ached where a blade had grazed him earlier, and he pushed the sensation aside the same way he pushed aside every ache that wasn't currently trying to kill him.

He didn't ask himself, in the moment, why he'd trusted a stranger's shouted order with his own life. There hadn't been time to ask, and some part of him understood, distantly, that asking would have cost him the half-second the trust had actually bought. A decade of contracts had taught him never to hand his own safety to someone else's judgment without cause. He'd handed it over anyway, without once deciding to, and it had been exactly right. He didn't have a name yet for what that meant about the man fighting at his back.

He caught his balance and glanced back, just once, toward the man whose voice had just saved him. Mikel Ardon had looked, minutes ago, like exactly what his file said he was — a soft-handed academic in over his head. He didn't look like that now. He was already tracking his next warning, eyes narrowed on the shifting formation ahead of him with the same cold focus Arin had spent a decade training into himself, and Arin found the sight of it considerably more unsettling than the demons still pressing in around them.

---

"You always give orders like that," Arin called over the clash of a fresh exchange, driving a demon back with three short blows, something close to genuine amusement threading through the danger in his voice, "or is this new?"

"No idea," Mikel shouted back, not breaking his own rhythm, already tracking the shift in the enemy line with the same cold clarity that had let him call the last warning in time. "But it's working. So shut up and flank." He drove the shelving fragment into a demon's exposed side without breaking stride, the motion no more deliberate than any other word in the sentence.

"Bossy for a professor," Arin said, already moving to cover the angle Mikel had called.

"Ask me about it later. Assuming there's a later." Mikel didn't have breath left to spare on anything cleverer than that, and found he didn't especially mind.

---

Arin actually laughed at that, short and startled out of him, before the next demon's approach swallowed the sound whole. It struck him, distantly, as an absurd thing to be doing in the middle of an ambush that was still very much trying to kill them both. He couldn't remember the last time a fight had left him room to laugh at all. He set the thought aside and turned back to the next blade already swinging toward him.

---

Something shifted between them a few exchanges later, subtle at first and then unmistakable, a synchrony that went past anything two strangers fighting well together should have been capable of producing. Mikel felt it as a kind of certainty settling into his own instincts, calling out angles before he'd consciously identified them, each warning landing in Arin's ear at the precise instant it would do the most good. It wasn't luck. It felt, if anything, like remembering a rhythm he'd danced to a thousand times before, in a life he had no memory of actually living. He called a fourth warning, then a fifth, each one arriving with less effort than the last, as though the calling itself had stopped requiring any conscious thought at all. Neither man led and neither man followed. They simply moved as though they had always been meant to move this way, together, and the demons in front of them began, for the first time since the fight had started, to visibly falter.

---

Arin felt it too, on his own side of the fight, his strikes finding gaps that opened a half-second before he swung toward them, as though something were clearing his path before he'd chosen it himself. He had fought beside capable partners before, across a decade of careful contracts. None of them had ever felt like this — two separate rhythms locking, without effort, into a single shared one. His body moved faster than it had any right to move this deep into a fight this outmatched, each strike landing exactly where the last one had opened space for the next, formation after formation fracturing in front of him under a pressure the soldiers' training had never once prepared them to answer.

For a handful of heartbeats, something passed between them that neither man could have named, and the air in the corridor seemed to hold something it hadn't held a moment before — a stillness underneath all the violence, taut and expectant, the way air goes taut before a held note finally resolves. Arin had no name for it either. He only knew it didn't last.

---

The enforcer, watching from his careful distance with the same unhurried patience he'd carried since the portal first opened, finally stepped forward himself, and the fight changed the instant he did.

Mikel felt the difference immediately. This wasn't another soldier added to an already desperate count. The enforcer moved through the corridor's chaos without wasting a single motion, closing the distance in three unhurried strides that somehow covered more ground than they should have, and where he moved, soldiers reorganized around him with an obedience that told Mikel plainly this was the true engine driving this ambush, not merely another blade among many. He carried no visible weapon that Mikel could identify, and somehow that absence unsettled him more than any blade would have. His eyes, when they finally settled on Mikel directly, carried the same flat, appraising patience he'd worn since he first stepped through the fire.

---

Arin took a hit he shouldn't have taken, distracted for half a second by the enforcer's sudden entry into the fight, and felt the wrongness of it in his own ribs before he'd finished processing where the blow had come from. He fell back a full step, breath punched half out of him, and forced himself upright again before the next strike could find him while he was down. A second blow caught his already-grazed shoulder a moment later, harder this time, and the pain of it registered clearly enough to slow him for a fraction of a second he couldn't afford to lose. Whatever had carried him and Mikel through the last several exchanges strained under the enforcer's weight, and the remaining soldiers pressed the advantage the instant they felt it strain.

---

The demons still standing had been a desperate fight on their own. The enforcer added to their number was closer to an ending. Mikel felt his own borrowed rhythm faltering, the warnings coming slower now, less certain, the strange effortless clarity of a few minutes ago already receding.

---

Mikel felt himself losing ground, the shelving fragment in his hand deflecting less than it had a moment ago, his breath running short. His arms had started to burn somewhere in the last several exchanges, a deep, unfamiliar fatigue settling into muscles that had never once been asked to work this hard, and his vision had begun narrowing at the edges. A cut he hadn't noticed taking stung along one forearm, blood already drying tacky against his skin. Whatever borrowed skill had carried him this far, his own untrained body simply didn't have the conditioning left to sustain it. He caught Arin's eye for a half-second across the narrowing space between them, and saw, in that single unguarded glance, the same understanding he'd already reached himself. This was not a fight either of them was currently winning.

---

Arin fared no better. Two soldiers pressed him back toward the corridor's collapsed shelving, forcing him into a defensive rhythm he had no room left to break out of, blocking and retreating a half-step at a time with nothing left over to spend on offense. His arms had gone heavy in a way he recognized from harder contracts than this one, the specific fatigue of a body running past whatever reserve it had started the night with. The enforcer closed the last of the distance himself, entirely unbothered by the chaos he was walking into.

He raised one hand, the same economical gesture he'd used to send his soldiers forward in the first place.

---

Mikel watched the hand rise and understood, plainly, exactly what it meant. In that final instant, with no time left to do anything about it, he felt something settle into him that had nothing to do with fear and nothing to do with the fight itself — a plain, wordless certainty that he was not, in this exact moment, entirely alone inside his own body. It wasn't a voice. It wasn't a presence he could have described to anyone who asked him to try. It was simply a sense, absolute and inexplicable, and for the first time since the dreams had started, months ago, he felt it without any of the fear that usually came bundled with them.

He had no evidence for the feeling beyond the feeling itself. He trusted it anyway, in the same unquestioning way his own hands had trusted the shelving fragment a few minutes ago, without needing a reason he could have explained out loud. He didn't try to put any of it into words, not with Arin still fighting at his back and no breath left over to spare on an explanation he didn't have anyway. Whatever it was, it didn't ask to be understood. It only asked to be believed, for exactly as long as this moment demanded believing it.

He didn't understand it. He didn't have time to understand it. He only knew, in the half-second before the enforcer's hand finished its arc, that he was no longer entirely certain how this moment was going to end, and that the uncertainty, strangely, felt less like fear now than it had a moment before. He held onto that instead of the fear, and waited to see which one turned out to be right.
