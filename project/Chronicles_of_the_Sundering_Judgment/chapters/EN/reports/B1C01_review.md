## Required fixes (blocking)

1. **[Pacing + Style + Codex — 3 independent reviewers]** The chapter's final line ("Back in the grove, the pulse subsided...") returns to Miguel's POV after the Lucifer scene without a scene-break marker, diluting the hook the beat plan calls for ("ends on his smile, leaving the reader to wonder what he knows"). Also an unmarked POV shift.
   - Fix: cut the chapter on Lucifer's line, or move the closing Miguel line before the scene break to Lucifer.

2. **[Pacing]** Word count is 2,145 — 455 below the 2,600–3,600 target. The "A Maker's Laugh" beat (highest narrative weight — major foreshadowing, the sword's amoral origin) gets only 238 words, on par with minor beats.
   - Fix: expand that beat by 150–250 words with more sensory specificity (smell of the forge, sound of hammer/anvil per the original beat description). Codex independently confirms this passage already "earns its space," so expansion should read as depth, not padding.

3. **[Style + Codex — 2 independent reviewers]** The ozone/petrichor/lightning image repeats near-verbatim three times in close proximity (Gabriel's arrival, the grove's edge, inside the grove).
   - Fix: keep one instance, replace the other two with a distinct sensory anchor.

## Suggested improvements (non-blocking)

- Gabriel's opening line ("The Council is concerned, Miguel...") reads as as-you-know exposition. Dialogue reviewer's suggested rewrite: *"The Council sent me to bring you back. They have a word for what's happening to you. I don't like the word."*
- Lucifer's single line is tonally correct (charismatic, unhurried) but reads slightly generic ("intrigued villain") relative to his "ruthlessly manipulative" trait — consider a possessive/calculating edge.
- Codex flag: "his hand drifted to the space where a sword hilt should have waited" creates unintended ambiguity (was he unarmed before Solmire, or is this specific to Solmire's absence?) — worth one clarifying phrase.
- Codex flag: Miguel's shift from horror at the vision to "the questions stopped mattering" happens fast — could use one more concrete signal that his will is being overridden, not just persuaded.

## What worked

- Miguel's POV interiority and the Gabriel dialogue (subtext + micro-actions) — all 4 Claude reviewers and Codex independently praised this.
- The forge vision is the best-earned passage in the chapter (thematically coherent with Ereloth, not arbitrary).
- No canon contradictions. Ereloth reveal pacing respected — the forging vision matches the pre-approved framing in his profile ("do not retcon it into a grievance or a scheme") and stays within Revelation Pacing's Book I "hints" allowance.
