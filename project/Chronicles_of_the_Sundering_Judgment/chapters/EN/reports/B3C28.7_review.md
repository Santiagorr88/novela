## Chapter Review Verdict — B3C28.7 "Two Ways to Ruin a Man" (POV Naamah)

**Result:** PASS WITH FIXES (all required fixes applied before finalization)

Panel: 4 Claude subagent reviewers (Continuity & Canon, Style & Voice, Pacing & Structure, Dialogue & Character Voice) + 1 independent Codex read (`codex exec -s read-only`, no canon context). Word count at first draft: 2,274. Final word count after fixes: 2,268. Target range 2,250-3,000 — PASS.

### Required fixes (blocking) — all applied

- **[Continuity/Craft, Codex]** Genuine temporal-clarity bug: the closing paragraph originally read "Two captains had left her hall **tonight**..." immediately after a scene break that had already jumped forward **eleven days**. Caught independently by the Codex read ("a temporal inconsistency... unless the narrative deliberately returns to the earlier evening, 'tonight' is incorrect or disorienting"). **Fix**: restructured the final paragraph to explicitly bridge both time points ("not that night, watching two captains leave her hall... and not now, eleven days further into the cost neither of them had thought to ask what the other was paying"), removing the ambiguous "tonight."
- **[Style]** The construction "the [adj.] X of a [noun] who Y" appeared three times in this chapter (Phenex's entrance, Leraje's "professional habit," and a near-duplicate of B3C28.6's Ronove sentence applied to Phenex — flagged as a cross-chapter repeat by both Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4). **Fix**: all three rewritten without the scaffold.
- **[Style]** POV slip: "because he was the sort of guest for whom a plain audience chamber would have felt like an insult" stated Phenex's interior standard as narrative fact rather than Naamah's inference. **Fix**: reframed as "because she judged him exactly the sort of guest who'd have taken a plain chamber as an insult." A second, similar ungrounded read at "something shifted behind his theatrical composure" was anchored the same way ("Naamah judged").
- **[Style]** Hard-wrap thesis ending: the chapter closed on an aphorism ("the trick was never making people believe a lie... exactly enough of the truth to make agreeing feel like their own idea") that restated an insight the prose had already delivered twice earlier in the same scene — flagged by Reviewer 2 as a hard wrap rather than a pivot/cost/question, and independently by Codex as "the third statement of the same conclusion." **Fix**: cut the restated aphorism; the paragraph now closes on the unresolved cost to Verrow's Hold instead (see the temporal-bug fix above, which folds into the same rewrite).

### Suggested improvements (non-blocking) — applied

- **[Craft, Codex]** Leraje's claim that a Mors arrow could look like natural death was under-specified for a reader with no lore access. **Fix**: added one clarifying clause on the toxin's slow, wound-closing mechanism.
- **[Craft, Codex]** Thessaly Vane was entirely abstract — a name and a statistic, so her death read as proof of Leraje's competence rather than an actual loss. **Fix**: added one grounding detail to the opening report (she walks her own recruits to the front personally, a cheap, rare gesture) to give the target one concrete, human trait before she's removed.
- **[Style, minor]** "Unhurried" repeated across a near-identical sentence template shared with B3C28.6. **Fix**: cut both instances in this chapter (0 remaining).

### Suggested improvements (not applied — judgment call)

- Codex proposed either ending the chapter immediately after "The canvas is primed. Go make it art." or continuing far enough to show Phenex's method and Verrow's Hold's actual fate, arguing the chapter "ends on an explanation of Naamah's gift rather than a demonstration of it." Not applied: Reviewer 3 (Pacing & Structure) confirmed all planned beats — including the deliberate cost-visible-to-reader-not-characters coda — are present, correctly weighted, and land the chapter inside the target range; showing Phenex's actual campaign belongs to a future chapter's beat plan, not a scope expansion of this interlude.
- Codex flagged the late cluster of Leviathan/Balam/Foras/Belial names as under-explained for a lore-blind reader. Not trimmed: Reviewer 1 (Continuity) confirmed the reference is chronologically accurate ("Belial's crumbling court," not his fall/death, which hasn't happened yet at this point in the timeline) and this level of background political texture without full explanation is already the established convention from B3C28.5.

### What worked

- Reviewer 1 (Continuity) confirmed no contradictions: weapons, ranks, and personalities for Naamah, Phenex, and Leraje all match `personajes.md`; the "Belial's crumbling court" reference checked against the chapter sequence and found chronologically sound (his fall/death is still in the future at this point in the timeline).
- Reviewer 4 (Dialogue) called the Phenex/Leraje friction "the strongest scene in either chapter" — genuinely opposed values in opposed cadences, no voice overlap.
- Reviewer 3 (Pacing & Structure) confirmed all beats present in order, including beat 3b (Leraje questioning the target) handled as a genuinely partial reveal rather than an under-delivered one, and the reputation-building motive in the coda landing as "a clean, exact match to the intended cost-visible-to-reader-not-characters beat."
- Naamah's established honesty mechanic (shape perception, never state falsehood) verified consistent with B3C28.5 by both Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 4.
- Codex's independent read praised Leraje's closing line ("I just get to go first") as ending the argument "with minimal fuss" and fitting her personality, and called the dialogue overall "enjoyable and often sharp."
