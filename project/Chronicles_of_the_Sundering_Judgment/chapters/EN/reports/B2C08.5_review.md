## Reviewer 5 — Codex: Independent Read (informe completo, sin editar)

> ## Clarity and outside-context problems
>
> The broad situation is clear: Hell and Heaven are at war across a destabilized border, Balam captures an angelic sentinel, and Barbas cannot extract her secret.
>
> Some specifics are unnecessarily vague: "She was guarding something." There is no concrete evidence presented that she was guarding anything... Balam's conviction therefore feels arbitrary unless he has observed something the reader has not been told.
>
> The stakes are also underdefined... "Contested line, three days out" supplies location-like information without emotional or strategic consequence.
>
> The opening's unstable geography is evocative but indistinct... Balam's thought implies agency or metamorphosis, but nothing later uses that idea.
>
> The three faces are understandable as a physical trait, but their mechanics remain fuzzy... One concrete physical action would anchor what otherwise feels like character-sheet information.
>
> ## The prisoner is too abstract
>
> The sentinel is the engine of the entire chapter, but she receives virtually no distinguishing feature—not a name, physical detail, spoken word, purposeful gesture, or fragment of the foundational belief Barbas finds. She becomes "the unbreakable object" rather than a person. [...] Even a partial image—a promise at a bedside, a remembered hand, a mundane duty—would make her resistance emotionally meaningful without revealing what she guards.
>
> ## POV voices
>
> Balam and Barbas are distinct as personalities, but not strongly distinct as narrative voices. Balam's sections should feel faster and harder, yet they use the same long, qualified, recursive sentence structure as Barbas's. [...] There is also frequent overexplanation after dialogue.
>
> ## Overwritten or purple sentences
>
> No sentence is incomprehensible, but several strain under too many abstractions and qualifications. [Cites the "Three faces turned..." sentence, the "Beneath it, though..." sentence, the "What he read instead..." sentence, and the closing paragraph's final long sentence.]
>
> ## Does the ending land?
>
> The admission—Barbas needs another night—lands. The material after it slightly fizzles. [...] A stronger endpoint might be Barbas consciously lying about tomorrow, recognizing that he no longer wants to break her, discovering one concrete impossible thing in her memory, or deciding not to tell Balam what her resistance means.
>
> **Overall verdict: needs targeted fixes—mainly aggressive compression, a concrete basis for the prisoner's resistance and importance, and a sharper final turn.**

## Required fixes (blocking) — all applied

1. **[Style] POV discipline break in the closing section** — the section from "Balam found him in the corridor..." to the end is anchored in Barbas, but two lines narrated Balam's inner state as direct narrator fact ("Balam's temper flared... the way it always did when a plan met a delay it hadn't budgeted for"; "already restless for the ground his patrol still owed him") instead of as Barbas's read of him. **Fix applied**: both reframed explicitly as Barbas's observation/inference ("Barbas watched the temper flare... the same flash he'd learned... to expect"; "the same restless half-turn Barbas had watched him make a hundred times before").
2. **[Craft, from Codex] The prisoner is too abstract** — zero distinguishing physical/emotional anchor for the character the whole chapter turns on. **Fix applied**: added a physical detail at capture (armor that "still fit like it had been issued rather than earned"), a bound-posture detail at the start of the interrogation (knees drawn up, "some old instinct for running still not caught up"), and — without leaking what she's guarding, which stays reserved per the arc's own reveal-pacing — one concrete grounding image for the "foundation" Dolorim finds ("a single ordinary promise," and the worn-stone simile for how long she's held it).
3. **[Craft, from Codex] Overloaded/purple sentences** — 4 specific sentences flagged (opening "three faces" sentence, "Beneath it, though—and this was the part..." sentence, "What he read instead..." sentence, closing-paragraph run-on). **Fix applied**: all 4 rewritten shorter and declarative; verified no sentence now stacks more than one figurative comparison.
4. **[Craft, from Codex] Balam's POV voice too recursive, insufficiently distinct from Barbas's** — Balam is established as impatient/blunt but his sections used the same long, self-qualifying sentence structure as Barbas's patient-craftsman cadence. **Fix applied**: the "watched Barbas work exactly once" paragraph split into shorter, more clipped sentences matching Balam's fiche ("Enraged, intense, forever testing limits").

## Suggested improvements (non-blocking) — applied anyway

- **Verbal tic**: "never once" appeared 15 times in ~2,800 words. Reduced to 5 — kept the 3 that form a deliberate dialogue echo (Barbas → Balam → Barbas, "never once complained... never once needed a second night... I've never once needed a second night") and the 2 that land at structural pivots (the expectation-before-the-anomaly beat; Barbas's own private doubt beat). Rewrote the other 10 with varied constructions.
- **Two flat section closings**: the guard's section and the "withdrew the needle" section both ended on plain summary rather than pivot/cost/question. Both rewritten to end on unresolved tension (the guard's ongoing dread; Barbas's temptation to shade his report).
- **Barbas's "delights in silent suffering" trait under-shown**: first draft was all controlled calm, no delight. Added one explicit, restrained beat of sadistic satisfaction during the expected-fear moment, without tipping into cartoonish glee.
- **Ending sharpened**: closing line changed from a repeated abstraction ("kept for himself") to a more specific turn — Barbas has already decided what he's withholding, and it isn't the modest, half-answer he just gave Balam in its place.
