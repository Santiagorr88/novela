## Chapter Review Verdict — B3C10.5 "Readiness, Not Disobedience"

**Result:** PASS WITH FIXES (fixes already applied to the file before this report was finalized)

**Context:** New interlude chapter, inserted between B3C10_EN.md ("The Summit's End") and B3C11_EN.md ("The In-Between War"). Gives Commander Kushiel his first on-page scene in the project's 143+ chapters (a prior audit found zero appearances). Expands outline entries B3C043 ("A Commander Who Wasn't Consulted") and B3C044 ("What the Fleet Doesn't Have") from `content/lore/arco_argumental_completo.md` (lines 1381–1387) into a full 8-beat chapter, and delivers the connective tissue into B3C11's opening ("Matched, near enough").

Reviewed by 5 parallel panels: Continuity & Canon, Style & Voice, Pacing & Structure, Dialogue & Character Voice (all Claude subagents), and an independent uncontaminated read via `codex exec`. A second, targeted Codex pass confirmed the fixes below actually landed in the revised text.

### Required fixes (blocking) — all applied

- **[Dialogue/Continuity] Sachael's on-page debut read as disciplined/legalistic rather than "unstable, just, explosive" per his dossier** — flagged independently by both the Continuity and Dialogue reviewers, with no documented canon correction on file the way Camael's stale "impulsive" trait has. Fix applied: added a beat where Fulmen's tip crackles involuntarily with his temper mid-objection before he reins it in, and gave him a concrete, specific precedent (a commander named Malakiel broken by the Rite of Questioned Command) rather than an abstract procedural objection, so the "explosive" trait surfaces in the dialogue itself, not just the narration around him.

- **[Style] Chained/multiple figured images in one sentence** — house style: "never chain multiple similes in one sentence." Fix applied to the two clearest violations: the deployment-road sentence ("the ground itself seemed to remember... like a road...") was cut; the hammer/hymn opposition, originally stated twice in near-identical language within one section, was trimmed to a single instance.

- **[Style] Systemic repetition tic** — "never once" appeared 9 times and "the particular X of a man who Y" appeared 5 times across ~2,000 words (also independently flagged by the Codex read as an authorial tic, and previously documented in `expansion_guidelines.md` as a known cross-project risk). Fix applied: all instances rewritten with varied constructions; one single remaining "the particular unease of..." instance is no longer a repeated pattern.

- **[Pacing] The chapter's central plot action (Kushiel's order to redirect a third of his force) was narrated with zero direct dialogue** — the most consequential beat in the chapter had the least narrative weight and no quoted order. Fix applied: added a direct, in-character spoken order from Kushiel before the mobilization sequence.

- **[Style] POV discipline — one small head-hop** — a line asserted Kemuel's internal premonition ("something in the air had already told him") as narrated fact rather than Kushiel's inference. Fix applied: reframed explicitly as something Kushiel had learned to read in Kemuel's body language.

### Suggested improvements (non-blocking, applied anyway)

- Trimmed the three-paragraph deployment sequence (Codex: "particularly padded... one strong paragraph would do more than three") down to two tighter paragraphs.
- Reworked Kushiel's two closing lines (end of the Sachael exchange, end of the deployment section) away from confident, fully-resolved maxims toward beats that leave a visible, felt cost/uncertainty on the POV character, per house style's "scene outs end on pivot/cost/question, not a hard wrap." This also resolved a separate finding that the two lines were rhetorically identical to each other (same "I've never lost an argument like this" shape), which risked reading as authorial tic rather than an established Kushiel idiolect on his very first appearance.
- Softened the Gabriel quote-attribution risk: Thariel's report now frames "supporting" as the word his two independent sources both used, rather than asserting it as Gabriel's own literal word choice (Gabriel's actual prose line, in B2C36, is paraphrased differently — "the wrong war," "a smaller quarrel" — so the chapter's language is spirit-consistent but not a verbatim quote).
- Clarified the "third of Kushiel's force" vs. "quarter of Camael's reserve" fraction relationship in the coda, which an earlier draft left ambiguous enough that a careful reader could read it as an arithmetic contradiction rather than two different totals.
- Disambiguated "vanguard" — reserved for Camael's canon-established force name (per B3C11) — from Kemuel's column, which no longer borrows the same word.

### What worked

- **Kushiel's voice lands immediately and distinctly on his first-ever appearance.** No introductory paragraph is needed before his first line ("You called retreat... on a flare that wasn't ours") — the accusation-as-fact register does the characterization work. "I don't punish fear. Fear is honest. I punish men who let it choose for them before I've had the chance to" was independently praised by two reviewers as threading the "brutal but not cartoonish, loyal to the cause not just to violence" needle the source material required.
- **Camael's brief closing POV section correctly follows the project's already-corrected characterization** (contained/practical, per the fix documented in `expansion_guidelines.md` for B3C28) rather than the stale "impulsive, aggressive" line in his base dossier — flagged as a clean pass by the Continuity reviewer.
- **Kemuel's voice was the cleanest hit in the panel's assessment** — "What did the choir sing this time?" and "We're built for exactly that kind of reaching" characterize him through diction rather than exposition.
- **No canon violations**: Aamon is never referenced (correctly, since he died in Book I); Hell's side stays deliberately generic/unnamed rather than risking a Vepar characterization error; no reveal-pacing violation (Thamorak/Aetheris, both locked until B3C16, never appear); clean, verified handoff into B3C11's opening line ("Matched, near enough").
- **Structural fidelity**: all 8 planned beats present, in order, none skipped or compressed into a throwaway line (verified against the beat plan by the Pacing reviewer); word count lands at ~2,280 words, within the 2,250–3,000 target band, well clear of the 1,800 floor.
