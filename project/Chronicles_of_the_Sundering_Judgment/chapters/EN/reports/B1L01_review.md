## Chapter Review Verdict — B1L01 (Lament Arc, Ch. 1 of 6 — "The First Threshold")

**Result:** PASS WITH FIXES

### Required fixes (blocking)
- [Style] Chained similes in one sentence — explicit house-style violation ("Never chain multiple similes in one sentence"). Evidence: *"He had carried that warning the whole way, the way a man carries an insult, turning it over until its edges softened into something almost like fuel."* Fix: cut to one figurative move.
- [Pacing + independent Codex read, corroborated by two separate reviewers] The guardian's reveal is deflated by editorial foreshadowing placed one beat too early. Evidence: *"He should have known that a place built entirely from grief would understand, better than anyone, the particular vanity of a man convinced he was owed an easy victory,"* placed directly before the guardian appears. Fix: cut this line or move it to after the retaliation — let Belial's failure teach the lesson instead of narrating it in advance.
- [Style + independent Codex read] General overwriting / ornament accumulation, flagged independently by the Style reviewer (image-density above "one image beats three" in the opening paragraphs) and by Codex with zero lore context ("nearly every image receives a second explanation, then often a third... cutting 25–35% would make the same events feel sharper"). Fix: trim the opening atmosphere paragraphs; cut several instances of the recurring "the particular X of a man who Y" construction (appears ~6 times per Reviewer 4); let the chapter's two strongest inventions — the crystal's scream read as *insult* rather than pain, and the guardian "grieving at him" — stand on their own instead of being buried in more sorrow-metaphor.

### Suggested improvements (non-blocking)
- "Ozone" used twice in close proximity as the same sensory anchor — vary the image on the second use.
- "Golden eyes narrowed, he pushed himself upright" reads as a generic fantasy stock phrase against otherwise careful prose.
- Codex: the ending states its conclusion three times in a row (force is useless / it was a warning about method / he must learn its grief / returning will cost him). Strongest actual endpoint is likely *"He would have to learn its grief before it would ever let him touch its truth"* — consider cutting the final two sentences.
- The single line of dialogue ("I am not here for you") is carried entirely by interiority; a small physical micro-action alongside it would satisfy Dialogue & Subtext guidance more fully.
- Codex flags Ruach's nature (mace, "killed gods," "doubt-laced impact") as under-explained for a cold reader. Lower priority — this chapter is read in sequence after five prior chapters that already establish Ruach, not as a true cold open.

### What worked
- Continuity/canon is clean: correct weapon timeline (Ruach only, no Lament), no leak of Thaeriel's identity or the Forgotten reveal-pacing rule, faithful to the original "A Plane of Sorrow" reference beat and the Sepulcher's established "resist the pull, fall deeper" paradox.
- Belial's characterization is dossier-accurate — pride, contempt, strategist framing — and the pride-crack beat lands through sequencing (shock → cost-framing → fury → reluctant acceptance → pride reasserting) rather than reading as an out-of-character jump.
- POV discipline is tight throughout; the scene-closing mechanic (ends on cost + open question, not a flat wrap) is well executed.
- Beat coverage and proportion (~25% / 15% / 44% / 16% across the four beats) matches narrative weight — the guardian confrontation, the real turning point, correctly receives the most space.
