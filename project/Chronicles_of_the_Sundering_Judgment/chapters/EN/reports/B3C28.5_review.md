## Chapter Review Verdict — B3C28.5 "Whose Idea This Was, Already" (POV Naamah)

**Result:** PASS WITH FIXES (all required fixes applied before finalization)

Panel: 4 Claude subagent reviewers (Continuity & Canon, Style & Voice, Pacing & Structure, Dialogue & Character Voice) + 1 independent Codex read (`codex exec -s read-only`, no canon context). Word count at first draft: 2,352. Final word count after fixes: 2,314. Target range 2,250-3,000 — PASS.

### Required fixes (blocking) — all applied

- **[Continuity/POV]** Full head-hop into Stolas's private interiority after Naamah had left the scene (original lines 47-53: "Stolas turned Naamah's absence over in his own mind... found... no clean way to say what actually unsettled him... he understood... that his commander had just made exactly that sacrifice"). Naamah was not present to infer any of this — a genuine scene from another character's head, not a filtering lapse. Flagged independently by Reviewer 1 (Continuity, blocking) and corroborated by Reviewer 2 (Style) and the Codex read (both noted the section over-explained the beat). **Fix**: restaged so Naamah has not yet left earshot — she overhears the Stolas/Vepar exchange externally (dialogue only) and infers Stolas's reaction solely from his observable behavior (his stillness, his choice not to press further), never from his stated private reasoning.
- **[Style]** "Never once" construction used 8 times across ~2,350 words — a stock tic flagged in prior chapter reviews this session. **Fix**: cut to 2 uses (one in dialogue, one in the reframed Stolas beat), reworded the other 6 instances with varied phrasing/imagery.
- **[Style]** "The particular X of a [noun] who Y" construction repeated 3+ times (Gusion's smile, Vepar's relief, Stolas's stillness), plus "particular" used as a generic intensifier 6 times total. **Fix**: removed the construction everywhere it appeared; "particular" now appears only twice, both in the neutral idiomatic sense ("this particular war," "that particular economy"), not the flagged pattern.

### Suggested improvements (non-blocking) — applied

- **[Continuity/POV, minor]** Two unfiltered assertions of another character's internal state as objective fact rather than Naamah's inference (Vepar "did not see... exactly how much he had just handed her"; the old lord "found nothing in it worth pressing further"). Fixed with explicit filter tags ("she was certain," reframed to describe only his observable action of dropping the matter).
- **[Style, minor]** "Turning X over" contemplative gesture used 4 times (Gusion's quill, Naamah's thought, Stolas's reasoning, Lucifer's account). Reduced to zero — varied verbs used instead (weighed, held, considered via context).
- **[Dialogue, minor]** Vepar's opening line ("Hell's court doesn't usually send its beauty to a staging ground") read as personal flirtation, a register never shown in his established appearances (`B3C28_EN.md`, `B2C21pt5_EN.md`), where his only warmth is oceanic detachment, not personal compliment. Reworded to dry commentary on court vanity in general ("anyone this polished") rather than a remark on Naamah specifically — closer to his established sardonic register.
- **[Craft, Codex]** First mention of Neriah (her weapon/veil) was too unanchored for a reader with no lore access. Added a brief clarifying appositive at first use ("the veil she wore as easily as skin").

### Suggested improvements (not applied — judgment call)

- Codex suggested cutting the chapter 15-25% for over-interpretation after dialogue. Reviewer 3 (Pacing & Structure) independently confirmed all 6 planned beats are present, correctly ordered, and proportionately weighted, with no blocking pacing issues — the target word count (2,250-3,000) is a hard project floor, not a ceiling to cut toward. Trimmed the tic-driven redundancy Codex was likely reacting to (see fixes above) without a wholesale length cut, since the beat-planner-mandated density was already correctly hit.
- Reviewer 4 flagged Naamah's court dialogue ("I only thought the throne should know...") as sitting right at the edge of dishonesty despite being technically true — assessed as intentional characterization consistent with Neriah's "weaponizes emotion, not falsehood" mechanic, not a fix.

### What worked

- All 6 planned beats (pattern 7, "Manipulación/Trampa") present, in order, each with real weight — confirmed by Reviewer 3.
- No contradiction found against `B3C28_EN.md`, `B2C21pt5_EN.md`, `B3C27_EN.md`, or the Naamah/Vepar/Stolas/Gusion fiches — confirmed by Reviewer 1. Timeline placement (weeks after B3C28, before B3C30) does not disturb the "fourth encounter, first spoken" arithmetic already fixed in prose.
- Four distinct character voices in one chapter (Naamah, Gusion, Vepar, Stolas, plus Lucifer and an unnamed court lord) — confirmed by Reviewer 4, no character interchangeable with another.
- No chained similes anywhere; dialogue well-anchored by micro-actions (the quill, Marea resting on Vepar's forearm, the requisition tablet) — confirmed by Reviewer 2.
- Codex's independent, lore-blind read called the chapter "lucid, intelligent political fantasy with a strong governing idea," found Naamah and Vepar's voices clearly distinct, and judged the ending ("here, as of tonight, it was already hers") the right final note.
