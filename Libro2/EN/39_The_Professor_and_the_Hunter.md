# Chapter 39 — The Professor and the Hunter

The count reached three and the door simply stopped fighting them. Mikel felt the old iron groan once against his shoulder, resistance he'd braced for giving way all at once rather than gradually, and then he was stumbling forward into open space, his flashlight beam swinging wild across a corridor he hadn't expected to find waiting on the other side — low-ceilinged, thick with undisturbed dust, lined with shelving that had collapsed in on itself decades ago and never been righted.

He caught his balance against the nearest wall and turned, and there, straightening from the same stumbling entrance on the corridor's opposite side, stood the man whose breathing he'd spent the better part of an hour listening to through a warped iron door. He didn't look surprised. He looked like a man arriving somewhere he'd already half expected to end up, which unsettled Mikel almost as much as the man's appearance itself did.

Mikel's first thought was that he'd been wrong. He had pictured, across that long hour of waiting, someone closer to himself — another researcher, perhaps, another man drawn here by curiosity rather than danger. What stood across from him instead had the stillness of something built for exactly one purpose, weight balanced on the balls of his feet even now, in the middle of stumbling recovery, as though his body had never once in its life allowed itself the luxury of an unguarded moment. Sharp features, dark hair cropped close and practical. A scar tracing the line of his neck, old and pale against skin that had clearly spent more time outdoors than any academic's ever did. He wore no visible weapon, no armor, nothing that marked him as anything other than an ordinary man in plain, worn clothing, and somehow that absence unsettled Mikel more than any blade would have. A man like this, he understood without needing to articulate why, had never once required a weapon to be the most dangerous thing in a room.

And the eyes — Mikel had read enough history to recognize the particular flatness in them, the specific quality he'd only ever encountered before in photographs of men who had killed and meant to do it again. He found himself, for one absurd instant in the middle of genuine fear, cataloguing the observation the way he might have catalogued a source for a paper, some stubborn scholar's habit refusing to switch off even now.

---

Arin caught his own balance a half-second behind the door's collapse, and found himself facing, across a corridor thick with forty years of undisturbed dust, exactly the kind of man he hadn't expected to find waiting at the end of a demonic trap.

Soft hands. That was the detail that struck him first, before anything else registered — hands that had never once held a weapon in earnest, ink-stained at the fingertips in a way that spoke of grading papers rather than gripping a blade, one of them still wrapped, out of some old habit Arin couldn't yet place, around a small pendant at his throat. White hair, though the man couldn't have been much past his mid-thirties by any measure Arin's trained eye could offer, framing a face more suited to a lecture hall than to whatever waited beneath this university's own foundations. Practical clothes, rumpled from a night that had clearly gone longer and stranger than he'd planned for. No visible weapon anywhere on him, and none of the practiced readiness a man carries when he expects to need one.

He didn't carry himself like a threat. He carried himself like a man who had never once needed to learn how threats carried themselves, which was, in Arin's considerable experience, sometimes more dangerous than the alternative and sometimes not dangerous at all. He ran the full assessment in the span of a single breath, the same disciplined habit that had kept him alive through every first meeting a decade of contracts had ever handed him, and filed the conclusion away without yet fully trusting it: unarmed, untrained, and almost certainly not the danger this trap had been built around.

Neither of them moved for a long moment, sizing each other up across the narrow corridor with the same wary stillness that had kept them both quiet on opposite sides of that door for the better part of an hour.

---

"Who are you?" Arin asked first, his voice carrying none of the warmth it might have offered a stranger encountered anywhere less dangerous than this. It wasn't a request. It was the kind of question a man asked when he still had every intention of acting on whatever answer he received.

Mikel held his ground, though every reasonable instinct in him argued for putting more distance between himself and a man who looked capable of closing it in under a second if he decided to. "Could ask you the same thing," he said, and heard, even as he said it, how much steadier the words came out than the pulse currently hammering in his own chest. Nine years of facing down skeptical department heads and bored undergraduates had taught him how to sound calm on command. It worked now, barely, against a fear this particular audience actually deserved.

Arin's eyes tracked him once, head to foot, cataloguing details with what looked, to Mikel, like the flat, professional thoroughness of a man assessing exactly how much of a threat he was actually facing. Whatever conclusion he reached, he didn't share it. "Fair enough," he said instead. "Mikel Ardon. I've had your name in a file for three days."

Mikel's stomach dropped at that. The word *file* was plain and procedural, and somehow that made it worse than any threat could have. "You've been watching me."

"Not you specifically. Not until three days ago." Arin's gaze didn't waver. "You've been at the center of something bigger than you understand yet. I'm guessing that's part of why you're standing in a collapsed archive at midnight instead of sleeping like a reasonable man would."

"You could have introduced yourself through the door," Mikel said, some small, defiant part of him refusing to let the fear entirely dictate the conversation. "Would have saved us both an hour of sitting in the dark."

"Would you have believed me?"

Mikel considered that honestly. "No," he admitted. "Probably not."

---

"I haven't come to fight," Mikel said, and meant it fully enough that his voice finally settled into something closer to the plain, tired honesty he'd been sitting on for months now, with no one worth telling it to. "I've come to understand something I've been chasing my entire life without knowing I was chasing it. If that means answering to whatever file you've got with my name in it, fine. I'd rather have answers than safety at this point."

Something in Arin's posture eased, fractionally, at that — not trust exactly, but what looked, to Mikel, like the loosening of a man recalculating a threat he'd been braced to meet head-on. "I haven't come to fight either," he said. "But I've spent the last few months learning that 'not here to fight' is rarely a decision that's actually mine to make." He didn't lower his hands from where they hovered, loose and ready, at his sides. "You said you've been chasing something your whole life. What, exactly."

Mikel considered the question longer than its size seemed to warrant, weighing how much of the truth a stranger with a file on him actually deserved to hear this early. "I don't fully know," he admitted, finally. "Dreams I can't place. A pull toward this exact spot that started before I had any research to justify it. Nine years of ordinary academic work, and then one season where nothing about my own life makes sense anymore, and every thread I pull leads back to the same three languages and the same impossible ground." He gestured, vaguely, at the collapsed shelving around them. "I was hoping tonight might finally answer that. Instead I got a locked door and a man who apparently already knows more about my own life than I do."

"I don't know more than you do," Arin said. "I just know a different half of it."

---

Mikel hadn't lowered his guard, not really, but for one unguarded pause, he stood still without bracing for an attack. It was in that pause that something passed through him, quick and unbidden, gone almost before he'd fully registered it.

Mikel saw it first in the hunter's eyes, a flicker beneath the flat, professional coldness that had nothing to do with tactics or threat assessment. For one disorienting half-second, he saw something else entirely behind that scarred, guarded face — a grief too large for any single human lifetime to have earned, and beneath it a fury that had been righteous for so long it no longer felt clean about itself. It looked less like a stranger's expression than like something remembered, surfacing in a face that had no right to hold it. Then it was gone, and the ordinary hardness of a stranger's face was all that remained. Mikel couldn't have said, afterward, whether he'd actually seen it or simply imagined it out of nerves and exhaustion. He found he couldn't shake it either way.

---

Arin saw something too, in that same unguarded half-second, looking into eyes that should have belonged to nothing more than a frightened, overextended professor. A flash of gold, there and gone before he could properly track it — wings, and behind them some impression of authority he had no framework yet for placing. It was there for less than a second. He blinked, and the professor stood there again, pale and human and entirely unremarkable. Arin told himself, immediately and with real conviction, that it had been nothing — bad light, an adrenaline-strained mind reaching for a shape that wasn't actually there. He'd seen stranger tricks of the eye than this under far less pressure than tonight had put him under.

---

Neither man named what he'd seen. Mikel told himself it had been nothing more than exhaustion and the corridor's bad light playing tricks on an already frayed mind.

---

Arin dismissed his own version of it just as quickly, filing it under the same growing list of unexplained noise this whole month had handed him — proximity to something old and strange enough, he reasoned, to leave anyone's nerves misfiring. He'd trusted stranger explanations than that one before and been right often enough to trust this one too. Neither man said a word about it to the other.

"You said you know a different half," Mikel said instead, steering the conversation back toward solid ground. "Of what, exactly?"

Arin opened his mouth to answer, and the corridor answered first.

---

The sound came from behind the wall they'd both just broken through — not a groan of settling stone this time, but laughter, low and delighted and entirely wrong for the space it moved through, the particular sound of something that had been waiting a very long time for exactly this moment to finally arrive. A seam of orange light split the darkness where the old iron door had stood, spreading fast along a crack neither man had noticed until it was already too wide to ignore, heat rolling off it in waves that had nothing to do with any fire either of them recognized. The laughter didn't come from one voice. It came from several, layered and overlapping, each one delighted in its own particular register, the specific sound of an audience that had been waiting patiently in the dark for a show it already knew the ending of.

Mikel took an instinctive step back, his pulse spiking hard enough that he felt it in his own throat. Arin didn't move back at all. He'd already turned fully toward the widening light, the careful truce of a minute ago gone from his face entirely, replaced by something harder and far more familiar to him.

"Whatever we were about to say to each other," Arin said, not looking away from the light, "it's going to have to wait."

Mikel found, to his own considerable surprise, that he agreed completely. Whatever answers he'd come down here for, they would have to stand in line behind whatever was currently laughing on the other side of that wall.
