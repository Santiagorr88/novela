# Chapter 40 — Ambush Below the Halls

The wall didn't burn so much as unmake itself, stone dissolving into a doorway of pure fire that had never once needed the old iron door to exist properly. Arin threw an arm up against the heat rolling off it and held his ground anyway, cataloguing the shape of it with the same flat professionalism he'd have given any breach — wide enough for three to pass abreast, edges licking upward in slow, deliberate curls that had nothing accidental about their construction. This was not a portal ripped open in panic. This was a door someone had built to open exactly this way, at exactly this hour, for exactly this purpose.

The heat reached him a full two seconds before the first shapes did, dry and metallic against his face, close enough now to sting his eyes, carrying a smell he'd learned to associate with exactly one kind of trouble across the last month of hunting. He backed half a step, weight settling low, already running the corridor's geometry through his head — one exit behind them, collapsed shelving on both sides narrow enough to slow a charge but not stop one, no cover worth the name anywhere close. Not good ground. He'd fought on worse.

The first demons came through in loose formation, armed and armored in a manner that told Arin immediately this was no scouting party. Six, then eight, blades and hooked polearms catching the fire's orange light, moving with the disciplined economy of soldiers who had drilled together rather than the scattered menace of low-ranked opportunists. Their armor bore matching insignia, dark iron worked into a sigil Arin didn't recognize but filed away regardless, some old professional habit refusing to let even a losing fight pass without gathering whatever intelligence it could. He counted them the way he counted everything worth tracking in a fight, filing numbers and positions before his body had finished deciding what to do about any of it.

---

Mikel's first instinct was to run, and his second, arriving almost as fast, was the plain recognition that running gave a creature with a polearm exactly the angle it wanted. He'd read enough about ambush tactics across nine years of military history to know that instinct, however loud, was rarely the same thing as the correct response. He stayed rooted where he stood instead, heart hammering, watching the fire-wreathed doorway disgorge more armed shapes than he could easily count, and found himself, absurdly, cataloguing their formation the same clinical way he'd have catalogued an illustration in a battle history.

They weren't human, not by any definition his eyes wanted to settle on — skin the color of old bruises in some, scaled and dry in others, eyes that caught the firelight wrong, throwing it back yellow rather than reflecting it. He'd spent a career studying wars fought by ordinary men against ordinary men, and nothing in nine years of careful scholarship had prepared him for the specific, bottomless wrongness of watching something like this file calmly through a wall.

He didn't know why the shapes filing through that doorway felt less like nightmare and more like recognition, some old unnamed part of him supplying the word *legion* before his conscious mind had finished processing what he was looking at. He pushed the thought aside. There would be time later, if there was a later, to ask himself why an ancient history professor's mind reached first for military vocabulary instead of ordinary terror.

---

The one who stepped through last carried himself differently from the rest — unhurried, armored in something sleeker than the soldiers flanking him, his approach carrying what read as the specific calm of a man who had never once needed to raise his voice to be obeyed. His eyes found Arin first, then Mikel, moving between them with an unnerving precision that had nothing searching about it at all. He already knew exactly what he was looking at.

He stopped a careful distance back from the fighting line his soldiers had already formed, hands loose at his sides, the particular stillness Arin read as a commander who preferred to let others absorb the risk of the opening move. Whatever rank he held, it plainly excused him from the kind of work the eight soldiers arrayed in front of him were about to be asked to do. He studied both of them a moment longer, unhurried, the way a man studies a ledger he already knows will balance in his favor.

"Lord Belial will be pleased," the enforcer said, his voice carrying an artificial evenness that unsettled Arin more than any battle cry would have. "The fallen Commander and the renegade Judge, in the same room, saving us the trouble of finding you separately." He raised one hand, unhurried, toward the soldiers arrayed behind him. "Take them."

---

Arin didn't look away from the demons already spreading into a loose, practiced encirclement, calculating angles and exits with the same cold speed a decade of contracts had trained into him. Eight against two, in a corridor too narrow to properly maneuver, with an unarmed academic standing at his back who had no combat training Arin could detect. The math wasn't good. It had been worse before.

"Trust me enough to fight back to back with a stranger?" he asked, low, not turning his head from the demons still closing the distance.

---

Mikel felt his body settle into something he hadn't consciously chosen, weight dropping low, hands rising loose in front of him in a stance he had no memory of ever learning. He hadn't decided to stand this way. His body had simply already known how. "I trust surviving this separately even less," he said, and heard, distantly, how steady the words came out despite the fear still roaring underneath them.

---

They moved into position without further discussion, shoulder blades nearly touching, an academic and a killer standing back to back against eight armed soldiers in a corridor thick with forty years of undisturbed dust. Arin felt the shift the instant it happened — not trust exactly, not yet, but something close enough to function the same way in a fight this outmatched. He'd fought beside strangers before, in contracts that had called for it, careful arrangements built on payment and mutual self-interest rather than anything resembling faith. This felt different. He didn't have time to examine why before the first blade came at him.

The enforcer gave a short, unhurried gesture, and the eight soldiers closed the last of the distance at once, blades and polearms rising in near-perfect unison, boots striking the old stone floor in a rhythm too disciplined to belong to anything but trained soldiers.

---

Arin met the first demon's strike with nothing more than his forearm and the flat, practiced brutality of a decade spent ending fights before they properly started, driving the creature back into the one behind it before either had finished registering the collision. He drove a knee into the staggered creature's ribs, felt something give under the blow, and used the falling body as a brief shield against the polearm already sweeping in from his left. He moved through the next several seconds the way he moved through every fight worth remembering — economical, precise, each strike calculated to cost the least effort for the greatest result, a whirlwind that never once wasted motion on anything resembling flourish.

He caught a blade's edge on his forearm rather than dodge it fully, accepting the shallow cut in exchange for the half-second it bought him to close distance and end the demon holding it, the sting registering somewhere far behind the part of his mind actually running the fight. He drove his palm into a fourth demon's throat hard enough to fold it backward into the wall, and used the collision to buy the space he needed to pivot clear of a hooked polearm swinging in from behind him. Two more went down in the time it took a third to finish raising its weapon. He didn't gloat over either. He simply moved to the next threat, already tracking three moves ahead of where his body currently stood — which of the remaining soldiers favored their left side, which one had already started favoring an injured leg, which one hung back from the melee in the particular way officers hung back to direct rather than fight.

---

Mikel had no training to draw on and no weapon to wield, and found, within the fight's first frantic seconds, that intellect alone could still buy him something worth having. He watched the demons' formation shift and called it out before the shift finished happening, a sharp warning that sent Arin pivoting half a second before a blade would have found his exposed side. He grabbed a fallen length of shelving and threw it hard into the legs of a demon circling wide, buying himself and Arin a precious extra second while the creature stumbled.

He kept his back pressed close to Arin's, close enough to feel the other man's weight shift with every strike, tracking the fight's shape the way he'd once have tracked an argument's structure across a crowded seminar room — where the pressure was building, where it was thinnest, which direction offered the fewest bodies between them and open ground. He called out a second warning, then a third. Each time, Arin used the half-second it bought him to put another demon down. Mikel found some grim, unexpected satisfaction in discovering that his mind, at least, had something real to contribute to a fight his body had no business surviving. He wasn't fighting so much as reading the fight, the same patient, pattern-hungry instinct that had carried him through months of research now applied, with startling ease, to keeping them both alive.

---

The flanking demon came from an angle Arin couldn't cover, too far around Mikel's exposed side for Arin to reach in time, cutting wide and low the way a soldier cuts when he's found a gap in the enemy's line and means to make the most of it. Mikel's body answered before his mind had finished registering the threat at all. His hand closed around a jagged length of broken shelving without any conscious decision behind the motion, and he brought it up and across in an arc that had no business looking as controlled as it did from a man who had never once held a blade in his adult life.

The strike was clumsy at the edges, footwork wrong, weight distributed the way an untrained man's weight always distributed under pressure. Underneath the clumsiness, though, sat something else entirely — the angle of the swing, the exact point of impact he'd aimed for without consciously choosing it, the follow-through that pulled the blow back into a defensible position rather than leaving him overextended. It connected anyway, driving the demon back with a force that startled Mikel almost as much as it startled the creature bleeding out at his feet a moment later.

He didn't have time to marvel at it. Another shape was already closing from his left, and his arm rose again on its own, the length of scrap metal finding a guard position he hadn't consciously chosen either. He stared down at it for the length of a single breath between strikes, as though it belonged to someone else, and then the next demon was on him and there was no more room left for wondering.

---

Arin caught the movement from the corner of his eye even mid-strike against his own opponent, and something in him went very still for the length of a single heartbeat despite the chaos still churning around them both. His eyes tracked the follow-through a half-second longer than the fight strictly allowed him to spare, something unreadable passing across his face before it hardened back into its usual flatness.

He said nothing. There was no time for it, and the demons pressing in from three directions gave him no room to spare the thought any further attention. He turned back to the fight, jaw tight, and drove the nearest soldier back with three short blows before it had finished registering that Arin's attention had ever left it at all.
