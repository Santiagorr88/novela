# Chapter 44 — Two Roads from a Crossroads

Mikel was unwilling to be the one who broke the silence first, the pendant at his throat still faintly warm, the figure's last words still too strange to make sense of. He thought of the prophecy, of the two roles it had assigned and the third it had left unnamed.

---

Arin stood a short distance off, arms loose at his sides, his own silence carrying a different weight than Mikel's — more exhaustion in it now than caution. He'd spent the last hour running through every threat assessment his training could offer, and none of them had prepared him for the quiet that settles over a man once the danger has actually passed. His shoulder still ached where the enforcer's blow had landed, a dull reminder that tonight had cost him something real.

---

He looked at Mikel across the empty corridor, still catching his breath, and realized, for the first time since he'd laid eyes on this soft-handed academic, that the sharpest edge of the distrust he'd carried into their first exchange had dulled. He tried, briefly, to summon back the flat, professional wariness that had carried him through a decade of first meetings with strangers far less trustworthy than this one. Some of it answered the call. Enough of it didn't that he no longer read the man across from him as a threat.

---

Mikel met his gaze and held it, and something passed between them that neither man tried to put into words — not friendship exactly, not yet, but the cautious respect of two people who'd just watched each other prove more than expected. Mikel thought of the killer's eyes he'd catalogued in that first frightened glance through the shattered door, and no longer saw only that in the man standing across from him now. He thought, too, of the shelving fragment still leaned against the wall behind him, and of the unhesitating certainty with which his own hand had wielded it. Whatever Arin had seen in him fighting, it had likely changed the other man's assessment of him as much as tonight had changed his own.

Neither of them moved to close the distance still separating them. It wasn't hostility keeping the space open, Mikel understood, studying the careful set of Arin's shoulders — it was the recognition that respect earned in one desperate hour wasn't yet trust earned over time. Standing there with the last of the dust still drifting behind him, he didn't mind the distance. It felt more honest than pretending an hour of shared violence had already settled something that would need more than one night.

---

"I don't know what we're supposed to be," Mikel said finally, his voice raw in the settling quiet. "But I know I can't pretend this didn't happen and go teach class tomorrow like nothing's changed."

Arin nodded slowly, the motion carrying more weight than the gesture alone should have. "I can't go back to hunting like the world's as simple as I thought it was yesterday either."

"What was it like," Mikel asked, curiosity winning out over the exhaustion still weighing on him, "before tonight. The version of the world you were operating in."

Arin considered the question before he answered it. "Clean lines. People who deserved what I brought them, and people who didn't. I'd been finding that harder to judge lately, if I'm honest. Tonight didn't erase the distinction. It just showed me how much smaller it actually was than I'd spent my whole life believing."

Neither man said anything further for a long moment, letting the admission sit between them, plain and unadorned. It was the first time either had said, out loud, that whatever life they'd been living before tonight no longer fit. Mikel felt an unexpected relief at hearing Arin admit it too — a small comfort in learning he wasn't the only one it was happening to.

---

"So what now?" Mikel asked, the question carrying less certainty than he'd meant it to.

Arin considered the question longer than its size seemed to warrant. "I need to know what I am before I go looking for what's supposed to be mine. Something that isn't just my own two hands. I've felt its edges before, in fights that should've gone worse than they did. I'm not ready. When I am, I'll find it."

"That makes two of us," Mikel said. "I keep dreaming about a sword I've never held. I think, before tonight, I'd have told you that was just stress. I'm not sure I believe that anymore. Whatever it is, I don't think I'm ready to go looking for it either. Not without understanding what I'm actually walking toward first. My whole life is built around understanding things properly before I act on them. I don't intend to abandon that now, of all moments."

They stood a moment in the quiet that followed. Neither pushed the other for more detail. Some things, Mikel understood, were better approached alone first, at whatever pace his own understanding was ready to move.

---

"For now," Arin said, "we walk separate roads. Not distrust. Just — neither of us is ready to carry the other half of this yet."

Mikel agreed, more readily than he expected to. "Separate roads," he repeated. "Same direction, eventually." He offered his hand, an oddly formal gesture given everything they'd just survived together, and Arin took it without hesitation.

---

Arin nodded once, and turned to check himself over before the walk ahead of him, an old habit that had nothing to do with trust and everything to do with survival. His shoulder still ached, but it moved freely enough. That would have to do for tonight.

---

Mikel hesitated before Arin turned to go — a question he'd carried half-formed for weeks now finally reaching the front of his thoughts. He'd never had the right moment, or the right person, to ask it. Tonight, apparently, had given him both. "Can I ask you something. Before you leave."

Arin waited, saying nothing — the stillness of a man who'd learned, across years of experience, not to rush a question before its asker was ready to finish it.

"Have you ever felt like you remember things that didn't actually happen to you? Not déjà vu. Something heavier than that — like a memory that belongs to someone else, and you're just the one carrying it around."

---

Arin went very still at the question, something shifting behind his eyes. He thought of a bar, months ago, a veteran turning his glass in slow circles, saying almost those exact words to him unprompted, in a voice gone quiet, closer to fear than drink. *Like the memory belongs to somebody else and I'm just renting space for it.* He'd dismissed it that night as drunk talk — one more odd moment in a strange month.

He didn't dismiss it now, not with everything the last hour had already forced him to reconsider.

"Yeah," Arin said, slower than he meant to. "I've felt that. More than once. There was a man in a bar, months back, who described it almost word for word the way you just did. I told myself he was drunk and left it there, because that was easier than the alternative." He held Mikel's gaze, some old certainty working itself loose behind his own eyes as he said it. "I'm starting to think neither of us has been remembering anything. I'm starting to think we're waking up."

---

Mikel considered that against the dreams, the pendant, the prophecy that had seemed to know what he was before he'd had a chance to explain himself — everything strange this last month had brought him. Waking up implied a sleep long enough that neither of them had any real memory of falling into it, older than either of their ordinary lives had any way of measuring. He had no argument against the idea. He wasn't certain, standing here, that he wanted one.

"If we're waking up," he said, "I'd like to know what we're waking up into. Before it's finished happening to me rather than something I actually understood."

"So would I. I don't think either of us gets that luxury tonight."

"No," Mikel agreed. "I don't suppose we do." Saying it out loud was easier than he'd expected. Some part of him had been waiting for this conversation longer than he'd let himself admit.

---

Neither man spoke the promise aloud. Standing there in the last of the settling dust, Mikel understood that some version of it had already been made between them. Find the third. Find whatever had broken alongside him, before whatever was forgetting out there in the world finished forgetting. He didn't know yet how to begin a search that vague. The not-knowing no longer felt like an excuse to wait.

---

Arin turned first, walking back the way he'd come without a final word. His footsteps faded into the dark of the passage, measured and even, the same pace he'd have used walking away from any ordinary job. He didn't look back. Looking back, he'd learned across a decade of harder departures than this, rarely made a parting easier, only longer. Whatever waited at the end of this new search, he'd face it the same disciplined way he faced everything else.

---

Mikel watched him go until the passage's darkness hid him, reluctant, for reasons he couldn't quite name, to be the one who moved first this time. He stayed a moment longer than he needed to, unwilling to be alone yet with everything tonight had left him to sit with. He turned the other direction eventually, toward the stairs and the ordinary campus waiting above, and walked alone through the quiet corridor, already missing the one person tonight who had understood what he'd just been through.

The stairwell's light, when he finally reached it, felt too ordinary for a night that had ended this strangely — fluorescent, flickering, indifferent to everything that had just happened three floors beneath it. Mikel climbed toward it anyway, one slow step at a time, his legs still unsteady. He had a direction now where he'd only had questions an hour ago, and understood, reaching the top of the stairs, that nothing about his life above them would feel the same again. He pushed through the door into the cool night air and stood there, breathing it in, before he trusted himself to walk further.
