# Chapter 35 — The Jester's Tune

Milo Ray had found a new bench since the last time, closer to the old fountain at the park's western edge, though he suspected the bench itself hardly mattered. He'd have felt the same pull sitting on the grass. The city reached him wherever he happened to be in it, and he had long since stopped bothering to find the ideal seat for listening. He played instead, letting his fingers wander the guitar's neck without any particular destination in mind, and let the day arrange itself around the music.

He felt Arin first, the way he usually did — not as a sight or a sound but as a note resolving, something sharp and decisive off in the tunnels beneath the campus settling into stillness. Milo let a chord ring out to match it, low and quiet, matching the shape of whatever had just finished without knowing what it was. He rarely knew the particulars of these things. He only ever caught the shape they left behind.

Mikel arrived differently, softer, more like a held breath than a resolved note, drawing steadily closer to a place Milo had been quietly watching for weeks now without ever once naming it aloud. He let that note hang unresolved too, an open chord he had no intention of closing yet, and kept playing through both of them at once, the two men's separate afternoons folding into the same song.

He thought of the two of them, sometimes, as instruments in an orchestra too large for either to hear properly — each convinced he played alone, neither able to make out the other section's part from across the hall. Milo could hear both parts. That was most of what made him different from either of them, and he had never found a tidy way to explain it.

He had tried, once or twice over the years, to explain some version of it to someone who seemed like they might listen. It never went especially well. People wanted the explanation to resolve into a fact they could repeat to a friend later, and Milo had never once managed to hand them one. He'd learned to stop trying. Some things were easier carried quietly than explained badly.

---

The discord arrived a few minutes later, and this one he recognized immediately for what it was — not the ordinary friction of two lives moving toward each other, but something deliberately built into the ground beneath the university's oldest library. Patient, low, entirely intentional. Someone had planted it there to catch a mind before it found what it was looking for, to turn a searcher's certainty into doubt exactly when doubt would cost the most.

He'd felt work like it before, always built for the same narrow purpose — not to stop a determined seeker outright, but to slow one down, to seed just enough confusion that the seeker doubted a true path once he finally stood on it. Whoever had built this trap had done competent work, not especially inspired work. But it would do what it was meant to do, and that was often worse than inspiration.

He found himself, not for the first time, faintly irritated on Mikel's behalf — the man had walked into this searching entirely on his own, following threads no one had forced him to pull. But there was a difference between a hard road and a road someone had deliberately booby-trapped.

He let his fingers still against the strings, listening more carefully, searching for whatever single point held the rest of the trap together. He found it soon enough — a single anchoring rune, tucked into the ward's furthest corner, doing the quiet work of keeping the rest of it from coming apart. Everything else about the trap was decoration. That rune was the only part that mattered.

---

A squirrel had settled near the base of his bench sometime during all this, drawn by whatever it was that always drew the local wildlife toward him. It was the same one, he was fairly sure, that had claimed this patch of grass on his last two visits, bold enough now to sit within arm's reach without flinching. "You again," Milo said to it, not looking away from the middle distance. "I don't have anything for you today. Just the noise." The squirrel didn't seem bothered by the distinction, settling in regardless, tail curled around its own feet, and Milo let it stay.

The boy appeared a few minutes after that, small and unsupervised in the particular way of children whose parents had wandered a few benches too far in conversation to notice. He wore a backpack far too large for him, one strap slipping off a shoulder he didn't bother fixing, and stood at a careful, curious distance, watching Milo with an unguarded scrutiny Milo read as belonging to a child too young yet to have learned tact. He glanced twice toward his parents' bench before he worked up whatever courage it required.

"Why do you talk to yourself?" the boy asked, planting his feet with the serious, deliberate stance of someone asking a question he considered extremely important.

Milo didn't stop playing, and didn't look up right away either, letting the boy's question sit a moment inside the melody before he answered it. "I don't talk to myself," he said, at last, with the mild, unbothered patience of a man who'd fielded this exact accusation more times than he could easily count. "I talk to everything that hasn't learned yet not to listen to me."

The boy frowned at that, grave and deliberate in a way Milo found oddly endearing. "That doesn't make sense," he said, though something in the way he said it made it sound more like an invitation than a complaint.

"Most true things don't, the first time you hear them," Milo said, and finally looked up, offering the boy the same easy, unguarded smile he gave most strangers who lingered long enough to earn one. "Give it a few years. It'll make more sense than you'd like it to."

The boy laughed at that, bright and sudden, though nothing in his face suggested he'd followed a word of it. He ran back toward his still-distracted parents a moment later, and Milo watched him go, more or less satisfied. He hadn't been trying to be understood. Understanding could come later, on its own time, if it came at all. The listening was the part that mattered today.

---

He turned his attention back to the anchoring rune once the boy had gone, and let his fingers find the single impossible chord the moment called for — not a shape any teacher could have taught him, not a fingering that belonged to any scale this guitar was built to hold. The chord rang out low and strange across the empty grass, a sound that seemed, for the half-second it lasted, to have more depth than six ordinary strings had any business producing.

Beneath the library, a mile off and out of sight, the rune cracked and went dark, its low patient malice guttering out like a snuffed candle. He felt it happen rather than saw it. The ward didn't vanish. It simply stopped being dangerous — a trap that would still be sitting there tomorrow but would no longer catch anything that walked across it.

---

The chord's last note faded into the evening air, and Milo sat very still afterward, his hand resting flat against the strings to quiet them. It wasn't tiredness. It was closer to homesickness — the sense of having done a small version of something else, somewhere he couldn't quite name.

He didn't reach for it directly. That rarely brought back anything worth having, only the vague sense of a shape where a memory should have been. He let it sit at the edge of him, unexamined. Whatever it was, it didn't frighten him. It only made him feel, for a breath or two, oddly small.

He couldn't have said why a single cracked rune should have stirred anything in him at all. He let the feeling pass through him without chasing it, and by the time his hand left the strings, it had gone quiet again.

---

It was on the park's far side, past the fountain and the last of the wandering students, that something caught his eye — a figure moving between the trees, there and then not there, gone almost before Milo's attention had settled on him. White hair, unhurried gait, a man deep enough in his own thoughts to have stopped noticing his surroundings entirely. He carried something under one arm, a bag or satchel Milo couldn't make out at this distance. Too far to be certain. Too brief to chase.

Milo came half up off the bench before he'd consciously decided to move, the guitar sliding forgotten off his lap, some old instinct straining toward the space where the figure had already vanished. He held himself there a long moment, caught between the pull and everything patient in him that had learned, across more years than he let himself count, what it cost to chase a thing before it was ready to be caught. He sat back down slowly. Soon, perhaps. Not yet.

He gathered the guitar case without his usual care, latching it in three quick motions, and stood a moment longer than he meant to, eyes still fixed on the trees where the white-haired man had disappeared. Arin's resolved note and Mikel's still-open one both sat forgotten now at the edge of his attention, crowded out by the one pull he hadn't let himself follow. He walked out of the park a different way than he'd come, unwilling, tonight, to pass anywhere near the spot where the fountain had briefly shown him someone he still wasn't ready to meet.
