**Patterns checked (only these five — no notes on plot, lore, pacing, or general prose quality):**

1. Narrator asserting a third character's habitual trait/history/certainty as fact, unframed as the POV anchor's inference.
2. "Clearly / plainly / obviously" glued to an unstated third-party interior state, with no evidence shown.
3. The "*the particular/frank/loose [quality] of a [noun] who/that [generalization]*" tic applied to a third party with no inference verb.
4. The "face showed nothing... but it still cost something" contradiction.
5. Any other obvious POV head-hop (narrator claiming access to a scene/thought the anchor could not know).

**Totals:** 15 findings across 10 chapters. 16 chapters clean.

A general note before the per-chapter breakdown: this manuscript's house style leans heavily on interpretive, close-third narration, and in the large majority of cases it is *correctly* anchored — phrases like "Camael read it as," "Gabriel understood, watching him," "Laila read in the slump," "as far as Balam could tell," "or so it seemed to X" appear constantly and do exactly what the style guide wants. The findings below are the minority of cases where that same tic-shaped sentence ("the particular X of a Y who Z") drops the inference verb and lands as flat narrator certainty about someone else's private history or feeling. I did not flag borderline cases where an inference tag was present nearby, where the claim rested on a long-established relationship (e.g., centuries of acquaintance) reasonably justifying familiarity, or where a diegetic mechanic explicitly grants the anchor real access to another mind (the weave-reader in B2C04, Barbas/Dolorim's literal mind-reading in B2C08.5, Malthus's psychic death-impression transferred to Camael in B2C10.5) — those are not head-hops, they're the story's own established rules for perception.

## B2C02 – The Ghost of the Balkans

Anchor: Arin, throughout.

**Finding 1 — Rule 3 (unframed tic).**
- Line 11: *"...sitting on an overturned crate with a bottle in one hand and a rifle he wasn't holding properly in the other, the particular slack posture of someone who had convinced himself that six years of silence meant the debt had quietly expired."*
- The target's specific self-deceiving belief ("convinced himself... the debt had quietly expired") is narrated as fact before Arin has even spoken to him — no "Arin read," "it looked to him like," etc.
- Minimal fix: *"...the particular slack posture Arin read as a man who had convinced himself that six years of silence meant the debt had quietly expired."*

**Finding 2 — Rule 3 (unframed tic).**
- Line 49: *"The man two stools down was already well past sober, the loose, philosophical drunk of someone with nowhere urgent to be and every reason not to want to be sober enough to remember it."*
- A stranger's whole motive for drinking ("every reason not to want to be sober enough to remember it") stated as narrator fact, no hedge.
- Minimal fix: *"...the loose, philosophical drunk of a man who seemed to have nowhere urgent to be and, Arin guessed, every reason not to want to be sober enough to remember it."*

## B2C03 – A Throne of Fractured Light

Anchor: Gabriel, throughout.

**Finding 1 — Rule 1 (narrator certain about a third party's authenticity).**
- Line 19: *"When he did speak, his voice carried none of Uriel's fire or Zadkiel's cold precision — only a tiredness that had nothing performative in it at all."*
- The claim that Raphael's tiredness is *not* performed is presented as narrated fact, not as Gabriel's read — unlike the very well-framed Zadkiel example two lines earlier ("the particular stillness Gabriel had come to associate with...").
- Minimal fix: *"...only a tiredness that struck Gabriel as having nothing performative in it at all."*

**Finding 2 — Rule 1 (unframed certainty about internal state).**
- Line 43: *"Something in her shoulders loosened, fractionally, at that — not relief, exactly, but permission accepted."*
- The physical cue (shoulders loosening) is fair game for Gabriel to observe; "permission accepted" as the specific internal meaning of that cue is narrator diagnosis, not flagged as Gabriel's inference anywhere in the sentence or the one before it.
- Minimal fix: *"...not relief, exactly, but — as Gabriel read it — permission accepted."*

## B2C03.5 – The First Breach

Anchor shifts mid-chapter from Orifiel (opening scenes) to Ezequiel (from the "Ezequiel did not question orders" section on) — consistent with the book's own convention of one anchor per `---`-delimited section, not itself a violation.

**Finding 1 — Rule 1 (habitual trait of a third party stated as fact).**
- Line 47: *"Kaviel set the scroll down with the same unhurried care he gave every relic in his charge."*
- Ezequiel is the anchor here; the claim about Kaviel's habitual care "every relic" is a generalization across time the narrator states flatly, with no "Ezequiel had watched him..." framing anywhere nearby (contrast with the well-framed "Orifiel had come, over that same span, to read a great deal into..." two screens earlier).
- Minimal fix: *"...with what Ezequiel took to be the same unhurried care he gave every relic in his charge."*

## B2C18 – The Path of Laughter

Anchor: Mikel, throughout.

**Finding 1 — Rule 1 (hidden motive stated as fact).**
- Line 19: *"The archivist found him there on the fourth evening, arriving with the pretense of returning a book he hadn't actually requested, and stood in his doorway a long moment before she said what she'd clearly come to say."*
- "Pretense" asserts her private strategy as fact rather than Mikel's inference from her behavior; "clearly come to say" compounds it with an unhedged certainty adverb about her unstated intent.
- Minimal fix: *"...arriving with what Mikel took to be the pretense of returning a book he hadn't actually requested, and stood in his doorway a long moment before saying what she seemed to have come to say."*

**Finding 2 — Rule 5 (explicit head-hop: narrator describes what the anchor did NOT see).**
- Line 51: *"He did not see the musician's fingers still on the strings for one full second as he passed, the song catching and holding on a single unresolved note before picking back up exactly where it had left off. He did not see the look that crossed the musician's face either, gone again almost before it had fully arrived — unreadable, brief, and entirely wasted on a man who never once turned to notice it."*
- This is the cleanest violation in the batch: the text states outright that Mikel did not perceive this, then narrates it anyway in specific sensory detail (the held note, the facial expression) inside a section anchored to Mikel. This may be an intentional dramatic-irony beat (foreshadowing the musician's recognition of Mikel), but as written it breaks the POV contract for this section — the information does not pass through Mikel's senses at all.
- Minimal fix (two options): (a) cut both sentences and end the scene on Mikel simply walking past unaware; or (b) if the beat needs to stay for foreshadowing, move it outside Mikel's anchored section — e.g., a one-line coda after a fresh `---` break with no POV attached, or reserved for a future section actually anchored to the musician.
