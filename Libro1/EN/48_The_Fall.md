# Chapter 48 — The Fall

Miguel drove Solmire forward in a strike that should have ended the exchange outright, and felt Lamentum turn it aside with a precision that matched his own blow exactly, neither yielding so much as an inch of ground. Blow answered blow, parry answered parry, the two of them locked in a rhythm so perfectly matched that Miguel could not, in the brief spaces between exchanges, identify any advantage either weapon had managed to claim.

He drove forward again, testing a different angle, and found the same maddening precision waiting to meet it. Belial's footwork gave away nothing — no sign of fatigue, no sign his commitment had wavered — every parry arriving exactly where it needed to with a consistency that suggested a mastery of the spear Miguel hadn't expected this early in Belial's claim to her. Whatever training Belial had undergone since the Weeping Sepulcher, it showed in every exchange passing between them.

He had expected, walking onto this dead plain, that one of them would eventually break the other's guard through sheer accumulated pressure. Neither guard broke. The two weapons met and parted and met again in a stalemate that showed no sign of resolving through force alone, and Miguel understood, somewhere beneath the exertion of it, that whatever finally decided this duel wasn't going to be decided by strength.

The ash beneath their feet had churned into a fine grey dust by the dozenth exchange, kicked loose by footwork too fast and too evenly matched for either to gain ground. Miguel felt his own arms begin to protest the sustained effort, a fatigue more physical, this time, than anything the sword's influence had added to the fight so far. Belial, across the narrowing distance between them, showed no more sign of tiring than Miguel did, both locked into an endurance test neither weapon seemed inclined to resolve on its own.

He had fought longer engagements than this one, had learned across centuries of combat to read the exact moment an opponent's guard began, however subtly, to falter. He read nothing of the kind in Belial tonight. Whatever weakness might decide this duel, Miguel had begun to realize, wasn't going to announce itself through his opponent's blade — not while the spear kept turning aside every strike with the same maddening precision as the last.

A brief lull opened between exchanges, both drawing back to reset their footing, and in that narrow space of silence Miguel felt something rise in him that the spear had played no part in summoning. He remembered, without seeking it out, the cold and absolute weight he had felt the first time he drew Solmire from her resting place — a weight that had lodged in his hand and, in all the time since, never fully released its hold.

He asked himself, in the fraction of a second the lull afforded him, whether he had made a single decision since that first drawing that had actually been his own. The question arrived unbidden and unwelcome, and he buried it as quickly as it surfaced, forcing his attention back to the fight before it could finish forming into something he'd have to answer.

He thought, in the same brief instant, of an order he had given long before this war took its current shape, a command he had issued with a certainty he no longer trusted had truly been his own to give. He hadn't examined that memory closely in longer than he cared to count. It surfaced now anyway, uninvited, alongside every quieter moment since where his own conviction had felt, in retrospect, less like conviction and more like something borrowed from a source he'd never thought to question.

It was not yet the doubt that would break him. It was only the old crack, the one that had lived quietly in him since that order and every silence that followed it, finding room now, in the silence of combat, to show itself for the first time. He reset his stance before it could widen further, forcing his attention back onto Belial's next strike, and told himself, without much conviction, that whatever had surfaced could wait until this duel was decided.

Belial felt the flicker pass through Miguel before any outward sign of it reached his blade, a predator's long-honed reflex for weakness sharpening his attention the way it always had against any opponent whose guard had briefly, invisibly, wavered. He did not press the attack physically. Miguel reset his stance a half-second slower than the exchange before, and Belial read the delay as exactly what he suspected — a sign that whatever had just moved through his opponent belonged to a battlefield more useful to fight on than the physical one.

He had studied Miguel across this entire war, had watched him from a distance long before either of them ever crossed blades directly, and had noted, across that long observation, a quality to Miguel's certainty that had always struck him as slightly too polished to be entirely genuine. Tonight's flicker confirmed what he had long suspected without proof. Whatever conviction Miguel wielded Solmire with, some part of it had never fully belonged to the man holding her, and Belial intended to find out how much of Miguel's guard that borrowed conviction had been propping up.

He had won duels before through patience rather than force, had learned long ago that the most decisive blow a warrior could land often had nothing to do with the weapon in his hand. Tonight offered him exactly that kind of opening, and he had no intention of squandering it by returning too quickly to the cruder exchange of blade against blade.

He lowered Lamentum fractionally, not in retreat but in something closer to deliberate patience, the way a hunter lowers a blade once the prey has already stopped running. Words, he understood, would do more damage here than another physical exchange, however evenly matched that exchange had proven so far.

Belial held his stance. "This power was never yours." The words carried nothing of a shout, and everything of a simple, devastating observation.

Miguel held Solmire aloft, and answered — not with the confidence Miguel had displayed earlier in the war, but with something closer to a prayer than an assertion. "Then tell me whose it is."

Belial said nothing further. He let the silence do the work the crack already visible in Miguel had begun on its own, understanding, with the same cold precision he brought to any calculated strike, that no answer he could give would land with half the force of the question left standing unanswered in the air between them.

The words landed in Miguel with a weight Lamentum's grief only amplified, pressing directly against fears he had spent this entire war refusing to examine. He had told himself, across every battle since Solmire first came into his hand, that the sword answered to him, that whatever verdicts she rendered carried his own judgment behind them. Belial's four words, arriving in the wake of the crack that had already opened in him moments earlier, offered him nothing left to hide that belief behind.

He tried, for one last desperate instant, to summon the same certainty that had carried him through every previous crisis of this war, the same conviction that had once made wielding her feel like an extension of his own will rather than a burden imposed on it. It did not come. In its place arrived only the plain, unanswerable question his own reply to Belial had already voiced aloud — if this power was never his, whose had it been all along, and what did that make every verdict he had ever delivered in her name.

He felt his own certainty give way beneath him, not in a single collapse but in a slow, total unraveling that carried every victory he'd ever credited to his own conviction down with it. He thought of Zaphor'el, of the stragglers the blade had erased against his own screamed refusal, of every moment since where he had told himself the blade still answered to him rather than the other way around. Each memory arrived now stripped of whatever comfort he had once found in it, offering only further confirmation of the accusation currently unmaking him.

Solmire's hum in his grip did not steady him. It only confirmed, in the same silent language she had always spoken, that Belial's accusation had landed exactly where it was aimed. Miguel's guard, held firm through every previous exchange of this duel, finally, fully broke, his arms losing whatever certainty had been holding it steady only moments earlier.

Belial saw the opening the instant it appeared, Miguel's stance faltering in a way no physical wound had managed to produce across their entire exchange, and did not hesitate to close the distance. He had waited for exactly this moment across every prior exchange of the duel, patient in a way that had cost him more restraint than the fight itself had cost him effort, and found no hesitation left in himself now that the wait had paid off.

Lamentum drove forward in a single, decisive thrust, and Miguel's guard, his stance already seeming to Belial like it had been shattered by something more than the blow itself, offered nothing left to meet it with.

The strike landed with a symbolic weight Belial felt clearly even through his own exhaustion, a blow that broke more than the body currently absorbing it. Something in the air around them shifted at the moment of impact, an old pillar of celestial order cracking somewhere far beyond this single plain, and Belial understood, watching Miguel stagger beneath the strike's full weight, that he had just broken something no simple victory over a single opponent should have been capable of breaking.

He held his position a moment longer than strictly necessary, letting the aftermath settle — a blow that had cost him nearly as much as it had evidently cost Miguel, every wound absorbed across this duel now making itself felt in full. His own arm ached from the strike's final delivery, a deep, bone-level fatigue worse than anything the earlier exchanges had cost him, and he allowed himself, for just a moment, to feel the full weight of how close this duel had run before it finally broke in his favor.

He had won cleaner victories across the length of his existence, had rarely needed to spend this much of himself against a single opponent. Tonight's cost, he judged as Miguel's knees finally buckled, had bought him something larger, by far, than a single soldier's defeat, and he found the exchange, weighed against everything it had cost him, worth every measure of the price.

Miguel went down slowly, Solmire slipping from fingers that appeared, to Belial's eye, unable to hold her any longer, her light guttering and finally dying against the ash-grey ground the moment she left his grip. Belial stood over him, breathing hard, every wound his own body had absorbed across the length of the duel making itself fully known now that the exchange had finally, decisively ended.

He allowed himself, standing there amid the wreckage of a duel that had cost him nearly as much as it had cost Miguel, a single triumphant smile — not broad, not gloating, simply the quiet satisfaction of a warrior who had finally proven, beyond any argument either weapon could still make, exactly which of them the field belonged to.

He lowered Lamentum slowly, the spear's grief settling into something closer to a satisfied stillness than the raw ache it had carried through most of the duel, and looked out across the plain toward whatever remained of both their scattered forces still watching from its distant edges. Neither side had moved from the plain's edge across the entire duel, both armies still, as if held by some unspoken agreement neither had ever needed to voice aloud, and Belial understood, scanning the distant, silent ranks, that every soldier on both sides had just watched their champion's fate decided in full view. He had no wish to announce the outcome. The field itself, empty and silent around the two of them, would announce it clearly enough once word traveled.

Miguel lay motionless on the ash-grey ground, Solmire dark and silent beside him, and the plain that had swallowed a previous battle's aftermath long before either of them arrived now swallowed the ending of this one just as completely.
