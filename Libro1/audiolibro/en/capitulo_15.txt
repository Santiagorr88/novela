Chapter 15. The Price of Truth.

The battle broke exactly where Belial had built it to break, Aamon's legion crashing into the angelic patrol with the same reckless hunger for glory that had carried Aamon into this trap. Celestial light and demonic fire tangled across the crossing, chaos convincing enough to hold every eye.

Blades met in a rising din of steel and screaming, the ground already churned to mud under a hundred boots on either side. A wounded angel went down near Belial's flank, wings scattering light across the dirt, and vanished beneath the advancing line before Belial spared him a second glance — one more body this deception would cost before the night was through. Demons pressed forward in close ranks, Aamon's banner still flying near the center of the press, every soldier on both sides certain this fight was the whole of what tonight demanded.

Fire from the demon lines lit the crossing in fitful orange bursts, answered each time by a flare of celestial light, the two colors chasing each other across the field without either winning ground for long. Screams rose and fell, swallowed fast by the general roar. An angelic banner-bearer went down hard off to Belial's right, his standard toppling into the mud under the press of bodies behind him. Two demon soldiers dragged a wounded angel to the ground a few strides further on, blades rising and falling before either side could answer, gone again into the press before the angel's body had stopped moving. The mud grew slicker by the minute, churned by boots and blood, sucking at heels, slowing whole formations. None of it was real, not in the way that mattered. It was theater, built to occupy every eye that might otherwise wander toward the ground Belial actually needed them not to look at.

Aamon fought at the center of that chaos with a disciplined ferocity that, watching, Belial judged his reputation had not exaggerated — his blade tearing through the angelic line with the brutal precision that had built his name across centuries of campaigns. He carved a path forward with each swing, wrenching the serrated blade free of one body to bury it in the next, roaring something wordless and triumphant that carried over the din. He never glanced down at the ground he stood on, or sideways at the commander riding beside him. Belial kept close enough to watch without appearing to watch, his mace heavy at his hip, matching Aamon's pace without ever quite drawing even with it, counting down the last moments of a plan that had stopped belonging to anyone but him.

...

The angelic captain leading the patrol caught something in the shape of the demonic formation that read wrong to his trained eye — a discipline too precise for an ambush thrown together on short notice — and shouted a command down his own line before the thought had fully formed: regroup, close the right flank before it stretched too thin to hold.

His voice cut clean through the noise, sharp with the kind of urgency soldiers obeyed on instinct. Nearby angels broke off at once, wings snapping wide as they wheeled toward the flank he'd named, blades lowering into a defensive line that might, given a few more seconds, have held. He'd led enough patrols to trust the wrongness he'd felt in the demons' formation, and allowed himself one brief, grim satisfaction at seeing his line answer so cleanly. His soldiers began the maneuver at once — a genuine chance to blunt whatever was coming. Something shifted near the enemy commander's position that the captain would never see, and everything he had just ordered became irrelevant before his soldiers could finish the turn.

...

Belial's own honor guard broke from the angelic line the moment his signal reached them, wheeling as one body away from the angels they'd been fighting and directly into Aamon's personal guard instead — soldiers he'd chosen for this precise role weeks earlier, drilled privately in a maneuver none of them had understood the purpose of until tonight.

The reversal took less than a heartbeat to land, and considerably longer for anyone else on the field to understand. For one suspended moment the whole battle seemed to hang on the same confused breath, blades frozen mid-swing as both sides tried to make sense of an enemy that had just, impossibly, multiplied. Angels who had braced for demons found demons cutting into other demons beside them, and the angelic line wavered and broke into confusion. Some pressed the sudden advantage, cutting hard into the exposed flank; others simply froze, blades lowered, eyes darting between two sets of demons now tearing into each other. Aamon's own guard fell in moments beneath blades that had been fighting beside them seconds before — a captain cut down mid-shout before he could name what was happening; two soldiers still turned toward the angels they'd been holding off when their former allies' blades found them instead. One of the honor guard's lieutenants drove three of Aamon's men back against a crumbling ridge until none of them had anywhere left to retreat, cutting them down one after another. Belial moved through the widening gap with the certainty of a man walking a path he'd already memorized, stepping over the fallen without slowing, his mace still clean at his hip — none of this violence had required him to lift it himself.

He found Aamon staggering at the center of the collapsing battle, already wounded, blood streaking down one arm from a wound his own guard's blades had opened, his eyes darting across the ruin of his line as though it made no sense to him yet. Belial drove him back, step by step, his mace turning aside the serrated blade in hard, deliberate blocks, herding him without appearing to herd him toward a stretch of ground marked with a sigil no ordinary soldier would have read as anything but scorched earth. He had chosen this ground weeks ago, walked it alone at night to fix its exact position, and every strike he traded with Aamon now only pushed the wounded commander closer to the spot.

Aamon fought back with everything his wound still allowed, his blade swinging in wide, furious arcs that Belial turned aside one after another, giving ground exactly as much as he needed and not a step more. Each parry rang out sharp over the dying noise of the wider battle, the two of them now isolated in a pocket of quiet neither side seemed inclined to cross into. Aamon's eyes found the sigil beneath his own feet and widened, his stance faltering for the first time since the fight began.

"What is this," he demanded, his voice cracking between the question and the shout he seemed to want it to be, the blade still raised though his grip had visibly loosened.

Belial began the invocation without waiting for him to finish understanding, words drawn from a text forbidden to any demon below Commander rank, each syllable landing with a weight that thickened the air around them. The ground beneath Aamon's feet answered first, a sickly purple light rising through the sigil's carved lines to wrap around his legs. He tried to step clear of it and found his boots would no longer answer him, rooted to ground that had held steady under him for centuries and chose, tonight, not to.

"For power, Aamon," Belial said, low enough that only the two of them would ever hear it. "The price must always be paid."

Aamon lunged at him anyway, wounded and roaring, his blade carving one last desperate arc toward Belial's throat with a strength that startled him despite everything he already knew about the man he was killing. Belial ducked under the swing, close enough to feel the wind of it pass over his shoulder, and answered with a single, decisive blow, the mace's head connecting hard enough to fold Aamon's knees and drop him onto the glowing carved lines.

The seal cracked open beneath them with a sound like a mourner's cry, and Aamon's essence — that vast demonic weight Belial had calculated so carefully in the loyalty rolls — began to pour into the widening crack, thin streams of dark light unspooling from his chest, faster with every second, as if the crack itself had grown hungrier. The mace's spiked head drove into the center of Aamon's black heart a moment later. No blood answered the strike, only a torrent of raw demonic power, ripped loose and drawn down into the sigil's hungry light. Aamon's body went still within seconds, the fury gone out of it entirely, his eyes fixed open on nothing. Belial straightened over him and let out a long breath he hadn't noticed himself holding, the mace's head still smoking faintly where it had struck.

Belial rose from the body slowly, blood-streaked and breathing hard, and found the portal to the Weeping Sepulcher already glowing where the sigil had stood a moment before, patient in a way nothing else on this battlefield had been. Its light was cold, threaded through with the same sickly purple that had climbed Aamon's legs, and it pulsed once, slow and deliberate, as if acknowledging that the price had finally been paid in full. A faint draft moved from its edge, carrying a smell like old stone and older grief, cool against skin still slick with battle-sweat.

Around him, the surviving angels were already pulling back from a fight that no longer resembled anything they'd marched out expecting, their retreat carrying the haste of soldiers who had just understood, too late to act on it, that something far worse than an ambush had been unfolding beneath their feet. The captain who had shouted his useless order minutes earlier was among them, dragging a wounded soldier toward the rear, his eyes finding Belial across the churned battlefield for one long moment before he turned and kept running. What remained of Aamon's legion stood scattered and leaderless, some still fighting out of habit, others wearing the stillness of soldiers beginning to understand their commander was never coming back. A few had already begun drifting toward the rear in loose, uncertain knots, no one senior enough among them to call an organized retreat.

Somewhere behind him, the last scattered pockets of fighting were dying out on their own, neither side finding much reason to keep swinging at an enemy that had stopped pressing forward. Belial paid it no further attention. He stood over Aamon's body one last moment, breathing hard, victorious, dark power still fading from the mace's head and blood cooling on his own hands, then turned from it without another glance. He crossed the last few paces of the battlefield without looking back, the mace still in hand, and stood at last before the threshold he had spent so long building toward, looking into the waiting portal without crossing it. The search that had brought him this far was, at last, within reach.
