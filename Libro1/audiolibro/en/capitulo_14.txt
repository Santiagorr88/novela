Chapter 14. Dreams of Laughter.

Miguel had not slept well since he first closed his hand around the sword's hilt, and tonight exhaustion finally won. The nights before this one had followed the same shape: an hour or two of shallow rest, then the long wakeful stretch before dawn where he lay still and waited for morning to make it official. Tonight his body refused to keep arguing. He knelt beside his bed first, as he always did, and prayed for a vision — a sign, however small, that the path he walked was still the one he was meant to walk. He asked for clarity, plainly, in the same words he had always used, hoping their familiarity might carry more weight than the words alone.

The prayer felt worn down by repetition, closer to habit than devotion. He had always trusted this part of each day without question — kneel, ask, and whatever came back would be enough. Tonight the need for an answer bordered on desperation, and he felt it even as he shaped the words.

Sleep took him somewhere in the middle of that prayer, the words trailing off into a darkness that did not feel like rest. Something began to take shape almost immediately, and even half-submerged in sleep, Miguel felt a current of resistance rise in him before he knew what he was resisting. Nothing about the shape forming ahead of him matched anything he had ever been taught to call sacred. He fought, briefly and without much success, against the pull to keep watching it.

He had prayed for a vision, and some sleeping part of him understood that whatever this was, it had not come in answer to that prayer. It felt older than any answer Heaven had ever given him, and entirely uninterested in whether he wanted to receive it. He tried to turn himself away from it, to will himself back toward ordinary dreamless dark. The pull only tightened, patient and unhurried, as though it had already decided the resisting would stop.

He could see no one. Only a presence, unseen, somewhere close in the darkness. What reached him instead was sound — a laugh, low and unhurried, present in the darkness the way a held breath is present before it's released.

It was careless. Almost cruel, if he let himself name it honestly — a sound with no reverence in it at all, nothing that resembled anything he had ever been taught to associate with the sword.

He knew that laugh. He had heard some fractured echo of it once before, on the shadow-plane, in the instant his own hand had turned briefly into someone else's — pale, slender, gripping his hilt with a cruel, laughing glint he had spent every night since trying not to remember. He had told himself it was nothing, a trick of exhaustion and guilt on a mind strained past its limits. The dream offered him no such comfort tonight. It offered no words to explain what it meant, and it didn't need to. Something in him understood, without being told, that Solmire had not come into being through any holy war. It had come into being for pleasure alone — anarchic, indifferent, answerable to nothing.

The laugh went on a long time, without faltering, without deepening into anything more solemn. Miguel kept waiting for it to change, for some note of purpose or reverence to make sense of what he was hearing. It never came. Whoever stood behind that laughter was simply enjoying themselves.

Miguel woke in his own chambers, drenched in a cold sweat that had soaked through the linens beneath him, the ghost of that laughter still ringing in his ears the way a struck bell keeps ringing after the hand has withdrawn.

He lay still a long moment before he trusted himself to move, staring at the dark ceiling until his breathing settled toward ordinary. His hands, when he finally looked at them, were only his own — steady, familiar in the low light. The relief that gave him felt almost embarrassing in its intensity, gratitude for something so basic he had never before thought to be grateful for.

Solmire rested in its stand across the room, its light banked low and quiet, and Miguel found he could not look at it the way he once had. It had always sat there as a gift, the clearest proof he owned of Heaven's trust in him. Tonight it sat there as something else — an object with a history he had never asked to know, and now could not stop knowing. He crossed the room toward it, though he couldn't have said what he meant to do once he arrived, and stood over the blade without touching it, studying its familiar lines as though a closer look might reveal what the dream had only hinted at. Nothing in its shape had changed. It caught the low light as it always had, patient and radiant, giving him no answer at all. He had trusted this blade without reservation for as long as he'd carried it. Standing over it now, he could not remember when that trust had started to cost him effort to maintain.

A soft knock came at his door not long after, hesitant in a way Gabriel's knock never was. He opened it to find his brother standing in the corridor, still dressed as though he had come straight from his own chambers.

"I felt something," Gabriel said, his voice careful, pitched low. "Through what's left between us. I didn't know if it meant I should come."

Miguel thought the admission had cost him something; he heard it in how long Gabriel let the silence sit before finding the rest of the sentence.

They stood there a moment, neither finding the next thing to say, the space between them oddly formal for two brothers who had once shared everything without asking permission. Not so many years ago, Gabriel would simply have walked in and sat at the foot of the bed, and Miguel would have let him without a second thought. Gabriel's eyes moved once, briefly, past Miguel's shoulder toward the disturbed sheets, then returned to his face without comment. Whatever bond still ran between them, thinned by months of unnamed distance, had carried enough of tonight's terror across its fraying thread to bring his brother to this door in the middle of the night.

Gabriel did not ask directly what Miguel had dreamed. He stood in the doorway instead, searching Miguel's face for an opening he didn't seem to know how to request outright.

Miguel did not offer him one. "It was nothing," he said, the words flatter and colder than he'd intended, a door closing in tone before it closed in fact. Something shifted in Gabriel's expression — not surprise, more as if he'd already suspected this distance and had hoped to find it smaller than he feared. "Get some rest, Gabriel. I'm sorry to have troubled you."

Gabriel held his gaze a moment longer, as if weighing whether to press past the closed door Miguel had just set between them. "You know you can tell me," he said, quietly, an offer made without much confidence it would be accepted.

Miguel said nothing further. Then Gabriel nodded once, a small, resigned motion, and stepped back into the corridor. He left without another word. Miguel shut the door himself, gently, and stood with his hand against the wood long after his brother's footsteps had faded, aware of exactly how much he had just chosen not to say.

Alone again, Miguel was walking before he'd made any clear decision to walk at all, his feet carrying him toward the wing where Raphael kept his chambers before his mind caught up to where he was going. The corridors at this hour stood empty and quiet, lit by the low glow that never fully dimmed anywhere in the Bastion, and he moved through them with none of his usual certainty.

Raphael was the only one who had ever questioned the blade aloud, months ago, a quiet unease Miguel had dismissed at the time as a healer's excessive caution. Raphael had asked whether a sword like this was truly meant to carry the whole weight of Heaven's justice, and Miguel had answered with more certainty than he'd actually felt. He remembered the conversation clearly — the careful way Raphael chose his words, testing the ground, and the flicker of disappointment Miguel had caught in his face when the reassurance came too quickly to sound earned.

He had told himself since then that the conversation ended cleanly, that Raphael's doubt had been answered and set aside. Standing two corridors from that same door tonight, Miguel knew it had never been answered at all. He had simply spoken with enough authority to make the question feel closed, and Raphael had let it lie rather than push against a certainty Miguel wore like armor. He stopped two corridors short of Raphael's door, his hand halfway raised. Something in him — pride, or the colder fear that Raphael had been right from the beginning — refused to let his feet carry him the rest of the way.

He tried to imagine the conversation anyway, standing in the empty corridor with his hand still raised. He would knock, and Raphael would open the door already half-expecting trouble, the way healers so often did. He would have to say it aloud — you were right, I heard the laughter, I know now what you always suspected — and once said, it could never be unsaid the way Raphael's original question had been quietly set aside. Miguel had spent his whole service being the one his brothers came to when the ground felt uncertain. He did not know how to reverse that direction, even now, even two corridors from the one door where reversing it might have helped.

He stood a long while in the empty corridor, the dream's laughter still faint beneath his own thoughts, then turned back the way he had come, taking his silence with him. The walk back to his own chambers felt longer than the walk here had been, though the distance itself hadn't changed.
