Chapter 16. The First Threshold.

Belial crossed the portal and felt the mud and sulfur of the front dissolve at his back with the speed of a page turned in the dark. In a single stride, reality narrowed to a silence so flat and dense it began to press against his ears with the physical weight of a slab of cold iron. The Weeping Sepulcher received him without banners or challenge, with a glacial indifference that made plain his arrival, like his inevitable departure, would change nothing in the stone.

The ground beneath his boots was a pavement of black crystal, uneven and broken, giving back a tremulous chime with every step — a sound too close to an orphan's sob for Belial's taste, dull, constant, and without a source he could trace. The air came thick with rancid brine and a cold that clung to his lungs with the taste of old copper and spent lime. A low, colorless haze hung over the flagstones, throwing misshapen shadows that refused to follow the ordinary physics of light. His black armor, usually alive, drew its plates inward with a defensive hiss, cooling until the iron began to bite at the skin of his shoulders, reminding him he had entered a realm whose laws made no allowance for his comfort.

He had not walked twenty paces along the crystal fissure when a cold pang flowered behind his sternum, sharp and physical enough that his hand moved on its own toward the pommel of his mace, hunting a wound that wasn't there. It was a single beat of pure, distilled pain, gone as quickly as it had come, leaving a bitter taste at the back of his tongue. Belial straightened with a low growl, forcing his spine back into the rigidity of command, and turned toward the pull's source with the stubbornness of a general who treated danger as nothing more than another tactical variable.

The black crystal path began to narrow, forcing him to shorten his stride. When he tried to force the pace back to his old metronomic rhythm, the stone answered him in its own cold language. A sharp crystal edge caught the side of his sabaton, tearing the thick leather with a dry shriek. A step later his heel sank into a seam that gave just enough to cost him his balance. Correcting his stance, his boots kicked loose a handful of black shards that scattered across the pavement with a chime that sounded exactly like a gasp swallowed by the void. Belial cursed under his breath, wiped the gray dust from his breastplate, and pressed on without slowing, filing the ground's warnings away as nothing more than the nuisance of terrain that hadn't yet learned to obey him.

The passage broke open, all at once, into a circular clearing, and there, waiting for him at the center of that stillness, rose the guardian. It did not descend from a sky of ash, nor cut its way through the stone; it gathered itself from the black crystal floor, accumulating shards and solid tears the way a heavy tide gathers before it breaks against a cliff. It was an immense shape, vaguely humanoid, but stripped of the ordinary symmetry of flesh.

The creature took no guarding stance. Its black crystal limbs hung at its sides with a stillness so devoid of threat that Belial, accustomed to reading tension in an enemy's shoulders before the first strike, found himself unable to calculate its reach. When the guardian spoke, the voice came not from a single physical throat but from a thousand slabs vibrating at once, a slow, mineral cadence that filled the clearing without the air ever moving.

"Nothing is taken here," said the voice, and the sound carried with it a taste of spent lime. "Only given."

Belial gripped Dubium with both hands, feeling the honest weight of its iron, and his smile recovered the edge of contempt he had polished in the courts of Dis.

"Then give it to me," Belial answered, and the volume of his own words came out thin, orphaned of echo, in the silence of the Sepulcher.

The guardian did not answer with words; it answered with the first blow.

It was a movement impossible for a structure of that size. A fist of black crystal, cold and dense, connected full against Belial's left shoulder with the force of a siege catapult, throwing him several steps back across the cracked pavement. The impact splintered the pauldron of his living armor and pain bloomed hot through the joint, but beneath the pain, carried in on the same blow, came the sorrow. A cold gust of pure loss — the feeling of watching a banner fall into mud that no longer belongs to anyone — slipped through the broken seam of his plates, chilling his blood before he could clench his teeth against it.

Belial roared, shaking the mist from his chest, and swung Dubium up in a rising arc aimed at the guardian's knee. The iron struck with a dry crack that should have shattered the crystal, but the stone absorbed the violence without fracturing; no shards flew, only a dull vibration that climbed his arm and left the fingers of his right hand numb. He struck again, faster, more furious, applying the same arithmetic of force that had won him a hundred skirmishes against the rivals of Dis, and each time he found the same firm, deaf, patient resistance that seemed to digest his haste.

The guardian's third blow caught him across the ribs with the sound of old wood splitting. Belial hit the ground on his back, the air torn from his lungs in a sour gasp, Dubium sliding out of reach across the flat crystal. This time the sorrow did not withdraw. It flooded his broken ribs like a tide of spent lime, undeniable, heavy with the grief of a hand opening again and again to let go of something irreplaceable, never once allowed to close around it again. He lay there, cheek pressed to the cold crystal, swallowing saliva that tasted of brine and his own hot blood, understanding in the silence that this was not an execution, but a correction.

The guardian did not press its advantage. It withdrew with the same slow, metronomic pace it had approached with, returning to the center of the clearing to dissolve back into the pavement, letting its outline blur until the stone lay flat and still once more. Its last words hung in the flat air, stripped of triumph or cruelty:

"Nothing is taken here."

Belial stayed down a moment longer than his general's pride would have liked, his left hand pressed against bruised ribs that throbbed with every ragged breath. There was no one there to witness his fall, no courtier of Dis before whom he'd have to dress up the humiliation, but the sting of defeat still tasted of ash in his throat. He rose slowly, wincing at the cold pull in his side, and dragged his boots to where Dubium lay, its head still faintly warm.

He weighed the iron in his palm, looking at the empty slab, and understood that brute force would win him not one further inch on this ground. The Weeping Sepulcher operated under a logic his long military war had never once required him to learn, and accepting that truth, for a man who had spent ages taking what he wanted through the pure violence of his will, turned out to be the deepest wound of all. He holstered the mace at his hip, swallowing a fury he had no idea how to spend against stone, and pushed deeper into the passage, the chime of the crystal following his slower steps like a choir that had finally found its measure.
