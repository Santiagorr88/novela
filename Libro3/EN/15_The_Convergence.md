# Chapter 15 — The Convergence

The canyon walls threw the midday heat back at Michael in waves he'd stopped minding somewhere around the second week of this particular training ground, sweat running freely down his back as he brought Solmire up to meet Ereloth's borrowed blade for the hundredth time that morning. He had trained in a hundred different terrains across a life that no longer felt like it belonged to someone else, and had never once found a teacher less interested in the terrain itself than Ereloth currently seemed.

The lessons themselves had stopped resembling lessons somewhere around the first week, less a matter of stance and footwork than of learning to notice things he'd spent centuries training himself not to notice at all — the particular weight of a held breath, the exact moment a decision made itself before the mind caught up to it. He had been skeptical of the method at first, had said as much more than once. Ereloth had simply smiled and told him skepticism was a fine thing to bring to breakfast, provided he left it there.

This morning's drill had started ordinarily enough, the same patient repetition Ereloth favored over anything resembling a real fight, blade meeting blade in a rhythm too even to count as combat by any standard Michael's old training would have recognized. He had stopped questioning the rhythm somewhere around the fiftieth exchange, letting his body settle into it the way water settles into whatever shape finally holds it.

"Again," Ereloth said, already resetting his stance, grinning the particular grin he wore whenever he intended to make a lesson out of something Michael hadn't yet noticed was a lesson.

Michael obliged him, and felt it the instant their blades met — not the clean, cold judgment Solmire had always hummed with in his hand before, that familiar note of absolute verdict he'd carried since the day he first lifted the sword from wherever the world had kept it waiting for him. This was warmer. It ran through the blade like a low current finding its way toward something specific, a longing rather than a verdict, pulling gently but insistently in a direction he hadn't consciously chosen to face.

He lowered the sword, more startled by the change than by anything Ereloth's blade had managed to do to him all morning.

"You felt it," Ereloth said, not a question.

---

"It's pulling," Michael said, turning the blade slightly to study it, as though a different angle might explain the change better than simply feeling it had. "Toward something. It's never done that before."

"Of course it hasn't." Ereloth sheathed his own blade without ceremony and dropped onto a flat rock at the canyon's edge, looking entirely too pleased with himself for a man who'd just watched his student's weapon start behaving strangely. "It spent a very long time humming a single, narrow note. Judgment doesn't leave much room for wanting things. Now that it remembers it was forged for something warmer than verdicts, it's finally allowed to want something too." He tilted his head, studying Michael with an expression that managed to be both entirely serious and entirely unbothered at once. "Feel it pulling anywhere specific, or just pulling?"

"Specific," Michael admitted. "I don't know toward what."

"You don't need to know toward what. You just need to stop arguing with it long enough to follow." Ereloth stood, stretched with the exaggerated care of a man twice his actual age, and set off down the canyon without waiting to see whether Michael intended to follow.

Michael sheathed Solmire and fell into step beside him, more unsettled by the change than he'd let on. He had spent his entire remembered existence trusting the blade's judgment the way a man trusts his own hands, certain of its verdicts even when the verdicts themselves cost him something to carry out. He had never once had to wonder whether it wanted something for itself. The wondering sat strangely in him now, not unwelcome exactly, but unfamiliar enough that he checked the hilt's warmth against his palm every few dozen steps, half expecting it to fade back into the old, familiar coldness if he simply waited long enough.

It didn't fade. If anything, it grew steadier the further they walked, a current settling into something closer to certainty than the vague pull it had started as.

"Do you feel it too?" Michael asked, still matching Ereloth's stride.

"Feeling it?" Ereloth laughed, the sound bouncing strangely off the canyon walls. "Brother, I'm *dancing* to it."

---

He wasn't exaggerating, Michael realized within the first hour of walking. Ereloth navigated the broken country ahead of them with a confidence that had nothing to do with sight lines or the sun's position or any of the ordinary methods a sensible traveler used to keep from getting lost. He changed direction at odd, unmarked points in the terrain, humming under his breath in a register Michael couldn't quite place, occasionally spinning on one heel before setting off again at a fresh angle entirely.

"You're not navigating," Michael said, after the fourth such turn. "You're improvising."

"I'm listening," Ereloth corrected, entirely unbothered by the accusation. "There's a rhythm to things that are broken and long to be whole again. Once you've heard it clearly enough, you don't need a map. You just need to stop stepping on the beat wrong." He glanced back over his shoulder, grin softening for just a moment into something closer to genuine warmth. "You're getting better at hearing it yourself. I can tell. You stopped fighting the pull about a mile back."

Michael hadn't noticed the exact moment that had happened, but found, testing the observation against his own memory of the last hour, that it was true. He'd stopped consulting the sun. He'd stopped trying to reason his way toward a destination he had no actual information about. He was simply walking, the warm current in Solmire's hilt guiding his feet with a confidence he hadn't earned through any deliberate effort of his own. It felt, oddly, less like following an order than like remembering the way home to a place he'd never actually visited before.

They stopped for water at midday, in the thin shade of an outcropping barely wide enough to shelter them both, and Ereloth studied him for a long moment before speaking again. "You keep looking like a man waiting for someone to tell him he's doing it wrong," he said. "Nobody's going to tell you that. There's no wrong way to follow this particular current. There's only following it, or not."

"Old habits," Michael admitted.

"Centuries of them, from what I understand." Ereloth's grin returned, softer than before. "We'll work on that too, eventually. One impossible thing at a time."

"Where are we going?" he asked, mostly for the pleasure of hearing Ereloth refuse to give him a straight answer.

"Somewhere I haven't been in longer than I'd like to admit," Ereloth said, and for once, offered nothing further to soften the honesty of it.

---

They made camp that night in the lee of a rock formation old enough to have weathered into something almost gentle, and Michael sat apart from the fire for a while after the meal, letting the day's walking settle into something he could actually examine. He closed his eyes, not quite meaning to meditate, and found the stillness arriving anyway, uninvited and complete.

The vision came without warning. A figure walked through a landscape of ash and quiet despair, pale, cold fire wrapped close around a form Michael recognized before he'd consciously placed why he recognized it — Thaeriel, unmistakable even reduced to this thin, distant impression, moving with the same unhurried, absolute purpose Michael remembered from every account he'd ever read of the Judge's long and solitary career. He had expected, watching that cold fire cross the ash, to feel the old rivalry he'd always carried toward Thaeriel's particular flavor of judgment — a verdict with no appeal built into it, no doubt permitted to soften its edges, the kind of absolute certainty Michael had once measured his own against and found wanting on more than one occasion. He felt instead a wave of something closer to grief, sharp and unexpected, kinship arriving in a shape he hadn't known he was capable of feeling for a man he'd never actually met.

He sat with the fading edges of the vision a while after his eyes opened, unwilling to let it go entirely before he'd examined what it had actually shown him. He had crossed paths with Thaeriel's reputation more than once across his long service, had read the reports of cities left standing straighter for his passing through them and the reports, less often spoken of, of the particular loneliness that seemed to follow a man who judged for a living. He had never expected to feel grief on the Judge's behalf. He examined the feeling now, honestly, and found it entirely genuine.

He opened his eyes to find Ereloth watching him across the fire, no trace of his usual performance left in his expression.

"You saw him," Ereloth said quietly.

"I think so. I don't know how."

"The pull works both directions, when it's strong enough. You're not the only one it's been reaching toward tonight." Ereloth looked back into the fire, and for the first time since Michael had met him, allowed a silence to stretch between them without filling it with anything at all.

---

They walked again in the morning, the pull sharper now, more certain, until the canyon finally opened onto a valley Michael hadn't expected after so many miles of broken red rock — green, plain, almost startlingly ordinary after everything that had led them here. No ruins marked it, no monument, nothing that announced itself as significant to anyone who hadn't already felt the pull that had brought them this far. He might have walked past it without a second glance, on any other morning, in any other company.

Ereloth stopped at the valley's edge, and Michael watched the last of his easy performance drop away entirely, replaced by something graver than anything he'd yet seen cross that face. The change unsettled him more than the valley itself did. He had grown accustomed, across these last two days, to a man who wore his gravity lightly, who let seriousness surface only in brief flashes before folding it back beneath a joke. This gravity did not fold away. It simply stood there, plain and unhidden, the way a man stands before a door he has spent a very long time avoiding.

"He's waiting for us there," Ereloth said, nodding toward a lone shape beneath a distant tree, small with distance but somehow unmistakable even from here. "The quiet one. My brother, Azael."

Michael studied the valley a long moment, and understood, with a clarity that arrived all at once rather than building slowly the way most understanding did, exactly what he was about to walk into. Three beings had shaped this world's oldest wound between them, three weapons sounding together in an accident none of them had chosen. He was not one of the three. He had never been part of whatever bond Ereloth, Thaeriel, and this quiet stranger beneath the tree shared, forged in an age before Michael had existed to share anything with anyone. Whatever conversation was about to happen in that valley belonged, at its center, to a history he could only ever stand adjacent to, however close the pull in his own hand had brought him.

He found, studying the small, still shape waiting for them across the valley, that the fact changed nothing about what he intended to do. He had spent long enough learning to set his own pride aside when a larger purpose asked it of him that the lesson no longer felt like sacrifice at all, simply the plain shape of what mattered most in a given moment. Whatever this reunion actually required of him, he would give it in full, brotherhood or no brotherhood, and follow Ereloth down into that valley exactly as certainly as he'd followed the pull in Solmire's hilt every step of the way here.

"Then let's not keep him waiting," Michael said, and started down the slope, Ereloth falling into step beside him without another word, the two of them walking together toward whatever the quiet stranger beneath the tree had been waiting this long to say.
